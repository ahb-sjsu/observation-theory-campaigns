\begin{abstract}
An equivariance monitor grades a paraphrase-invariance residual
$e = h_\ell(g x) - \hat\rho_\ell h_\ell(x)$ in raw L2 norm. We ask whether
grading it instead in the downstream model's own read, measured as the
model's output response when $e$ is patched into layer $\ell$, catches more
real behavioral changes at matched flag rates. The answer is conditional, and
we pin the condition through three preregistered families, two of which failed
their own sealed bars and are reported as executed. The consumer read cuts
monitor false-clears by 14 to 21 percentage points under backtranslation, but
under paraphrase it does the opposite, adding 35 to 57 points, at two model
scales. Two sealed attempts to gate the effect on the equivariance map's own
fit both failed. The variable that separates the regimes, identified from the
failures and then preregistered as a law, is transform locality: the held-out
$R^2$ of the identity map $h_\ell(g x)\approx h_\ell(x)$. Where the transform
is local in representation space the patched residual points along the true
perturbation and the consumer read is informative even when the equivariance
map is a poor fit; where it is not, the read is dominated by map error and
inverts. The practical rule is a mechanical, calibration-only gate: below a
locality floor a monitor must fall back to the raw metric or abstain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Internal-equivariance monitors gate on $h_\ell(g x) \approx \hat\rho_\ell(g)\,
h_\ell(x)$: a paraphrase $g$ should move a layer's representation the way a
fitted linear map $\hat\rho_\ell$ predicts, and a residual that violates this
flags a possible spec-gaming or drift. The residual is scored in raw L2 norm.
Observation Theory raises an objection that applies to any such monitor: the
layer's activations have a consumer, the downstream computation, and the norm
that matters is the consumer's read of the residual, not its raw magnitude. A
large residual in a direction the downstream network ignores is a false alarm;
a small residual exactly where the network reads is a false clear, which is the
case the monitor exists to catch.

This paper measures the consumer-read alternative and finds it conditional, in
a way that is itself the contribution. We grade the residual by its measured
patch-response: inject $e$ at layer $\ell$ and record the model's output-KL
response, the consumer's actual sensitivity to that residual. Across three
preregistered families we find a clean advantage under one transform family, a
clean inversion under another, and, after two sealed attempts to predict which
is which from the wrong variable, a locality law that does predict it. The
methodological spine is preregistration: pass/fail thresholds and seeds frozen
in version control before evaluation, verdicts committed as executed. Two of
the three families failed, and both failures are load-bearing evidence for the
law the third family states.

\section{Method}

\textbf{Setup.} An open causal LM (Qwen2.5, 0.5B and 1.5B) reads
Social-Chemistry-101 action texts and their transforms. For each layer $\ell$
the whitepaper's ridge estimator fits $\hat\rho_\ell$ on a calibration split;
the residual $e$ is injected at $\ell$ by a last-position activation patch and
the metric is the model's output-KL response. Ground truth is the model's
actual next-token KL between $x$ and $g x$. The monitor flags the top quartile
by each metric; the reported quantity is the false-clear rate, the fraction of
behavior-changed pairs not flagged, at matched flag rates, and the advantage
$\Delta = fc_{\mathrm{raw}} - fc_{\mathrm{true}}$.

\textbf{Discipline.} Each family is a sealed preregistration naming its cells,
bars, seeds, and manipulation checks before any evaluation data is opened, with
a one-day cooling-off between construction and seal. Graded verdicts are
committed exactly as the frozen rules grade them. All records, seals, and
post-mortems are in the campaign repository.

\section{The effect is real, and it inverts by transform family}

The construction cells established the shape at shakedown grade: under
backtranslation, the consumer read of the residual predicts behavioral change
better than raw L2 and cuts false-clears by 15 to 21 points at matched flag
rates, replicated on a second model (TinyLlama-1.1B) and a second
backtranslation language. The final-layer equivariance map fails to generalize
in every run, held-out $R^2$ collapsing from above $0.8$ in calibration to at
or below zero, so a monitor calibrated at the final layer grades against a
broken map. Both findings held under seal (family V1): the sealed
backtranslation cell cut false-clears, and the final-layer collapse recurred.

Family V1 nonetheless graded FAIL on its quantitative bar, and its post-mortem
found the effect's magnitude unforecastable from the crude single-split
headroom it had qualified on. Family V2 repaired the instrument and introduced
paraphrase as a second transform family. The result was decisive in an
unexpected direction: under paraphrase, at both model scales, the consumer read
did not merely lose its advantage, it inverted, adding 35 to 57 points of
false-clears over raw L2. The mechanism is visible in the records: paraphrase
drives the equivariance map's held-out $R^2$ to zero or below, so the patched
residual is dominated by map error, and injecting map error probes the model's
sensitivity to a direction that is not the perturbation.

\section{Two sealed attempts to gate the effect on map fit both fail}

The natural gate is the equivariance map's own quality: use the consumer read
only where $\hat\rho$ generalizes. Family V2's sealed secondary and the V2
successor both tested a version of this, gating on the held-out $R^2$ of
$\hat\rho$. It fails, and the failure is precise. In the deep layers of the
1.5B backtranslation cells the map's held-out $R^2$ is slightly negative, so a
$\hat\rho$-fit gate calls them out-of-regime, yet the consumer advantage
persists there at 9 to 17 points. The advantage does not need a good map; it
needs the patched residual to point roughly along the true perturbation. Map
fit is a proxy for that and a leaky one.

The variable that separates the regimes, read directly off the failures, is
transform locality: how nearly $h_\ell(g x) \approx h_\ell(x)$, measured as the
held-out $R^2$ of the identity map,
\[
  \Lambda_\ell = 1 - \frac{\sum \lVert h_\ell(g x) - h_\ell(x)\rVert^2}
                          {\sum \lVert h_\ell(g x) - \overline{h_\ell(g x)}\rVert^2}.
\]
It requires no map fit. On the V2 records it separates the outcomes with no
overlap: every backtranslation unit, including the two the $\hat\rho$-gate got
wrong, has $\Lambda$ between $+0.28$ and $+0.45$; both paraphrase units have
$\Lambda$ at $-1.3$ and $-1.8$. When a transform is local, $h_\ell(g x) =
h_\ell(x) + \delta$ with small $\delta$, so even a noisy $\hat\rho$ leaves the
residual approximately equal to the true perturbation and the consumer read
stays informative; when the transform relocates the representation wholesale,
the residual is map error and the read inverts.

Family V3 preregisters this as a law rather than a threshold. It grades cells
spanning a constructed locality ladder, from synonym substitution
($\Lambda \approx 0.9$) through interior word-shuffling ($\Lambda \approx 0.4$,
textually very different yet representationally local, the case that
distinguishes locality from map fit) to half-truncation ($\Lambda \approx
-0.7$). The sealed predictions are a threshold law, $\Delta \ge 0.05$ when
$\Lambda \ge 0.20$ and $\Delta \le 0.01$ when $\Lambda \le -0.20$, and a
monotonicity, Spearman$(\Lambda, \Delta) \ge 0.7$ pooled over all units.

\section{Consequence for monitor design}

The rule the three families support is operational and cheap. A consumer-metric
equivariance gate must be conditioned on a mechanical, calibration-only
locality check: compute $\Lambda$ on a held-out slice of the calibration split,
and below a locality floor fall back to the raw metric or abstain, because the
consumer read there is worse than the raw metric it would replace. This is the
$\hat\rho$-freshness and held-out-validation requirement of the whitepaper,
sharpened: it is not enough that $\hat\rho$ was fit; the transform the monitor
faces must be local enough for the read to be informative, and that is
measurable before deployment without fitting $\hat\rho$ at all. The two failed
paraphrase families are the measured demonstration of the cost of skipping the
check.

\section{Scope and limitations}

Two model scales of one family, Social-Chemistry text, backtranslation and two
paraphrasers plus the V3 string-operation ladder, the consumer read measured in
$x$'s context. The final-layer map collapse and the paraphrase inversion are
robust across every run measured; the locality law's monotonicity claim is
under seal at submission and reported as graded. We claim nothing about
transforms outside the measured locality range, and nothing about monitors
whose consumer is an external evaluator rather than the model's own output,
which the read operator would have to be re-derived for.
