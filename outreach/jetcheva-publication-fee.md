Subject: Support for a $1200 publication fee, accepted paper

Dear Jorjeta,

I have a paper accepted at the Journal of Science and Law and a $1200
publication fee to cover. I am writing to ask whether the department can
help, or to point me to the right office if this sits elsewhere.

The paper is "The Legal Bond Index: A Matched-Neighbourhood Diagnostic of
Algorithmic Disparity, Applied to COMPAS." COMPAS is the pretrial risk
algorithm at the centre of the 2016 ProPublica investigation. The paper
introduces a measurement that asks a question the existing fairness
metrics do not: whether the algorithm scores defendants differently from
their closest matched counterparts across racial lines, once you hold the
legitimate predictors fixed. It reports the result and then spends most of
its length trying to break it, including a specification under which the
effect largely attenuates, and it withdraws a second finding that did not
survive corrected inference.

The journal is open access and edited by David Eagleman at the Center for
Science and Law. It went through three rounds of editorial review. Every
number in it reproduces from the public ProPublica data with one seeded
script, which is bundled with the submission.

The fee is what makes the article free to read, which matters more than
usual for this subject. Criminal-justice risk scoring is argued over by
public defenders, journalists, and county agencies who will not pay for a
paywalled paper.

Three questions. Does the department hold funds for publication charges,
and if so what is the process and the timing? If not, is there a college
or research-office line I should apply to instead? And is anyone tracking
these requests, so I can plan for future submissions rather than asking
case by case?

I am glad to send the accepted manuscript, the acceptance correspondence,
or the journal's fee schedule.

Thank you,
