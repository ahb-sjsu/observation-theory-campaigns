\title{Games and Decisions Under Projection:\\
Blackwell Wedges, Budget Flips, and Prospect Signatures from
Declared Consumers}

\author{Andrew H. Bond}
\email{andrew.bond@sjsu.edu}
\thanks{ORCID: 0009-0003-2599-6158}
\affiliation{San Jos\'e State University, San Jos\'e, California, USA}
\date{August 6, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
An information set is the fiber of a projection, many hidden states
behind one observed signal, and Blackwell's ordering of experiments
is the decision-theoretic twin of the data-processing inequality.
This paper asks whether the behavioral structures of
imperfect-information decision problems, the value of information,
the signature distortions of prospect theory, reference dependence,
and budget-limited equilibrium play, are consumer-relative phenomena,
derivable from a declared channel, task, environment, and budget with
nothing inserted.  Seven experiment runs in exact finite models
answer with measurements.  A validated instrument layer computes
exact values of experiments and certified garbling relations.  In an
ensemble of two thousand random experiment pairs, 281 pairs were
ordered unanimously by all seven declared divergences while certified
Blackwell-incomparable, and every one of the 281 carried a task that
strictly prefers the divergence-dominated experiment.  A constructed
pair makes the wedge extreme, one experiment beats the other on every
divergence by at least 0.429 while a declared screening task prefers
the loser, and the pair is incomparable by proof in both directions.
In a zero-sum game where information is bought under a cell budget,
the read that maximizes mutual information is worth exactly the
no-information value while the task-optimal read at the same budget
attains the full-information value, a flip of exactly three fifths,
and a strict flip appears in 193 of 200 random games.  Budgeted
Bayes-optimal readers with no weighting function, no reference point,
and no loss-aversion coefficient among their inputs produce
regressive linear weighting in closed form, the full inverse-S
weighting curve, a crossover that tracks the declared environment, a
loss-side response ratio of 1.208 from a loss-heavy environment, and
diminishing sensitivity, with every distortion shrinking along the
declared budget ladders and vanishing at zero noise.  Reference
dependence appears exactly where a declared reference functional
interacts with an environment-adapted encoder, with an observer
spread of 29.6, a ranking reversal with margins 12.4 and 10.8, and a
translation-covariance breach of 6.44 that the adapted observer
avoids exactly.  The rational-inattention logit of Matejka and McKay
is replicated to fourteen digits, consideration sets form exactly as
the budget prices actions out, and in a coordination game of two
budgeted consumers equilibrium attention is complementary, a
better-informed opponent makes attention more valuable.  Two runs
failed their declared bars on their own declarations' bookkeeping,
the records carry the failures, and corrected redeclarations were run
afterward.  Nothing in this paper is a claim about human beings.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This campaign models observation as consumption.  A consumer reads a
hidden state through a declared channel, spends a declared budget on
the read, and acts on a declared task.  Earlier stages of the
campaign measured what such consumers can distinguish in quantum
models \cite{QuantumPaper} and what entropy they observe in
reversible classical models \cite{SourcesPaper}.  Imperfect-information
decision theory has worked with the same objects under other names
for seventy years.  An information set is the fiber of a projection,
many hidden histories collapsed behind one observed signal.
Blackwell's ordering of experiments, under which one information
source is better than another exactly when it is better for every
decision problem \cite{Blackwell1953}, is the decision-theoretic twin
of the data-processing inequality \cite{CoverThomas2006}.  Rational
inattention prices the read in information units \cite{Sims2003,
MatejkaMcKay2015}.  Prospect theory catalogs the distortions that
appear when valuations are measured \cite{KahnemanTversky1979,
TverskyKahneman1992}.

The question of this track is whether the behavioral structures of
imperfect-information domains are consumer-relative, derivable from
declared channel, task, environment, and budget structure with
nothing inserted.  The distortions of prospect theory are usually
presented as departures from rationality.  The efficient-coding
account holds instead that they are what optimal reading through a
limited channel looks like \cite{ZhangMaloney2012, FrydmanJin2022}.
That account makes an exactly checkable claim in a finite model.
Declare the channel, the environment, and the budget, forbid every
behavioral ingredient as an input, compute the optimal read, and see
which signatures appear.

Three claim types run through the paper and are never conflated.
Instrument claims state that values of experiments, garbling
relations, and optimal budgeted reads can be computed exactly in
finite models, and they are checked against closed forms.  Structural
claims state that declared consumer structure produces a named
behavioral signature without that signature being inserted, or
measurably fails to, and they carry the campaign's exploratory label
because they are measured in declared models.  Replication claims
state that a published theoretical result is reproduced exactly in a
declared finite instance.  Nothing in this paper is a claim about
human beings, about empirical behavioral data, or about the
rationality of any person, and no run is labeled as supporting such a
claim.

An anti-circularity contract governs every run.  No probability
weighting function appears anywhere in a model's declarations.  No
reference point is chosen after inspecting outcomes.  No
loss-aversion coefficient enters as an input.  No task list is
selected after seeing which tasks favor a wanted verdict.  Budgets,
channels, priors, and utilities are declared before the runs that
bear claims, and any behavioral signature must appear in the measured
optimal reads or the claim fails.

The paper reports seven experiment runs and one instrument layer.
The instrument layer computes exact values of experiments and
certified garbling relations and must pass closed-form controls
before anything else runs.  The first experiment measures the wedge
between scalar fidelity orderings and task orderings and locates it
exactly in the region Blackwell's theorem leaves open.  The second
transplants the campaign's budget flip, task-optimized reads beating
fidelity-optimized reads at scarce budget \cite{QuantumPaper}, into a
game against an adversary.  The third asks whether prospect theory's
signature distortions emerge from budgeted readers that contain none
of them.  The fourth locates reference dependence in the choice of a
declared reference functional read through an environment-adapted
encoder.  The fifth replicates the rational-inattention logit exactly
and then measures which equilibrium structures survive when every
player is a budgeted consumer.  Two of the runs failed their declared
bars.  In both cases every measured quantity matched its closed form
and the failure was an error in the declaration's own bookkeeping, a
threshold applied to the wrong object in one case and a double count
in the other.  The records carry the failed verdicts, the corrected
declarations were rerun, and both accounts are reported in full.

\section{The instrument layer passes exact controls}
\label{sec:gd0}

An experiment in Blackwell's sense is a row-stochastic likelihood
matrix $A$ with $A_{sy}$ the probability of signal $y$ in state $s$.
For a prior $\pi$ and a task, a utility matrix $u(a,s)$ over actions
and states, the value of the experiment is the expected payoff of the
posterior-optimal action,
\begin{equation}
  V(A) \;=\; \sum_y \max_a \sum_s \pi_s\, u(a,s)\, A_{sy},
  \label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
an exact finite sum.  Experiment $B$ is a garbling of $A$ when
$B = AM$ for some row-stochastic $M$, and Blackwell's theorem states
that this holds exactly when $A$ is worth at least as much as $B$ for
every prior and every task \cite{Blackwell1953}.  The instrument
layer implements Eq.~\eqref{eq:value} directly and implements a
garbling checker as a projected-gradient search for $M$ whose checked
object is the residual $\max\,\lvert AM - B\rvert$ against a declared
feasibility tolerance of $10^{-7}$.  The solver is only a search.
The residual is the evidence.

Five controls with known answers gate the layer, and no later run in
the track executes until all five pass.  The uninformative and
perfect experiments reproduce the no-information and full-information
closed forms exactly.  Across a declared battery of twenty tasks,
a battery being a list of tasks fixed before the run, garbling never
raises any value, with a worst measured gain of $1.1\times10^{-16}$.
A declared garble is certified feasible at residual
$5.6\times10^{-17}$ while its reverse direction is refused at
residual $0.260$, and a signal permutation is certified in both
directions.  The binary symmetric channel family is ordered by its
error rate, and the checker reproduces that ordering across the
declared grid of error rates from $0.05$ to $0.45$.  The last
control constructs an incomparable pair, refused in both directions,
whose two declared tasks disagree, one prefers the first experiment
by $0.05$ and the other prefers the second by $0.15$.  That pair
seeds the next section.

\section{Unanimous fidelity orderings guarantee nothing outside
comparability}
\label{sec:gd1}

The campaign's quantum track found task-optimized encoders beating
fidelity-optimized encoders at scarce budget \cite{QuantumPaper}.
Blackwell's theorem gives that finding a sharp decision-theoretic
frame.  A garbling loses for every task, while any scalar fidelity
measure is a single number, so scalar orderings can only disagree
with task orderings where no garbling relation holds in either
direction.  This experiment measures the disagreement exactly.  The
setting is a binary state with uniform prior and experiments with
three signals, so every scalar fidelity in the declared battery is an
$f$-divergence between the two conditional signal rows.  The battery
holds seven, Kullback-Leibler in both directions, total variation,
squared Hellinger, chi-squared in both directions, and
Jensen-Shannon.  A wedge is a pair where all seven divergences
strictly order one experiment above the other while some declared
task strictly prefers the other.

The declared exhibit is a rare decisive signal.  The two experiments
are
\begin{equation}
  A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.90 & 0.05 & 0.05\\
                      0.05 & 0.05 & 0.90 \end{pmatrix},
  \quad
  B = \begin{pmatrix} 0.98 & 0.019 & 0.001\\
                      0.881 & 0.02 & 0.099 \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:exhibit}
\end{equation}
and the exhibit task is a screening decision, a safe action worth
zero in both states against a treat action worth $-20$ in the healthy
state and $+1$ in the sick state, so treating pays only above the
posterior threshold $20/21$.  $A$ is far more informative on every
divergence, with margins from $0.429$ to $15.1$ across the battery,
but its best posterior is $0.947$ and never clears the threshold, so
its value for this task is exactly zero.  $B$ is nearly
uninformative except for one rare signal, probability $0.05$, whose
posterior is $0.99$, and its value is $0.0395$.  The task prefers
$B$ by $0.0395$ while every declared fidelity prefers $A$ by at
least $0.429$.

Three further items were declared with the exhibit.  A
data-processing control drew 500 pairs related by a declared random
garble and checked them against the battery of 205 tasks, 200 random
three-action utilities and five screening thresholds, all seed-fixed.
No divergence rose under any garble, the smallest margin in the
required direction measured $1.8\times10^{-4}$, and no task gained
more than $3.3\times10^{-16}$, which is Blackwell's theorem plus the
data-processing inequality holding in the instrument, and a violation
would have indicted the instrument rather than the theorems.  A
localization item classified a declared ensemble of 2000 random pairs
by garbling certificate and required every wedge to fall in the
incomparable class.  Figure~\ref{fig:gd1} shows the outcome, 1044
comparable, 767 incomparable, 189 ambiguous, and zero wedges among
comparable pairs, with 144 wedges among the excluded ambiguous pairs.
The prevalence measurement, declared with no bar, is the substantive
finding.  Among the 767 certified-incomparable pairs, 281 were
ordered unanimously by all seven divergences, and every one of the
281 carried a task reversal in the declared battery, with a maximum
reversal margin of $0.0776$ and a median of $0.0214$.  In this
ensemble, unanimous scalar-fidelity ordering guaranteed nothing about
tasks in a single instance outside certified comparability.

The recorded verdict of this run is FAIL.  The exhibit's declared bar
required both garbling-search residuals to exceed $0.01$, a round
number declared without computing the scale it would be compared
against.  The reverse residual measured $0.4198$ and passed.  The
forward residual measured $0.004486$ and failed the bar, while every
divergence margin and the task margin passed.  The verdict stands as
recorded, computed from the declared items.

The redeclared run replaced the threshold with a proof and passed all
five of its items.  Incomparability of the exhibit is established in
both directions without any residual bar.  In one direction, the
joint-distribution columns of any garbling of $A$ are conic
combinations of $A$'s joint columns, so a target column $(t_0, t_1)$
with $t_0 < r\, t_1$, where $r$ is the smallest first-to-second-row
ratio among $A$'s joint columns, forces a residual of at least
$(r\, t_1 - t_0)/(1+r)$ for every row-stochastic $M$.  Computed from
the declared matrices, the bound is $0.004263$ against its declared
bar of $10^{-3}$, more than four orders of magnitude above the
feasibility tolerance, so $B$ is not a garbling of $A$.  In the other
direction, total variation is an $f$-divergence, a garbling cannot
raise it, and $A$'s total variation exceeds $B$'s by $0.751$, so $A$
is not a garbling of $B$.  The consistency item then reads the failed
bar's own number correctly.  The search residual $0.004486$ sits
above the analytic bound and within five percent of it, so the search
was measuring genuine infeasibility, and the original failure was a
bar thresholding a search residual instead of certifying
infeasibility.  The exhibit therefore stands proved, every declared
$f$-divergence orders $A$ above $B$ by at least $0.429$, the declared
screening task strictly prefers $B$, and the pair is
Blackwell-incomparable in both directions.  Together with the
ensemble measurement, the wedge that the campaign's budget flip
exploited is exhibited exactly in decision form and localized to
exactly the region Blackwell's theorem leaves open.

\section{The fidelity-optimal purchase buys nothing against an
adversary}
\label{sec:gd2}

The second experiment transplants the budget flip into a game.  Six
hidden states carry a declared prior.  Player one buys a
deterministic coarsening of the state, a partition into at most $k$
cells for budget $k$, and the choice of partition is public.  Player
two observes nothing.  Both then move simultaneously in a zero-sum
game with state-dependent payoffs, player one maximizing.  With two
actions for player two the value is exact.  Player one's best
response to a mixture $q$ is greedy within each cell, so the game
value is the minimum over $q$ of a convex piecewise-linear function,
minimized exactly by enumerating the crossing points of the per-cell
action lines.  All 203 partitions of six states are enumerated.  The
fidelity-optimal read at budget $k$ maximizes mutual information
between state and cell, which for a deterministic coarsening is the
entropy of the cell masses, with ties collected within $10^{-12}$ and
the flip always charged against the best-valued member of the tie
set.  The task-optimal read maximizes game value.  The flip at a
budget is the task-optimal value minus the best fidelity-optimal
value.

The declared exhibit is a stake game built so that the flip has
rational closed forms.  States are pairs of a payoff-relevant bit
with prior mass $0.8$ and $0.2$ and a payoff-irrelevant trit uniform
within each bit value, so the six priors are $4/15$ three times and
$1/15$ three times.  Player one guesses the bit, earning $+1$ on a
match and $-2$ on a miss, multiplied by a stake of $1$ or $3$ chosen
by player two.  The declared closed forms follow from the structure.
The no-information value is $2/5$.  The task-optimal two-cell read is
the bit split, worth exactly $1$, which is also the full-information
value.  The fidelity-optimal two-cell reads are the mass-balancing
partitions that split $8/15$ against $7/15$, each mixing the bit
inside one cell, each worth exactly $2/5$.  The fidelity-optimal
spend of the budget buys exactly nothing, and the flip is exactly
$3/5$.

The first run of this experiment recorded verdict FAIL, and the
failure is an integer in the declaration.  Every measured value
matched its declared closed form within $10^{-15}$, the task-optimal
two-cell value measured exactly $1$, every fidelity-optimal two-cell
read measured $0.4$, the flip measured $0.6$ against its bar of
$0.5$, the ladders were monotone, and no partition beat the finest,
which is the zero-sum theorem that more information never hurts,
carried as an instrument-indicting control.  The one failed item was
the declared count of tied fidelity-optimal two-cell partitions, six
declared against three measured.  The declaration had tallied each
two-cell partition once from each of its cells.  The mass-$7/15$
subsets are exactly the complements of the mass-$8/15$ subsets, three
partitions, not six.  The redeclared run corrected that count, held
every other object fixed, and passed all six items.

The ensemble measurement, declared with no bar and identical across
both runs, shows the flip is generic rather than constructed.  Over
200 random games with Dirichlet priors, three player-one actions,
two player-two actions, and payoffs uniform on $[-1,1]$, all
seed-fixed, the task-optimal read strictly beat every
fidelity-optimal read in 193 of 200 games at budget 2, with median
flip $0.070$ and maximum $0.328$, and in 188 of 200 games at budget
3.  Figure~\ref{fig:gd2} shows the exhibit ladder and the ensemble
magnitudes.  The campaign's flip is now measured in a game against
an adversary.  At scarce budget, buying the read that maximizes
mutual information is generically the wrong purchase.

\section{Prospect signatures emerge from consumers that contain
none of them}
\label{sec:gd3}

Prospect theory's weighting function overweights small probabilities,
underweights large ones, crosses the identity near one third to one
half, and is steep at both ends \cite{KahnemanTversky1979,
TverskyKahneman1992}.  Its value function is asymmetric between
gains and losses and exhibits diminishing sensitivity.  The
efficient-coding account predicts that these shapes are properties of
optimal reading through a limited channel under an environmental
prior \cite{ZhangMaloney2012, FrydmanJin2022}.  This experiment
declares three budgeted consumers, none containing a weighting
function, a reference point chosen after outcomes, or a
loss-aversion coefficient, and measures which signatures their
optimal reads exhibit.  The verdict is computed from audits on the
machinery alone.  The findings carry declared directional bars and
are recorded pass or fail individually, and either outcome is a
result.

The first consumer reads a win probability $p$ through $n$ Bernoulli
draws under a Beta$(2,2)$ environmental prior and reports the
posterior mean, with the sample budget $n$ on a declared ladder from
$1$ to $200$.  Its implied weighting has the closed form
\begin{equation}
  w(p) \;=\; \frac{2 + np}{4 + n},
  \label{eq:p1}
\end{equation}
and the measured read matches Eq.~\eqref{eq:p1} within
$6.8\times10^{-14}$ at every $n$ with maximum curvature
$1.5\times10^{-14}$.  This consumer produces overweighting of small
probabilities and underweighting of large ones, regression toward
the prior mean, with no inverse-S curvature at all.  The weighting
is exactly linear.

The second consumer is a log-odds reader.  The hidden state is the
log-odds $\ell$ of the probability, on a grid from $-6$ to $6$ in
steps of $0.01$, with a Gaussian environmental prior of variance
$1.5$.  The channel adds discrete Gaussian noise of width $\sigma$ to
the coded value, and the read is the exact posterior mean of
$p = 1/(1+e^{-\ell})$.  The implied weight $w(p)$ is the channel
average of the read.  The declared budget ladder is $\sigma$ equal to
$1.0$, $0.5$, $0.25$, and exactly $0$ as the identity control.
Figure~\ref{fig:gd3} shows the measured curve at $\sigma = 1.0$.  It
is the full inverse-S, from nothing but a log-odds code, a Gaussian
budget, and Bayes-optimal reading.  The crossover is exactly $0.5$ by
symmetry.  The endpoint secant slopes measure $3.00$ at both ends
against a declared bar of $1.1$, the middle secant measures $0.51$
against a bar of $0.9$, and the overweighting and underweighting
margins on the declared bands measure $0.099$ where $0.01$ was
required.  The declared shifted environment, the same reader with the
prior centered at log-odds $-1$, moves the crossover to $0.338$,
inside its declared bar of $0.35$.  The distortion tracks the
environment, not the machinery.

The third consumer reads outcome magnitudes from $-100$ to $100$
through the compressive code $\mathrm{sign}(x)\ln(1+\lvert x\rvert)$
with Gaussian channel noise, under a symmetric Laplace environment
and, in a declared variant, an asymmetric environment whose loss side
is heavier, scale $40$ against $20$, declared on the efficient-coding
rationale that the question is whether a budgeted read transmits
environmental asymmetry into valuation asymmetry.  Under the
symmetric environment the read is odd to $1.5\times10^{-13}$, so the
machinery inserts no asymmetry.  Under the loss-heavy environment the
measured response ratio at $\pm50$ is $1.208$, the loss side read
less compressed, with nothing resembling a loss-aversion coefficient
anywhere in the model.  Diminishing sensitivity is unambiguous, the
declared coarse second differences on the gain side are all negative
with maximum $-0.092$ and mean $-0.402$.

The audits that compute the verdict all passed.  The first consumer
matches its closed form.  The zero-noise consumers read the identity
within $10^{-12}$, so the machinery inserts nothing.  Symmetric
environments produce exactly symmetric reads.  And the maximum
deviation from the identity strictly decreases along every declared
budget ladder, from $0.392$ to $0.0096$ for the sample reader, from
$0.149$ to zero for the log-odds reader, and from $45.0$ to zero for
the magnitude reader, so every distortion is budget-borne and
vanishes when the budget constraint does.  All five declared findings
passed their directional bars.  The signature set of prospect theory,
regressive weighting, the inverse-S, environment-borne loss
asymmetry, and diminishing sensitivity, emerged in declared budgeted
consumers with none of it among the inputs.

\section{Reference dependence is the declared functional read
through an adapted encoder}
\label{sec:gd4}

A design note recorded in the protocol shaped this model and is
itself a finding.  A posterior-mean read decoded under the gamble's
own distribution is exactly unbiased in expectation, iterated
expectation cancels every reference effect, so reference dependence
cannot live there.  The declared model is therefore an encoder
adapted to a fixed environment and presented with novel gambles,
which is also the efficient-coding picture, and the reference point
is the encoder's adaptation level.

The reader encodes deviations $d = x - R$ from a reference $R$
through the compressive code $\mathrm{sign}(d)\ln(1+\lvert d\rvert)$,
a discrete Gaussian channel of width $\sigma$ acts on the code, and
the read is the exact posterior mean of $d$ under the declared
environmental prior, a symmetric Laplace of scale $30$.  The
valuation of a gamble is the mean reference plus the expected read of
its deviations.  Four reference functionals are declared, none chosen

The lagged observer takes the previous outcome of an independent
two-period presentation.  A declared battery of six gambles probes
them, including a pair related by a shift of ten.

The audits all passed, and they are what makes the observer
differences lawful rather than anomalous.  At zero noise every
valuation equals the gamble mean within $1.8\times10^{-15}$.  The
symmetry anchors hold within $7.1\times10^{-14}$ at every budget, the
adapted observer reads the symmetric gambles at their means, and the
lagged observer reads every gamble at its mean because independent
two-period differences are symmetric.  The adapted observer's
translation covariance is exact, its valuation of the shifted gamble
equals its valuation of the base gamble plus ten with measured
deviation zero against the declared $10^{-10}$ tolerance.  And the
maximum deviation of any valuation from the gamble mean falls
strictly along the budget ladder, $29.6$ at $\sigma = 2.5$, $20.1$ at
$1.0$, machine zero at $0$.

Table~\ref{tab:gd4} shows the valuations at the scarcest budget, and
the declared findings are large rather than marginal.  The maximum
spread across observers on a single gamble is $29.6$, fifty-nine
times its declared bar.  A ranking reversal sits inside the declared
battery, the status-quo observer prefers B1 to B5 by $12.4$ while the
lower-median observer prefers B5 to B1 by $10.8$.  The status-quo
observer breaks translation covariance by $6.44$ on the declared
shift while the adapted observer holds it exactly, so which
reference effects appear is decided by the declared functional, and
every appearance is predicted by the detector model.  Reference
dependence in this model is not a preference anomaly.  It is the
declared functional's interaction with an environment-tuned budgeted
encoder, with every difference between observers derived from
declared structure.

\section{The rational-inattention logit replicates exactly and
equilibrium attention is complementary}
\label{sec:gd5}

The last experiment has a replication half and a campaign half.  The
replication target is the result of Matejka and McKay that a
rational-inattention consumer, maximizing expected payoff minus
$\lambda$ times the mutual information between state and action,
chooses by a weighted logit \cite{Sims2003, MatejkaMcKay2015}.  The
declared instance has four uniform states and three actions, a safe
action worth $0.6$ everywhere, and two risky actions each worth $1$
on their own half of the states.  The optimum is computed by
Blahut-Arimoto iteration \cite{Blahut1972, Arimoto1972} with the
logit step in log space, run to a fixed-point residual of
$10^{-14}$, over the declared cost ladder $\lambda$ from $10^{-6}$
to $5$.

Five declared bars gate the replication and all five passed.
Stationarity, the unconditional action distribution equal to the
marginal of the conditional strategy, holds with residual exactly
zero.  The weighted-logit identity of Matejka and McKay,
\begin{equation}
  P(a \mid s) \;=\;
  \frac{p_0(a)\, e^{u(a,s)/\lambda}}
       {\sum_b p_0(b)\, e^{u(b,s)/\lambda}},
  \label{eq:mm}
\end{equation}
with $p_0$ the unconditional action distribution, holds at the fixed
point to $9.5\times10^{-15}$.  None of 200 declared random feasible
perturbations of the conditional strategy improves the net objective,
the recorded maximum improvement is exactly zero.  The limits land on
their closed forms, value $1$ with information equal to $\ln 2$ to
the last digit at vanishing cost, and prior play at cost $5$, where
the safe action carries unconditional probability $1$ within
$10^{-9}$.  Table~\ref{tab:gd5} shows the ladder.  The
consideration-set phenomenon appears exactly as the replication
target describes.  The safe action is excluded at every cost up to
$1$, with unconditional probability $3\times10^{-23}$ at cost
$0.05$, and at cost $1$ the optimum is genuinely soft, value
$0.7311$ with information $0.1109$ for a net objective of $0.6201$,
strictly better than both pure safe play at $0.6$ and full
attention.  Feasible actions drop from the consideration set exactly
as the budget prices them out.

The campaign half puts two budgeted consumers in a coordination
game.  Two uniform states, two actions each, payoff $1$ for matching
the state plus $0.5$ for matching the other player, so given the
opponent's conditional strategy $Q$ the effective payoff is
$u(a,s) = \mathbf{1}[a{=}s] + 0.5\, Q(a \mid s)$ and best responses
are Blahut-Arimoto solves, iterated from uniform play to a joint
residual of $10^{-12}$.  All three declared equilibrium bars passed.
The iteration converges at every declared budget pair.  Symmetric
budgets give the symmetric equilibrium, including state-flip
symmetry, with deviation $2.1\times10^{-14}$.  Along the common
budget ladder the equilibrium information is nonincreasing, matching
accuracy at the finest budget is above $0.95$, and at every ladder
point the measured equilibrium accuracy $q$ sits on the independent
closed-form curve
\begin{equation}
  q \;=\; \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(1 + 0.5\,(2q-1))/\lambda}},
  \label{eq:fp}
\end{equation}
the scalar fixed point of the weighted logit applied to the
coordination payoff, with residual $2.1\times10^{-14}$.  The
Shannon-cost solution is interior at every finite budget, and the
measured equilibrium lands on the curve that an entirely separate
route computes.

The declared finding measures how one player's budget moves the
other player's equilibrium attention.  Player one's cost is fixed at
$0.2$ while the opponent's cost ladders over $0.05$, $0.2$, $1$, and
$5$.  Figure~\ref{fig:gd5} shows the result.  Player one's
equilibrium information falls monotonically from $0.6884$ to
$0.6637$ nats as the opponent's budget coarsens, passing its
declared direction and minimum-drop bars.  In this game attention is
complementary.  Information bought by one player raises the value of
information to the other, exactly as declared before the run.

verdicts.  Read together they answer the track's question inside its
declared scope.  The value of information is task-relative in a
precise sense, scalar fidelity orderings carry no task guarantee
outside certified Blackwell comparability, and in the measured
ensemble the unanimous-fidelity wedge was never empty of task
reversals.  The budget flip survives an adversary, and the
fidelity-optimal purchase can buy exactly nothing while the
task-optimal purchase at the same budget buys everything.  The
signature distortions of prospect theory, regressive weighting, the
inverse-S, loss-side asymmetry, and diminishing sensitivity, are
producible by declared budgeted consumers that contain none of them,
with each distortion shrinking as the budget grows and vanishing at
zero noise, and with the shape tracking the declared environment.
Reference dependence in these models is the choice of a declared
reference functional read through an environment-adapted encoder,
with every observer difference predicted by the detector model.  The
rational-inattention logit is replicated exactly, and in the one
equilibrium measured, attention is complementary across players.

Two verdicts in the ledger are FAIL, and both failures were located
in the declarations rather than in the measured structures, a
residual threshold where a proof was needed and a double-counted tie
set.  The corrected redeclarations passed while holding every
measured object fixed.  The bars ruled in both cases, which is what
they are declared for.

These are structural results about declared finite models carrying
the exploratory label of the campaign's evidence-grading scheme.
They show that the named behavioral signatures can arise from
channel, task, environment, and budget structure alone.  They do not
show that human behavior arises this way, and no experiment in this
track can bear on that question.

\section{Methods and reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods}

All runs follow the campaign's standing discipline.  Priors,
utilities, channels, budgets, batteries, seeds, tolerances, and bars
were declared in the track document before the runs they govern, per
the anti-circularity contract, and verdicts are computed from the
declared items, never written first.  Every result-bearing run
writes an append-only JSON evidence record containing the declared
parameters, the measured outcomes, and a canonical SHA-256 hash of
the record body.  The records for this paper are
\texttt{gd0-instrument.json},
\texttt{gd1-flip-blackwell.json},
\texttt{gd1b-exhibit-certificate.json},
\texttt{gd2-budget-flip-game.json},
\texttt{gd2b-budget-flip-game.json},
\texttt{gd3-prospect-signatures.json},
\texttt{gd4-reference-functionals.json}, and
\texttt{gd5-budgeted-equilibrium.json} in the campaign repository,
with the committed runners alongside them.  The failed GD-1 and GD-2
records are retained unchanged, and the corrected declarations are
separate records, so the sequence of verdicts is itself part of the
evidence.  Every number and every figure coordinate in this paper is
read from those committed records.  The figure tables are generated
deterministically by a committed script with no free parameters, and
the one plotted curve not stored in a verdict record, the weighting
curve of Fig.~\ref{fig:gd3}, is a committed figure-data artifact
recomputed by the committed GD-3 instrument and bound by hash to the
sealed record it accompanies.  A reducer may summarize records but
may not rewrite them.
