\title{Auditing Emergent Physics Claims in Rewriting Systems:\\
Exact Tests of Dimension, Causal Invariance, and the Second Law}

\author{Andrew H. Bond}
\email{andrew.bond@sjsu.edu}
\thanks{ORCID: 0009-0003-2599-6158}
\affiliation{San Jos\'e State University, San Jos\'e, California, USA}
\date{August 5, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
The Wolfram Physics Project claims that simple rewriting systems
exhibit emergent spatial dimension, relativistic structure from causal
invariance of the rewriting, and a second law of thermodynamics from
computational irreducibility.  Each of these claims rests on an
inference pattern that the present campaign has already made exactly
measurable for its own purposes, so the claims can be audited rather
than debated.  This paper reports six audits, run under the campaign's
sealed-instrument discipline and framed so that a defender of the
program would accept every pass condition as fair.  A shell-based
dimension estimator is exact on controls of known dimension and
refuses the diverging control, while the naive ball-volume slope that
casual dimension measurements use reads low on every exact control by
a finite-radius bias that small-graph dimension claims inherit.  A
manifestly reversible cellular automaton, built on the featured rule
of the program's own second-law writings, raises its declared
coarse-grained entropy from an ordered seed and then retraces the
curve bitwise exactly with exact recovery of the initial microstate,
so the entropy growth is observational, which sharpens the program's
observer-relative account of the second law into a measured statement.
For string rewriting, a causally invariant sorting rule agrees exactly
under every update order, a planted non-confluent rule is flagged with
three distinct terminal strings, and a growth rule splits into
conserved counts that are invariant and an arrangement that belongs to
the update order.  Causal-graph invariants built from token genealogy
are identical across update orders where causal invariance holds, and
extremal foliations achieve the causal depth bound exactly while
random foliations spend up to twice as many slices, so simultaneity
bookkeeping moves while the invariants stay frozen.  A hypergraph
rule's measured dimension then passes a held-out scaling test, beating
a preregistered exponential alternative forty-fold in held-out error.
The capstone audit shows that the same rule's bulk bookkeeping is not
invariant.  Five declared match orders give different relation counts,
different vertex counts, and a seventeen percent spread in the
dimension estimate, so the surviving dimension claim carries a
measured prescription caveat at these sizes.  The verdict pattern is
uniform.  Quantities are exact where an algebraic property protects
them, prescription-borne where matching conflicts exist, and the
second law of reversible computation is observational entropy.
Nothing in this paper bears on whether the program is correct as
fundamental physics.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Wolfram program proposes that fundamental physics emerges from
simple rewriting systems.  Space and its dimension are read from the
growth of neighborhood volumes in an evolving hypergraph
\cite{Wolfram2020}, special relativity is attributed to causal
invariance of the rewriting, the property that every order of applying
the rules yields the same causal structure \cite{Gorard2020a}, quantum
mechanics is read from the branching of a multiway evolution
\cite{Gorard2020b}, and the second law of thermodynamics is attributed
to computational irreducibility acting on computationally bounded
observers \cite{Wolfram2023}.  The program's claims have been debated
mostly at the level of plausibility, because the inference patterns
they rest on, reading a dimension from a growth curve, asserting
independence from an update order, counting structures relative to a
slicing, and locating an entropy in an observer, are rarely stated
with pass and failure conditions that both sides accept in advance.

This campaign built exact instruments for all four of those inference
patterns before turning to the Wolfram program, for reasons internal
to its own questions.  A foliation-covariance audit asks whether a
claimed event survives a passive change of the observer's slicing, and
the campaign's fold theorem states exactly which changes of
multiplicity are legitimate under such a change.  A prescription audit
asks whether a claimed quantity survives a change of update
prescription, after an earlier stage of the campaign proved that a
published threshold belonged to a discretization rather than to the
dynamics.  An estimator-validation rule forbids any instrument from
measuring anything until it has passed exact controls, including a
control it must refuse.  And the campaign's entropy track separated
observational entropy from thermodynamic entropy by exhibiting a
reversible system whose observed entropy swings while its hidden
ensemble is recovered exactly \cite{EntropyPaper,SourcesPaper}.

These audits test
claims, not people, every pass condition is one a defender of the
program would accept as fair, every failure condition is a finding
about a model class rather than a criticism of a research effort, and
a claim that survives comes out stronger than it went in.

The paper reports six audits.  An instrument net validates the
dimension estimator on exact controls and, along the way, measures the
finite-radius bias of the naive estimator that casual dimension claims
use.  A reversible cellular automaton makes the program's second-law
account a measured statement.  A string-rewriting audit measures which
quantities survive a change of update order, with a planted
non-confluent rule as the trap the pipeline must catch.  A
causal-graph audit checks the same question on causal structure and
adds a foliation sweep.  A hypergraph audit tests a dimension claim on
held-out data it was never fitted to.  The capstone audit then turns
the update-order question on the hypergraph rule itself and finds its
bulk bookkeeping prescription-borne.  A closing section assembles the
scorecard, which claims are invariant, which are observational, and
which belong to the prescription.

All
systems are finite and computable, all measurements are exact or carry
declared tolerances, and no result bears on whether the Wolfram
program is true or false as fundamental physics.  A claim shown
prescription-borne here is a result about a declared rule at measured
sizes under a declared audit, nothing more.

\section{The claims and the audit instruments}
\label{sec:mapping}

The track document of this campaign records a mapping with one row per
program claim, the instrument that audits it, and what a pass and a
failure mean.  Four rows were run and are reported here.

Emergent dimension from ball-volume growth is audited by the
estimator net of Sec.~\ref{sec:wm0} followed by the held-out scaling
test of Sec.~\ref{sec:wm4}.  A pass means the rule's estimated
dimension is finite, stable on radii never used for fitting, and beats
preregistered alternative growth laws.  A failure means the dimension
estimate belongs to the fitted radius window, a statement about
estimator scope on that rule.

Causal invariance, the basis of the program's relativistic claims, is
audited by the prescription protocols of Secs.~\ref{sec:wm2}
and~\ref{sec:wm2h}, with the update order as the prescription.  For a
rule with provable confluence the declared invariants must agree
exactly across independent update orders.  For a rule without such a
proof the order dependence is measured rather than assumed away, and
any quantity that varies is classified prescription-borne for that
rule.

Structures counted relative to a slicing are audited by the
foliation protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:wm3}.  The campaign's fold theorem
allows an honest middle outcome, a multiplicity that varies across
slicings while a declared signed invariant is conserved, so the audit
never demands naive invariance of raw counts.

The second law from computational irreducibility is audited by the
reversible-automaton protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:wm1}.  This row is not
adversarial at all.  The program's own account already locates the
second law in the observer \cite{Wolfram2023}, and the audit supplies
the exact-arithmetic witness that turns that account from an argument
into a measurement.

The fifth row of the mapping, quantum mechanics from multiway
branching \cite{Gorard2020b}, remains design only and no experiment
for it is reported here.

Two standing disciplines bind every run.  Every pipeline carries a
planted artifact, a deliberately non-confluent rule whose order
dependence is known in advance, and a pipeline that fails to flag its
own trap voids the run.  And every window, coarse-graining,
prescription list, and alternative model is declared before the run it
governs, per the track's anti-circularity contract.

\section{The dimension-estimator net and the naive-slope bias}
\label{sec:wm0}

No dimension is quoted in this paper before the instrument that
measures it has passed exact controls, including a control it must
refuse.  For a graph, let $S(v,r)$ be the number of vertices at graph
distance exactly $r$ from a base vertex $v$, and let $B(v,r)$ be the
number within distance $r$.  The campaign's estimator fits the slope
of $\log S$ against $\log r$ over the window $2 \le r \le 10$,
declared before any rule measurement, averages over $24$ declared base
points, and reports
\begin{equation}
  \hat d \;=\; 1 + \frac{\dd \log S}{\dd \log r},
  \label{eq:dhat}
\end{equation}
with a sealed tolerance of $0.15$ and a refusal condition.  If the
local log-log slope grows across the window instead of stabilizing,
the graph is flagged non-finite-dimensional and no dimension is
quoted.

Table~\ref{tab:wm0} shows the net.  The path graph returns $1.000$
with local slopes flat to machine precision, the two-dimensional torus
returns $2.000$ within $2\times10^{-16}$, and the three-dimensional
torus returns $2.958$, a deviation of $0.042$ against the tolerance of
$0.15$, with local slopes climbing from $1.919$ toward $2$ across the
window as the lattice shells converge on their asymptotic law.  The
regular tree, the control the estimator must refuse, shows local
slopes growing from $2.71$ to $10.43$ across the window with no
stabilization, so the recorded refusal condition fires and the graph
is flagged non-finite-dimensional.  The record retains the number a
non-refusing estimator would print, $6.47$, and quoting it as a
dimension would fail the net as
badly as misreading a torus, because the point of the net is
that the instrument must know what it cannot measure.

The net also produced a finding about the estimator the program's
informal practice suggests, the log-log slope of the ball volume
itself.  On the same exact controls that slope reads $0.896$, $1.771$,
and $2.657$ against exact values of $1$, $2$, and $3$.  The origin is
elementary.  A ball volume growing as $r^d$ with a subleading term
of relative order $1/r$ has a log-log slope that sits below $d$ by a term of
order $1/r$ at every finite radius, and over practical windows the
accumulated deficit is $0.10$ to $0.34$ in the measured cases.  Any
dimension read from a ball-volume slope on a small graph inherits this
bias, and it acts in one direction, toward reading low.  The shell
form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dhat} removes the accumulation by
differentiating first, which is why it is the instrument used in
Secs.~\ref{sec:wm4} and~\ref{sec:wm2h}.

\section{The second law of a reversible automaton is observational}
\label{sec:wm1}

The program's second-law claim is already observer-relative.
Computational irreducibility makes the microdynamics opaque to a
bounded observer, and the entropy that observer assigns grows
\cite{Wolfram2023}.  The campaign's entropy track proved a sharpened
form of this statement for reversible hidden dynamics, an observed
binned entropy that swings while the hidden ensemble is recovered
exactly, so that the entire change is observational
\cite{EntropyPaper,SourcesPaper}.  This audit transplants that
protocol onto the automaton family the program itself uses.

The system is a second-order reversible elementary automaton,
\begin{equation}
  a_{t+1} \;=\; F(a_t) \oplus a_{t-1},
  \label{eq:secondorder}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is an elementary rule applied to the current row and
$\oplus$ is addition modulo two.  The update is invertible by
construction, since $a_{t-1} = F(a_t) \oplus a_{t+1}$ recovers the
past exactly, in the family reviewed by Toffoli and Margolus
\cite{ToffoliMargolus1990} and explored in the program's foundational
book \cite{Wolfram2002}.  The declared instance takes $F$ to be
Rule~122, the featured example of the program's own second-law
writings \cite{Wolfram2023}, on $1024$ cells with periodic boundary
for $1500$ steps, from a random $256$-cell center region with the
prior row zero.  The declared coarse-graining is the Shannon entropy
of the empirical histogram of non-overlapping four-cell blocks,
declared before the run.  The arithmetic is exact over the two-element
field, so the reversed trajectory is not approximately the forward
trajectory, it is the forward trajectory bitwise, or the
implementation is wrong.

The measured result.  The coarse-grained entropy rises from $1.646$
bits at the ordered seed to $3.288$ bits at the turnaround, a rise of
$1.641$ bits against a declared bar of $1.0$.  Under reversal the
entropy curve retraces with a maximum defect of exactly $0.0$ bits at
every mirrored time, and the initial pair of configurations is
recovered exactly, cell for cell.  The entropy of the finite
reversible system fluctuates along the way, as it must, and the claim
is the rise from the ordered seed together with the exact retrace,
not monotonicity.

The reading is the campaign's entropy-track conclusion verbatim.  The
dynamics loses nothing, the observer's coarse-graining produces the
entire entropy change, and the second law of this reversible
computational system is observational entropy.  This is the program's
own account
\cite{Wolfram2023}, converted into a statement with an exact witness,
and any second-law claim for reversible rewriting systems now has a
measured form to be compared against.

Three instrument findings bound the protocol's scope.  First, the reversible form
of Rule~30, the program's flagship pseudorandomizer, runs from a
single seed into a dense phase in which the rule's output vanishes on
saturated neighborhoods and the block entropy falls, so that rule and
seed pair never randomizes under the declared coarse-graining.
Second, the symmetric initialization that sets the prior row equal to
the current row makes the orbit time-reflection symmetric, and
Rule~122 then shows a near-recurrence toward order within a thousand
steps, so the initialization must be asymmetric for the rise to be
sustained.  Third, eight-cell byte blocks are too coarse for
structured automaton fields and four-cell blocks resolve the growth.
A fourth note concerns the bar itself.  An early version of the
reversal swapped the wrong pair of rows, a one-step misalignment, and
the exact-retrace bar caught it immediately, which is what a bar set
at exactly zero is for.

\section{Strings under a change of update order}
\label{sec:wm2}

Causal invariance is the program's stated basis for relativistic
claims \cite{Gorard2020a}.  Its operational content is that the
physics of a rewriting system should not depend on the order in which
applicable rules are applied.  The campaign audits exactly that, with
the update order as a prescription in the sense of its earlier
threshold finding, under three declared orders, leftmost match,
rightmost match, and seeded random match, all from the same declared
initial string of $60$ letters over the alphabet $\{A,B\}$.

The causally invariant control is the sorting rule $BA \to AB$.  Each
event removes exactly one inversion, one misordered pair of letters,
so the algebra fixes the event
count in advance.  The measured result agrees exactly.  Under every
one of the three orders the rule terminates in the same sorted string
after exactly $406$ events, and $406$ is the inversion number of the
declared initial string.  The invariance is not approximate and not
statistical.  It is the confluence of the rule made visible, and it is
what a genuine causal-invariance claim looks like when measured.

The planted trap is the rule pair $\{AB \to B,\ BA \to A\}$, built to
be non-confluent because overlapping matches conflict.  The pipeline
must flag it or the run is void.  It fires.  The three orders
terminate in three distinct strings, $B$, $BBBB$, and $BB$, after $7$,
$4$, and $6$ events respectively.  Everything about this rule's
outcome, its terminal state, its event count, its history, belongs to
the update order, and the pipeline records it as prescription-borne.
The trap discharges the campaign's standing obligation to prove that
the pipeline can catch the artifact class it is hunting.

The growth rule $A \to AB$, run for $200$ declared events, splits
along exactly the line the campaign's fold theorem predicts.  The
conserved quantities agree exactly across all three orders, two $A$
letters and $201$ $B$ letters in a final string of length $203$,
because each event preserves the $A$ count and adds exactly one $B$
regardless of where it applies.  The arrangement of those letters
differs between orders.  Counts protected by a conservation law are
prescription-invariant while the configuration carrying them is
prescription-borne, the signed-versus-unsigned split of the campaign's
earlier work measured here in rewriting form.

\section{Causal graphs and foliations}
\label{sec:wm3}

The previous section audited terminal states.  This one audits the
causal structure itself, the object the program's relativistic claims
live on \cite{Gorard2020a}.  The causal graph is built from token
genealogy.  Each event consumes tokens and produces tokens, and a
causal edge runs from the event that produced a token to the event
that consumed it.  The audit runs the sorting rule on a declared
$24$-letter string under leftmost and rightmost orders and compares
declared invariants of the two causal graphs, then sweeps foliations
of one graph.

For the sorting rule the causal-graph invariants are identical across
prescriptions.  Both orders give $55$ events, $88$ causal edges,
causal depth $13$, and the same out-degree multiset, one event of
out-degree zero, twenty of out-degree one, and thirty-four of
out-degree two.  The causal structure of the confluent rule is a
prescription-independent object, measured rather than asserted.

The trap's causal graphs differ radically, as they must.  The leftmost
order gives $7$ events joined by $6$ causal edges, while the rightmost
order gives $4$ events with no causal edges at all, a causally
disconnected history in which every event consumes only initial
tokens.  For a non-confluent rule not even the causal graph's gross
shape survives the prescription, which is the precise sense in which
relativistic claims require causal invariance rather than merely
invoke it.

The foliation sweep then measures the observer's remaining freedom on
the one invariant causal graph.  A valid foliation is a sequence of
antichains respecting the causal partial order and exhausting the
graph.  The earliest and latest layerings both use exactly $13$
slices, achieving the causal depth bound exactly, with maximum slice
width $7$.  Five seeded random foliations use $19$ to $26$ slices with
maximum widths of $4$ and $5$.  The invariants are frozen while the
number of slices an observer spends, the simultaneity bookkeeping,
moves by a factor of two between the extremal and the sampled random
foliations.  This is the campaign's foliation lesson in rewriting
form.  What varies under a change of slicing is the observer's
accounting, and what survives is the partial order, so any claimed
structure must be checked against the invariants and not against the
accounting.

\section{A dimension claim that survives held-out scaling}
\label{sec:wm4}

With the estimator net passed, the audit turns to the program's
dimension claim on a hypergraph rule of the program's own class
\cite{Wolfram2020}.  The declared rule is
\begin{equation}
  \{\{x,y\},\{x,z\}\} \;\to\;
  \{\{x,z\},\{x,w\},\{y,w\},\{z,w\}\},
  \label{eq:wm4rule}
\end{equation}
where $w$ is a fresh vertex, run from a double self-loop for $11$
generations under one declared prescription, generational maximal
scan-ordered non-overlapping matching.  The final hypergraph has
$1794$ relations on $897$ vertices.  The shell estimator of
Sec.~\ref{sec:wm0} is calibrated on the training radii $2 \le r \le 5$
over $30$ declared base points, giving $\hat d = 2.305$, and the
claim is then tested on the held-out radii $6 \le r \le 9$, which were
never used in fitting.  Two preregistered growth laws, the
finite-dimension power law and the exponential alternative that a
non-finite-dimensional graph would follow, are each calibrated on the
training window and made to predict the held-out shell counts.

Figure~\ref{fig:wm4} shows the held-out comparison.  The power law
predicts the four held-out shell counts with a summed squared error of
$0.028$ in log space, while the exponential alternative accumulates
$1.09$, a factor of forty in favor of the finite-dimension reading.
The measured shells at radii $6$ through $9$ are $36.8$, $47.6$,
$57.0$, and $69.3$, and the power-law predictions track them to
within eight counts.  This dimension claim genuinely survives the audit at
the measured sizes.  The
measurement is at finite radius on a finite object, a crossover
beyond the held-out window cannot be excluded, and the asymptotic
dimension claim of the program is not settled by any finite-size
audit.  A second caveat is measured in the next
section.

\section{The capstone, hypergraph bookkeeping is prescription-borne}
\label{sec:wm2h}

The string audit of Sec.~\ref{sec:wm2} found exact prescription
invariance where confluence protects it.  The capstone audit asks the
same question of the hypergraph rule of Eq.~\eqref{eq:wm4rule}, whose
left-hand side consumes two relations sharing a vertex, so that
candidate matches overlap and a maximal non-overlapping matching
depends on the order in which candidates are scanned.  Five match
orders were declared in advance, the storage order of the relation
list, that order reversed, two seeded random permutations, and a sort
by second vertex.  Each order evolves the same initial condition for
the same $11$ generations, and the same declared quantities are
measured on each result over $24$ base points to radius $9$, a
configuration distinct from the WM-4 calibration, which fitted the
training radii with thirty base points, so the storage order reads
$2.454$ here where WM-4 read $2.305$, and the prescription caveat is
measured in this capstone configuration.

Table~\ref{tab:wm2h} and Fig.~\ref{fig:wm2h} show the result.  The
five prescriptions give five different evolutions.  Relation counts
span $1710$ to $1882$, vertex counts span $855$ to $941$, degree sums
span $3420$ to $3762$, and the shell-estimator dimension spans $2.43$
to $2.88$, a seventeen percent spread against the audit's five percent
invariance bar.  Because matches conflict, even the number of events
per generation is a prescription choice, so the bulk bookkeeping of
this rule, its sizes, its degree structure, and its measured
dimension, is prescription-borne at these sizes.  The contrast with
the string audit is the finding.  The sorting rule was exact under
every order because an algebraic property, confluence with a
conserved inversion count, protected it.  The growth rule has no such
protection, and the audit measures the consequence.

The WM-4 verdict of Sec.~\ref{sec:wm4} therefore inherits a measured
caveat.  Its dimension of $2.305$ was measured under the storage-order
prescription, and the same rule under other declared orders yields
dimensions up to $2.88$.  One part of the verdict is robust.  The
power law beats the exponential alternative under every one of the
five prescriptions, with error ratios from six-fold to several
hundred-fold, so the qualitative finding of finite-dimensional
scaling at measured sizes survives the prescription sweep, while the
quantitative value of the dimension does not.  A dimension claim for
a rule with match conflicts must either declare its prescription or
report a prescription band, and at these sizes that band is wide.

The planted trap of this audit is a contraction rule on a
twelve-relation path with a three-branch fork at its midpoint, run to
exhaustion.  It fires as required, terminating in three distinct
final states across the prescriptions, and the pipeline flags it.
The trap's own construction preserved a lesson.  The bare contraction
path without the fork was found confluent en route, any contraction
order composes the path to a single edge, so a trap must contain a
genuine conflict and not merely an aesthetic of disorder.  The fork
supplies the conflict, and the recorded lesson is part of the audit's
evidence.

\section{The scorecard}
\label{sec:scorecard}

Assembled across the six audits, the verdicts sort into three classes,
and the sorting principle is visible in every row.

Invariant, exactly.  The sorting rule's terminal state and event
count, protected by confluence and equal to the inversion number.
The conserved letter counts of the growth rule, protected by a
conservation law.  The causal-graph invariants of the confluent rule,
identical across prescriptions.  The causal depth bound, achieved
exactly by the extremal foliations.  And, robustly though not
exactly, the qualitative finite-dimension verdict of the hypergraph
rule, which survives both a held-out test and a five-prescription
sweep.

Observational.  The second law of the reversible automaton.  The
entropy rise of $1.641$ bits is real for the declared observer and
the dynamics underneath it loses nothing, with retrace defect exactly
zero and exact microstate recovery.  This class is where the
program's own second-law account already lives, now with a measured
witness.

Prescription-borne.  The arrangement of the growth rule's letters.
The entire outcome of every planted non-confluent rule.  And the bulk
bookkeeping of the flagship hypergraph rule, its relation counts, its
vertex counts, its degree structure, and its quantitative dimension
at these sizes, spread across a seventeen percent band by declared
match orders.

The pattern is uniform and is the paper's summary finding.  A
quantity is exact across observers and prescriptions precisely where
an algebraic property protects it, a confluence proof, a conservation
law, an antichain bound, and it is prescription-borne wherever
matching conflicts exist and no such protection applies.  Claims of
the Wolfram program that rest on protected quantities measured with
validated instruments survive these audits and come out stronger, as
the fair-framing clause intended.  Claims that rest on bulk
bookkeeping of rules with match conflicts inherit a measured
prescription caveat, and the naive dimension estimator adds a
one-directional bias on top.  None of this decides the program's
physics.  It decides which of the program's numbers are about the
rules and which are about the bookkeeping.

\section{Methods and reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods}

All audits follow the campaign's standing discipline.  Estimator
windows, tolerances, coarse-grainings, prescription lists, base-point
counts, seeds, and alternative growth laws were declared before the
runs they govern, per the track's anti-circularity contract, and the
fair-framing clause of the track document, that the audits test
claims rather than people and that a surviving claim comes out
stronger, governs the reading of every verdict.  Every result-bearing
run writes an append-only JSON evidence record containing the
complete declared parameter set, outcome arrays, and a canonical
SHA-256 hash of the record body.  The records for this paper are
\texttt{wm0-dimension.json}, \texttt{wm1-reversible-ca.json},
\texttt{wm2-prescription.json}, \texttt{wm3-foliation.json},
\texttt{wm4-hypergraph.json}, and \texttt{wm2h-prescription.json} in
the campaign repository, each carrying the exploratory label of the
campaign's evidence-grading scheme, with the committed runners
alongside them.  Every prescription audit carries a planted
non-confluent trap whose detection is a validity condition of the
run, and both traps fired.  Every number and every figure coordinate
in this paper is read from those committed records, and the figure
tables are generated deterministically by a committed script with no
free parameters.  A reducer may summarize records but may not rewrite
them.
