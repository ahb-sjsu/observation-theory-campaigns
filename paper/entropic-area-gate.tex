\title{An Exact Area Gate for Entropic Accounts of Geometry:\\
Volume Laws, Count Splitting, and the Causal-Determinism Mechanism}

\author{Andrew H. Bond}
\email{andrew.bond@sjsu.edu}
\thanks{ORCID: 0009-0003-2599-6158}
\affiliation{San Jos\'e State University, San Jos\'e, California, USA}
\date{August 5, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Entropic accounts of gravity require that the number of independent states
associated with a spatial region scale with the area of its boundary.
Generic hidden multiplicity scales with volume instead, so the requirement
is a gate that any candidate mechanism must pass, and the expected outcome
at the gate is failure.  This paper reports exact measurements at that gate
for declared mechanism classes on two-dimensional lattices, with every
count exact and every closed form reproduced across two decades of region
size.  An independent-spin control shows the guaranteed volume law.  The
two most concrete constraint mechanisms, a Gauss-law ensemble and its
gauge-quotient reading, fail the gate with an exact volume law in the
primary count, the conditional entropy of the region given its boundary.
The same ensemble carries an exact area law in its mutual information with
the rest of the lattice, so the two counts split, and the split is
measured exactly.  A third arm shows that an observer restricted to
boundary data obtains an area-scaling image while the hidden interior
keeps a volume ambiguity, so an access limit is not a distinguishability
limit.  An elementary lemma makes the negatives structural.  Any
translation-invariant ensemble with positive entropy density forces a
volume law in the primary count, so every mechanism class in that regime
fails before it is built.  The lemma points to ensembles with zero entropy
density, whose natural uninserted origin is deterministic local dynamics
with the region read as a spacetime region, and a causal-cone theorem
shows that every deterministic nearest-neighbor rule bounds the states of
a square spacetime patch by its perimeter, with nothing tuned.  Nothing in
this paper bears on physical gravity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Every derivation of gravity from entropy begins by assuming that the
entropy associated with a region of space is proportional to the area of
the region's boundary.  Jacobson derived the Einstein equation as an
equation of state from exactly that proportionality together with a local
form of the Clausius relation \cite{Jacobson1995}.  The recent lineage
descending from that argument, an entropic action built from quantum
relative entropy \cite{Bianconi2025}, a modular-theory refinement on
bifurcate Killing horizons \cite{DorauMuch2026}, and an equilibrium
treatment of stretched light cones \cite{AlonsoSerrano2025}, refines the
machinery but keeps the same input.  In each case the proportionality of
entropy to area is assumed, not derived.  None of these works says what
the microscopic states are, or why counting them should follow the
boundary of a region rather than its bulk.

That assumption is where a constructive program must start, because it is
where generic counting fails.  If an observer sees a projection of a
hidden system, the hidden states compatible with a region of observations
generically multiply with the number of hidden degrees of freedom inside
the region, which grows with the region's volume.  A framework in which
gravity is to emerge from hidden-state counting therefore faces a gate.
Some declared structure must make the independently distinguishable
states of a region scale with its boundary, and must do so for a
structural reason that was not itself inserted.  The present campaign
calls this the area gate, treats failure as the expected outcome, and
treats a clean negative as a result.

This paper reports the campaign's passage to and through that gate at the
level of exact counts.  Four results are reported, and each carries an
explicit evidence label.  First, an instrument layer, validated against
closed forms, that measures conditional entropy and relative entropy of
hidden-state ensembles at finite resolution and is invariant under
relabeling of the hidden coordinates.  Second, exact measurements of the
gate itself for three declared arms on two-dimensional lattices, an
independent-spin control, a Gauss-law constraint ensemble read two ways,
and a boundary-limited observer, across two decades of region size with
every count an integer computed in closed form and verified at every
size.  The constraint mechanisms fail the gate in the primary count, and
the failure is exact.  The same measurement finds an exact area law in a
secondary count, the mutual information between the region and the rest
of the lattice, so the two counts split and the split itself is a
measured, exact structure.  Third, an elementary lemma, proved here,
showing that any translation-invariant ensemble with positive entropy
density must fail the gate, which converts the measured negatives from
instances into a classification.  Fourth, a theorem about deterministic
local dynamics, proved here, showing that the states of a square
spacetime patch under any deterministic nearest-neighbor rule are bounded
by causal-cone data whose size scales with the patch perimeter.  This
identifies a mechanism class, causal determinism, that satisfies the
gate's scaling requirement for every rule in the class, with nothing
tuned.  A measured instance and its structural-origin audit are specified,
and their numbers are bound to a committed evidence record.

The scope of these claims is narrow.  The lemma and the theorem are
statements about lattice ensembles and cellular automata.  The
measurements are exact counts in declared toy constructions.  No claim is
made about physical spacetime, about real gravitation, or about the
correctness of any entropic-gravity proposal.  What the paper establishes
is a discipline.  It says which mechanism classes cannot produce the
scaling those proposals assume, it exhibits one class that provably can,
and it shows that the choice of operational count decides the verdict, so
that any future claim of area scaling must state its count before it
states its result.

\section{The gate and the operational count}
\label{sec:gate}

The setting is the campaign's standard one.  A hidden state $Z$ lives in
a declared state space, an observer sees $X = \pi(Z)$ for a declared
observation map $\pi$, and every ensemble, resolution, and measure is
fixed before any claim-bearing run.  The experiments of this paper use
finite lattices, so every entropy below is a finite Shannon entropy in
bits and every count is exact.

Fix a region $Q_R$ of linear size $R$, with boundary $\partial Q_R$ and
interior $Q_R^{\circ}$.  The gate asks how the number of independently
distinguishable hidden states associated with $Q_R$ scales with $R$.  On
the two-dimensional lattices used here, volume scaling means growth
proportional to $R^2$ and area scaling means growth proportional to $R$.
The phrase ``independently distinguishable states associated with a
region'' admits several formalizations, and they disagree, so the choice
had to be made before any run.  The track document records the decision
in these words.  ``The primary count is the conditional entropy of the
region's hidden state at resolution delta given the boundary data,
$H(\text{region}\,|\,\text{boundary})$, because it directly counts
interior distinctions not fixed by the boundary, which is what the gate's
independence requirement is about.''  The mutual information
$I(\text{region}\,;\,\text{complement})$ is a mandatory secondary
witness, computed in every arm, because the two counts can diverge and
the divergence is itself informative.  Any arm whose verdict differs
between the two counts is reported under both, with the primary count
deciding the gate.

One standing warning from the track design governs the reading of every
arm.  Concentration of measure near a boundary is not area scaling.  A
measure concentrated in a thin shell near the boundary still counts
volume-independent distinctions unless the interior distinctions are
genuinely not independent.  The gate is about independence of
distinctions, not about where the measure sits, and the third arm below
is that warning made quantitative.

\section{The instrument layer}
\label{sec:eg0}

Before the gate could be measured, the instruments had to be.  The
instrument experiment extends the campaign's projection-entropy stack
\cite{EntropyPaper,SourcesPaper} to the full conditional-entropy surface
and to relative entropy between deformed and undeformed fiber measures,
and validates every part against exact closed forms.  All numbers in this
section are from the committed evidence record of that run.

The first part reproduces the campaign's published closed-form controls.
The fold's branch entropy is measured at one bit within
$1.4\times10^{-14}$, the symmetric slice of the double fold at exactly
$1.5$ bits, the degenerate cubic at exactly zero bits, and the binned
caustic entropy approaches its differential limit within
$1.4\times10^{-3}$ at bin width $10^{-5}$, matching the published
resolution law.  The second part measures the conditional-entropy surface
$H(Z_\delta \mid X_\epsilon)$ at hidden resolution $\delta$ and observed
resolution $\epsilon$.  An aligned linear control reproduces its exact
value of $\log_2 100$ bits within $1.6\times10^{-14}$, and both
resolution sweeps converge, with successive deviations shrinking by a
factor of $6.2$ per refinement step in $\delta$ and $1.4$ per step in
$\epsilon$.  The third part measures relative entropy between fiber
measures.  Nested uniform fibers reproduce their exact value of $2$ bits
within $4.4\times10^{-16}$.  A Gaussian pair reproduces the closed-form
divergence of $0.2644$ bits within $1.75\times10^{-10}$, and a widening
control identifies that floor as range truncation, since the error falls
to $1.7\times10^{-16}$ when the integration range is widened to fourteen
standard deviations.  A piecewise-deformed fold matches the binary
divergence closed form within $3.3\times10^{-16}$ in every one of its
$90$ interior bins.  The fourth part verifies reparametrization
invariance.  Relabeling the hidden coordinate changes no witness beyond
$1.4\times10^{-16}$, and Jacobian-weighted re-gridding changes none
beyond $8.2\times10^{-16}$, shrinking to $4.4\times10^{-16}$ at quarter
resolution, which identifies the residual as quadrature rounding rather
than a coordinate dependence.  An instrument whose entropies moved under
a hidden coordinate change would be measuring the coordinates, not the
ensemble, and this check is what entitles the counts below to be called
properties of the constructions.

\section{Three arms at the area gate}
\label{sec:eg1}

The gate experiment measures three declared arms on two-dimensional
lattices at region sizes $R$ from $3$ to $301$, fifteen sizes spanning
two decades.  Each arm reports the primary count
$H(Q_R^{\circ} \mid \partial Q_R)$ and the secondary count
$I(Q_R\,;\,\text{complement})$ at every size.  Every reported number is
an exact integer count in bits, every closed form quoted below is
reproduced by the measured count at every one of the fifteen sizes, and
the scaling fits compare a free power law against the two fixed
alternatives, exponent $2$ for volume and exponent $1$ for area, all
declared before the run.  All numbers in this section are from the
committed evidence record of the gate run.

\subsection{Arm A, independent spins}

The control arm places an independent uniform binary spin on every site.
The primary count is exactly $(R-2)^2$ bits at every $R$, one bit for
every interior site, and the mutual information with the complement is
exactly zero at every $R$.  The fitted free slope over the full range is
$2.328$, and the fixed-exponent comparison prefers volume, with residual
$0.869$ against exponent $2$ versus $2.862$ against exponent $1$.  The
free slope exceeds $2$ because the offset in $(R-2)^2$ bends the small
end of the log-log line, not because the count is superextensive.  This
is the guaranteed volume law that the gate exists to beat.

\subsection{Arm B, Gauss-law constraint and gauge quotient}

The first serious mechanisms are interior constraint and gauge
redundancy.  Both are realized in one construction.  Binary variables
live on the edges of a periodic square lattice, a torus of side
$L = 2R + 9$ so that the embedding lattice grows with the region and the
padding stays fixed, and the ensemble is uniform over the configurations
satisfying a constraint of Gauss-law type at every vertex, meaning that
every vertex touches an even number of occupied edges.  The satisfying
configurations form the cycle space of the lattice graph, the set of
edge subsets in which every vertex has even degree, which is a linear
space over the two-element field.  The Gauss-law reading counts this
constrained ensemble directly.  The gauge-quotient reading counts
equivalence classes under a symmetry group acting on vertex variables,
and its quotient count is the same cycle-space count, so the two
mechanism classes share one measurement.  Every entropy of this ensemble
equals the dimension of a cycle space, and those dimensions are computed
exactly by counting connected components of the relevant graphs, so no
estimator appears anywhere in the arm.

The region $Q_R$ is an $R \times R$ patch of the torus.  The measured
primary count is exactly $(R-1)^2$ bits at every $R$.  Given the entire
boundary of the patch, the interior still carries one exact bit per
interior plaquette.  The fitted free slope is $2.132$, with residual
$0.333$ against exponent $2$ versus $2.405$ against exponent $1$, so the
arm is classified volume and the two most concrete constraint mechanisms
fail the gate in the primary count.  The constraint removes exactly the
boundary-linked distinctions and not one more.  The interior of a
Gauss-law ensemble is not boundary data in disguise.  It is an extensive
reservoir of independent distinctions, and the gate's independence
requirement sees it.

The same ensemble then produces the finding beyond the negative.  Its
mutual information with the complement is exactly $4R - 5$ bits at every
$R$, an exact area law, with fitted free slope $1.086$ and residual
$0.220$ against exponent $1$ versus $1.939$ against exponent $2$.  The
marginal entropy of the patch equals $(R-1)^2 + 4R - 5$ bits exactly, the
two pieces adding with no interference term.  The counts split.
Constraint structure does put area scaling into the correlations between
a region and its complement, exactly where the classical Gibbs-ensemble
confound anticipated by the track design would put it, while leaving
interior independence extensive.  The preregistered decision quoted in
Sec.~\ref{sec:gate} is what makes this a clean verdict rather than an
ambiguity.  An entropic-gravity reading built on this mechanism class
would have to live on the correlation count, which the decision relegated
to secondary, and would have to justify that choice against the
independence requirement the gate is about.  The split is measured in one construction
on two-dimensional lattices, and nothing here decides how general it is.

\subsection{Arm C, boundary-limited access}

The third arm implements the mechanism most tempting to claim and
measures why it does not count.  The observation map reads the interior
of the region only through its boundary data, so the observer-accessible
image carries exactly $4R - 4$ bits by construction, area scaling with
fitted free slope $1.066$.  The hidden interior, however, retains a
residual of exactly $(R-2)^2$ bits that no observation can reach, a
volume law with fitted free slope $2.328$, identical to the control arm.
Restricting access manufactures an area-scaling image without removing a
single interior distinction.  The distinctions are still there, still
independent, and still extensive.  This is the standing warning of the
track design made quantitative.  An access limit is not a
distinguishability limit, and an area law obtained by refusing to look at
the interior is not a pass at this gate.

\section{The entropy-density lemma}
\label{sec:lemma}

The measured negatives are not accidents of the particular arms.  They
are forced by an elementary property that all three arms share, a
positive density of entropy per site.  The following lemma makes this
exact and is the paper's organizing statement.

\begin{lemma}[Positive entropy density forces a volume law]
\label{lem:density}
Let each site of the two-dimensional integer lattice carry a symbol from
a finite alphabet $A$, let the ensemble of configurations be invariant
under lattice translations, let $Q_R$ denote the square of $R \times R$
sites with boundary $\partial Q_R$ and interior $Q_R^{\circ}$, and let
\begin{equation}
  h \;=\; \inf_{R \ge 1} \frac{H(Q_R)}{R^2}
\end{equation}
be the entropy density in bits per site.  Then for every $R \ge 2$,
\begin{equation}
  H\!\left(Q_R^{\circ} \,\middle|\, \partial Q_R\right)
  \;\ge\; h R^2 - c R,
  \qquad c = 4 \log_2 |A| .
  \label{eq:lemma}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if the primary count is bounded by $aR$ for all $R$ and
some constant $a$, then $h = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Translation invariance and subadditivity of joint entropy give
$H(Q_{mR}) \le m^2 H(Q_R)$ for all $m$, since $Q_{mR}$ is covered by
$m^2$ translates of $Q_R$ whose marginal entropies are equal, and joint
entropy is at most the sum of marginals \cite{CoverThomas2006}.  By the
standard subadditivity argument the sequence $H(Q_R)/R^2$ converges and
its limit equals the stated infimum, so $H(Q_R) \ge h R^2$ for every
$R$.  The chain rule splits the region as
\begin{equation}
  H(Q_R) \;=\; H(\partial Q_R)
  + H\!\left(Q_R^{\circ} \,\middle|\, \partial Q_R\right).
\end{equation}
The boundary has $4R - 4$ sites, each carrying at most $\log_2 |A|$
bits, so $H(\partial Q_R) \le (4R-4)\log_2|A| \le cR$, and
\eqref{eq:lemma} follows.  If additionally
$H(Q_R^{\circ} \mid \partial Q_R) \le aR$ for all $R$, then
$h \le (a + c)/R$ for every $R$, so $h = 0$.
\end{proof}

The lemma converts the gate run into a classification.  Arm A has
entropy density one bit per site.  Arm B has positive entropy density,
one bit per plaquette in the limit, which is why its constraint could
remove only a perimeter's worth of distinctions.  Arm C inherits the
density of its hidden ensemble untouched, because access does not enter
the lemma at all.  Every mechanism class whose declared ensemble has
positive entropy density fails the primary count before it is
constructed, and this includes generic realizations of most of the
mechanism families the track design listed.  The lemma is proved for
translation-invariant ensembles on two-dimensional lattices with finite
alphabets, and the same argument runs in any dimension with volume and
boundary read accordingly.  A mechanism can pass the gate only from
inside the class the lemma leaves open, ensembles whose bulk entropy
density is exactly zero.

\section{The causal-determinism mechanism}
\label{sec:eg1b}

\subsection{From zero density to determinism}

Zero entropy density is a strange-looking requirement for an ensemble
that must still contain states worth counting, but one class satisfies
it without any insertion.  If the hidden configuration is generated by
deterministic local dynamics from initial data, and the region is read
as a spacetime region, a patch of the history rather than a snapshot,
then all randomness lives on the initial surface and the bulk of the
patch adds none.  The track recorded this as a corollary decision before
any run of the mechanism.  For deterministic dynamics the conditional
count $H(\text{interior} \mid \text{boundary})$ is exactly zero, which
is degenerate rather than informative, so the operational count of
states associated with a spacetime region is the marginal entropy of
the region, compared against spacetime volume and spacetime boundary,
with the conditional count still computed and reported.  A pass
additionally requires a structural-origin audit.  The same construction
with the structural feature removed, by adding bulk noise, must revert
to a volume law, showing that the scaling tracks the declared structure
and nothing tuned.

\subsection{The cone bound}

The mechanism's scaling is not a property of a favorable rule.  It is a
theorem about the entire class.

\begin{theorem}[Causal-cone bound]
\label{thm:cone}
Let a deterministic rule of radius one update a row of binary cells on
the one-dimensional integer lattice, each cell's next value a fixed
function of its own value and the values of its two nearest neighbors.
Read the resulting spacetime as a plane whose rows are successive time
steps, and let $P_R$ be a patch of $R$ consecutive cells over $R$
consecutive time steps.  Then the configuration of $P_R$ is a function
of the initial data on a segment of $3R - 2$ cells, and therefore for
every probability ensemble on initial conditions
\begin{equation}
  H(P_R) \;\le\; 3R - 2 \ \text{bits}.
  \label{eq:cone}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let the patch occupy columns $x_0$ through $x_0 + R - 1$ and rows $t_0$
through $t_0 + R - 1$, and let $S$ be the segment of row $t_0$ from
column $x_0 - (R-1)$ to column $x_0 + R - 1 + (R-1)$, which has
$3R - 2$ cells.  We show by induction on $k$ that the restriction of row
$t_0 + k$ to columns $x_0 - (R-1-k)$ through $x_0 + R - 1 + (R-1-k)$ is
a function of $S$.  For $k = 0$ this restriction is a subset of $S$.
For the step, each cell of row $t_0 + k + 1$ in the stated range depends
only on three cells of row $t_0 + k$ lying in the previous range, since
the rule has radius one.  Taking $k = 0$ through $R - 1$ shows every
cell of the patch is a function of $S$.  The entropy of a function of a
random variable is at most the entropy of that variable, which is at
most its cell count in bits, giving \eqref{eq:cone}.
\end{proof}

The bound $3R - 2$ is linear in $R$, the scaling of the patch perimeter,
while the patch has $R^2$ cells.  The bound holds for any rule of the
class and any initial ensemble, so nothing about it is tuned, selected,
or fitted, and for an alphabet of $q$ symbols the same argument gives
$(3R-2)\log_2 q$.  For a rule of radius $r$ the segment has
$R + 2r(R-1)$ cells, still perimeter scaling for every fixed $r$.  The
companion fact recorded in the corollary decision is equally elementary.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic interiors are boundary functions]
\label{prop:interior}
For any deterministic radius-one rule, the interior of a spacetime patch
$P_R$ is a function of the boundary of $P_R$, so
$H(P_R^{\circ} \mid \partial P_R) = 0$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The boundary contains the patch's top row and its two side columns.
Given the full top row of the patch, the rule determines the next row's
interior cells, whose missing endpoints are supplied by the side
columns, so the next row is known in full.  Iterating down the patch
determines every interior cell.
\end{proof}

The proposition is why the primary count of Sec.~\ref{sec:gate} is
degenerate here and the marginal count decides, exactly as the corollary
decision required.  The causal-determinism mechanism thus passes the
gate's scaling requirement in the only count that is informative for it,
by a proof that quantifies over the whole rule class.

\subsection{The measured instance and its audit}

The measured companion to the theorem, the mechanism run of the
campaign, instantiates the class with the elementary rule numbered
ninety, the linear rule in which each cell updates to the sum modulo two
of its two neighbors.  For a linear rule acting on uniform initial data
the patch ensemble is uniform over a linear subspace, so its entropy in
bits equals the rank of that subspace over the two-element field and is
computed exactly.  The run measures patch counts over a sweep of patch
sizes up to $R = \egbRmax$, together with the structural-origin audit,
the same construction with one fresh uniform bit added to each bulk
cell after its deterministic update at each step, equivalent to
independent flips with probability $\egbNoiseRate$.  The audit
arm restores a positive bulk entropy density, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:density} its count must revert toward volume if the
deterministic structure, and nothing else, was carrying the perimeter
scaling.

The numbers of this subsection are bound to the committed evidence
record of the mechanism run.  At the largest patch size the rule-ninety
count is $\egbRankNinety$ bits against the cone bound of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cone}, a ratio of $\egbConeRatioNinety$, and the same
saturation holds exactly at every measured size.  Over the full
sweep the deterministic arm has fitted free slope $\egbSlopeDet$ and is
classified \egbClassDet{} against the fixed alternatives.  The audit arm

\section{Relation to entropic accounts of geometry}
\label{sec:literature}

The results bear on the entropic-gravity lineage at exactly one point,
the input every member of the lineage shares.  Jacobson's derivation
consumes an entropy proportional to horizon area
\cite{Jacobson1995}.  Bianconi's entropic action postulates a
relative-entropy functional whose weak-coupling limit is Einstein
gravity, with no microscopic ensemble whose coarse-graining produces its
density matrices \cite{Bianconi2025}.  Dorau and Much prove a
flux-entropy equality by modular theory but still consume the
entropy-area formula as an input \cite{DorauMuch2026}.  The stretched
light-cone treatment consumes the same proportionality together with an
assumed temperature \cite{AlonsoSerrano2025}.  In every case the area
law is purchased, not earned.  The present paper prices that purchase in
a setting where everything can be counted.  Constraint and gauge
structure, the mechanisms closest to how the lineage's states are
usually imagined, provably do not deliver an area law in the
independence count, and deliver an exact one in the correlation count,
so which count a proposal's entropy refers to is not a technicality but
the whole question.  A proposal whose microscopic states are ever
specified will face this fork, and the causal-determinism class is, so
far, the one measured mechanism family that passes the scaling
requirement with a structural origin.

Two disciplines from the track's contract govern how far such statements
may be pushed, and they are the reason this paper's claims stop where
they do.  The first is the reverse-engineering caveat.  For any
potential one can define an entropy whose gradient reproduces the
corresponding force, so an entropic account earns content only when the
entropy is computed from an independently specified ensemble and
coarse-graining, with the target law nowhere inserted.  The second is
the anti-circularity contract that binds every run reported here.  The
ensembles, projections, measures, counts, and fit alternatives were
declared before the runs, the forbidden-input list excludes any inserted
area proportionality or target law, and a construction that obtained its
scaling by insertion would void the run rather than pass the gate.  The
boundary-limited arm exists precisely to demonstrate the nearest
temptation, an area law that is an artifact of access rather than a
property of the ensemble, and the audit arm of the mechanism run exists
to catch the corresponding temptation for determinism.  Nothing here supports or
undermines any entropic account of real gravity.  What is established is
which mechanism classes could never have supplied the states such
accounts assume, and which class provably could, in models where every
bit is countable.
