\title{One CSI Report Cannot Serve Every Consumer:\\
The Consumer-Relative False-Clear Rate in 5G/6G Link Adaptation}

\begin{abstract}
A channel-state-information (CSI) report is a certificate: an implicit claim that the
channel is good enough to transmit at a selected rate. That certificate is not read by
the channel; it is read by a \emph{consumer}---a scheduler with a reliability target and
a decoder at a moment in time. We make the rate at which a CSI certificate
\emph{false-clears} (selects a rate the link cannot support) a first-class, measured KPI
on a real 5G~NR link-level chain (Sionna LDPC decoding), and show it is
\emph{consumer-relative} along two axes. First, the reliability target: the identical
CSI-derived modulation-and-coding selection that meets an eMBB target
($\mathrm{BLER}=10^{-1}$) misses a URLLC target ($10^{-3}$) by more than two orders of
magnitude ($112\times$), while a target-aware policy meets it. Second, the refresh
horizon: as the report ages, the false-clear rate grows, and the largest safe report
period is proportional to the Clarke coherence time ($\approx 0.177\,T_{\mathrm{coh}}$,
$R^2=0.915$). The witnessed corrections that hold each axis---outer-loop link adaptation,
a coherence-driven refresh---already exist; our contribution is the measured number that
says when each is needed. All results are drawn from sealed, pre-registered records.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
CSI feedback, link adaptation, URLLC, outer-loop link adaptation, reliability, 5G NR.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Adaptive modulation and coding (AMC) turns a channel-state-information (CSI) report into
a rate decision: the transmitter selects the highest modulation-and-coding scheme (MCS)
whose required SINR the reported CSI clears. The report is thus a \emph{certificate}---an
implicit assertion that the link will decode at the chosen rate. Like any certificate,
it can be wrong in a way that matters only to whoever reads it. We call a wrong-in-the-
optimistic-direction certificate a \emph{false-clear}: the CSI cleared an MCS that the
true channel cannot support, and the transport fails (a HARQ NACK, a block error).

Our thesis is that the false-clear rate is not a property of the channel or even of the
report alone; it is a property of the report \emph{and its consumer}. Two schedulers
reading the same CSI can disagree on whether it false-clears, because they read it
against different requirements. We make the false-clear rate a measured KPI on a real
5G~NR link-level chain and demonstrate this consumer-relativity along two operationally
important axes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reliability target} (\S\ref{sec:urllc}): one CSI-derived MCS adequate for
enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB) is catastrophically optimistic for ultra-reliable
low-latency communication (URLLC)---a false-clear by more than $100\times$.
\item \textbf{Refresh horizon} (\S\ref{sec:refresh}): as the report ages the false-clear
rate grows, and the safe refresh period scales with the channel coherence time by a
simple measured law.
\end{itemize}
Neither observation requires a new estimator or scheduler. The corrections are known:
outer-loop link adaptation (OLLA)~\cite{olla} closes the target-relativity gap, and
coherence-driven CSI reporting closes the refresh gap. What has been missing is the
\emph{measured, consumer-relative false-clear rate} that quantifies when each correction
is load-bearing and how large the exposure is when it is absent.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Substrate.} We use the real 5G~NR LDPC decoder in Sionna~\cite{sionna} as the
error witness. For each MCS we first \emph{measure} a block-error curve $e_m(\gamma)$ by
decoding $400$ coded blocks per SNR point (grid $-8$ to $28$\,dB, $2$\,dB spacing) over
AWGN. The time-domain link then drives a per-TTI SNR trace through a 3GPP~TR38.901 TDL-A
channel (carrier $3.5$\,GHz, delay spread $30$\,ns) and, at each TTI, resolves the HARQ
outcome of the selected MCS from $e_m$ at the instantaneous SNR---a standard
link-to-system mapping built on \emph{measured} LDPC curves, not a fabricated formula.
The MCS set is the 3GPP NR table (TS~38.214 Table~5.1.3.1-1; QPSK--64-QAM, code rates
$R\ge 1/5$). Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters.

\textbf{System model.} Let $\gamma(t)$ (dB) be the true SNR at TTI $t$ and $\hat{g}(t)$
the CSI-reported estimate: for \emph{fresh} CSI $\hat{g}(t)=\gamma(t)+n(t)$,
$n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma=1$\,dB; for a report \emph{held} with period
$P$, $\hat{g}(t)=\gamma\!\left(P\lfloor t/P\rfloor\right)$, so the estimate ages. Let
$e_m(\gamma)$ be the measured LDPC error probability of MCS $m$ and
$\rho_m(\beta)=\min\{\gamma:e_m(\gamma)\le\beta\}$ its required SNR at target $\beta$. The
AMC \emph{certificate} selects, using a \emph{fixed reference} target $\beta_0=10^{-1}$
and backoff $\Delta$,
\begin{equation}
m^\star(\hat{g})=\max\{m\in\mathcal{M}:\rho_m(\beta_0)+\Delta\le\hat{g}\},\quad
\Delta{=}0\ \text{(naive)},
\label{eq:cert}
\end{equation}
so the \emph{operating point} is set by $\beta_0$ but the outcome is \emph{judged} by the
consumer's target $\beta$. The \emph{consumer} is $\beta$; the \emph{witness} is the HARQ
outcome $\mathrm{NACK}(t)\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(e_{m^\star}(\gamma(t))\big)$. A
false-clear is a cleared MCS the channel cannot support at $\beta$, i.e.\
$\rho_{m^\star}(\beta)>\gamma(t)$. We report two estimators of its rate. For the refresh
study (\S\ref{sec:refresh}) the \emph{empirical} HARQ NACK rate
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{FC}}=\tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{NACK}(t)],
\label{eq:fc_emp}
\end{equation}
which the outer loop consumes; for the reliability-target study (\S\ref{sec:urllc}),
whose $10^{-3}$ tail $T{=}6000$ TTI cannot resolve empirically, the \emph{expected} rate
$\mathrm{FC}(\beta)=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t e_{m^\star}(\gamma(t))$, read against $\beta$.
Because \eqref{eq:cert} fixes the operating point at $\beta_0$ while the verdict uses
$\beta$, one trace $\{\hat{g}(t),\gamma(t)\}$ yields different results for the eMBB
($\beta{=}10^{-1}$) and URLLC ($\beta{=}10^{-3}$) consumers---the consumer-relativity we
measure.

\textbf{URLLC-aware policy.} It raises $\Delta$ (a conservative MCS) \emph{and} combines
$K{=}3$ diversity branches: a backoff alone cannot reach $10^{-3}$, since a single
Rayleigh branch has a $\sim$$4\%$ deep-fade outage floor regardless of $\Delta$. The
branches $\{\gamma_k(t)\}$ are $K$ \emph{independent} TDL-A realizations (distinct seeds;
frequency/spatial replicas, not retransmissions), and a block fails only if all fail,
$\mathrm{FC}_{\text{aware}}=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t\prod_{k=1}^{K}e_{m^\star}(\gamma_k(t))$.
This is a \emph{best case}: independent branches lower-bound the joint outage, so
correlated diversity raises $\mathrm{FC}_{\text{aware}}$ and the true cost of URLLC
safety is at least what we report.

\textbf{OLLA.} The witnessed correction (\S\ref{sec:refresh}) distrusts $\hat{g}$ and
selects $m^\star(\hat{g}(t)-o(t))$, updating the offset asymmetrically from HARQ feedback,
\begin{equation}
o(t{+}1)=o(t)+\delta_\uparrow\mathbf{1}[\text{ACK}(t)]
-\delta_\downarrow\mathbf{1}[\text{NACK}(t)],
\label{eq:olla}
\end{equation}
with up-step $\delta_\uparrow=0.1$\,dB and $\delta_\downarrow=\delta_\uparrow(1-\beta)/\beta$,
so the equilibrium NACK rate is $\beta$. For held CSI the \emph{refresh floor} is
$P^\star(f_D)=\max\{P:\widehat{\mathrm{FC}}\le\beta\}$.

\textbf{Discipline.} Each experiment is pre-registered with numerical bars and
manipulation checks fixed before the graded run; the sealed record is content-hashed and
graded on seeds disjoint from those used to design it, and negative or failing outcomes
are kept~\cite{otcampaigns}. The numbers below are the graded, sealed values
(Table~\ref{tab:results}).

\section{Consumer-Relativity in the Reliability Target}
\label{sec:urllc}
A base station serving mixed traffic derives one CSI estimate per user and selects an
MCS. Consider the same estimate consumed under two targets: eMBB
($\mathrm{BLER}\le 10^{-1}$) and URLLC ($\le 10^{-3}$). We drive a real NR link at high
mobility, apply the naive CSI-derived MCS, and measure the achieved BLER against each
target; we then compare a \emph{target-aware} policy that sizes its margin to the
consumer's target.

Figure~\ref{fig:urllc} reports the sealed result (three graded seeds, $T{=}6000$ TTI,
fresh CSI with $\sigma{=}1$\,dB report noise; expected-rate estimator). The naive
certificate achieves $\mathrm{BLER}\approx 0.112$. Read by the eMBB consumer this is at
target---the certificate is sound. Read by the URLLC consumer the \emph{same} $0.112$ is
a false-clear by $112\times$ (against $10^{-3}$): every such block is a reliability-SLA
violation, invisible to a scheduler that assumed one CSI report serves all traffic. The
target-aware policy---the same CSI, a conservative MCS backoff combined with the $K{=}3$
independent diversity branches of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} (a backoff alone cannot reach the
$10^{-3}$ tail on one branch)---achieves $\mathrm{BLER}\approx 1.2\times10^{-5}$,
comfortably within target, at the cost of the backoff's spectral efficiency and the
diversity's resource overhead. No single certificate is \emph{efficient} for both: the
one that is sound for eMBB false-clears for URLLC by $112\times$, and the one that is
sound for URLLC over-provisions eMBB. The false-clear rate is the number that prices the
difference.

\section{The Refresh Horizon}
\label{sec:refresh}
The second axis is time. A CSI report is fresh when issued and ages as the channel
decorrelates; a certificate that was sound at report time false-clears once the channel
has moved. Here we use the empirical HARQ NACK rate~\eqref{eq:fc_emp}. At $f_D = 200$\,Hz
($v\!\approx\!62$\,km/h at $3.5$\,GHz) with a $20$-TTI report period, the aged estimate
has a mean absolute error $\tfrac1T\sum_t|\gamma(t)-\hat{g}(t)|\approx 5.7$\,dB
(aging-dominated; report noise contributes $\sim$1\,dB), and the naive (held-CSI)
certificate reaches $\mathrm{BLER}\approx 0.36$ against a $0.1$ target---a false-clear on
roughly a quarter of transmissions---while OLLA~\eqref{eq:olla}, the deployed
HARQ-witnessed correction, holds it at $\approx 0.10$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:refresh}(a) shows
the aging across mobility).

The operational question is how often to refresh. We sweep Doppler
$f_D\in\{10,25,50,100,200,400\}$\,Hz ($T_{\mathrm{coh}}$ from $42$ down to $1$\,ms, an
order of magnitude) and report period $P\in\{1,\dots,80\}$ TTI, define the \emph{refresh
floor} $P^\star(f_D)$ as the largest $P$ keeping BLER at target, and fit it by
least-squares \emph{through the origin} (linear, not log-log) against the Clarke
coherence time $T_{\mathrm{coh}}=0.423/f_D$:
\begin{equation}
P^\star \;\approx\; 0.177\,T_{\mathrm{coh}}, \qquad R^2 = 0.915
\label{eq:ot14}
\end{equation}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:refresh}(b)). The fit is dominated by the low-mobility points; at high
Doppler the floor \emph{saturates} at the minimum report period ($1$ TTI, the dotted
line in Fig.~\ref{fig:refresh}(b)). This saturation is operationally decisive: above a
mobility threshold ($f_D\gtrsim 200$\,Hz here) \emph{no} reporting cadence the frame
permits keeps the naive certificate at target, so a HARQ-witnessed correction (OLLA)
stops being optional and becomes mandatory. Below it,~\eqref{eq:ot14} is a concrete
provisioning rule: re-report before the channel decorrelates past
$\approx 0.18\,T_{\mathrm{coh}}$, not on a fixed calendar.

\section{Related Work}
Link adaptation, CSI aging, and outer-loop correction are mature. OLLA~\cite{olla}
adjusts an SINR offset from HARQ ACK/NACK to hold a BLER target under imperfect or stale
CSI; it is precisely the witnessed correction our refresh-horizon result quantifies.
Short-packet and URLLC analysis~\cite{urllc} establishes the reliability-target regime in
which the eMBB-to-URLLC gap of \S\ref{sec:urllc} lives. Current practice configures CQI
reporting on a fixed periodicity~\cite{cqireport}; our refresh floor recasts that period
as a coherence-time budget ($\approx 0.18\,T_{\mathrm{coh}}$)---a mobility-adaptive rule
rather than a fixed calendar. Learned CSI feedback trained on reconstruction
error~\cite{csinet} is an active direction; we note only that the reconstruction-versus-
consumer objective gap it might imply did not appear at practical operating points in our
controlled tests, and we make no claim on it here. Our contribution is orthogonal: not a
better estimator, scheduler, or offset rule, but a \emph{measurement}---the false-clear
rate as a first-class, consumer-relative KPI---and the two relativities and the refresh
law it exposes.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The unit of correctness in link adaptation is not the CSI report but the pair (report,
consumer). Sizing margin and refresh cadence for the consumer's reliability target and
coherence time, rather than for a nominal channel, is what keeps a certificate honest.
The false-clear rate makes the cost of ignoring the consumer measurable: $112\times$ for a
mismatched target, a coherence-scaled horizon for a stale report. The Sionna link-level
chain is the evidence rung; an over-the-air or srsRAN testbed with real HARQ traces is the
external-validity graduation.

\textbf{Scope.} The evidence is one TDL-A profile (DS $30$\,ns), one mean SNR ($12$\,dB),
an MCS set to 64-QAM, and---for the target axis---one mobility; the refresh law is fit
across $f_D=10$--$400$\,Hz. We expect both relativities to persist and \emph{sharpen}
elsewhere: at cell edge the naive certificate false-clears even for eMBB, so the target
gap only widens; 256-QAM adds steeper cliffs that deepen the aging penalty; and richer
profiles or correlated diversity raise $\mathrm{FC}_{\text{aware}}$. Each omitted factor
increases, not removes, the measured cost. The methodology is not radio-specific---the
same consumer-relative false-clear appears in optical quality-of-transmission and
database replication~\cite{otcampaigns}---but the results here stand on the 5G~NR CSI
certificate alone.
