\begin{abstract}
A channel state report drives one rate decision, and different consumers hold
that decision to different error budgets. We measure what that costs on an NR
LDPC-based link-to-system simulation, in three ways. First, overrun. A selection
policy whose achieved block error rate sits near a broadband budget of one block
in ten overruns a low-latency budget of one in a thousand by a factor of 112 on
the same channel, and the overrun holds at every mean SNR from cell edge to cell
centre. A budget-aware policy removes it at a stated cost in margin and
diversity. Second, age. With the link calibrated so that a fresh report actually
meets the broadband budget, we ask how long a report may be held before the
budget is lost. The answer is four transmission intervals at walking pace and a
single interval at moderate mobility and above. Periodic reporting alone then
meets the budget only at the frame minimum, and correction driven by
acknowledgements becomes necessary rather than optional. Third, reach. Four
further NR reports fail the same way against their own witnesses, the millimetre
wave beam index, the precoder, the rank, and the uplink timing advance. A held
periodic report fails at roughly three to four times the target on all four. The
deployed re-reporting loops bring three of them to the target and leave the
precoder above it. The results come from sealed, pre-registered experiments,
and a superseded relaxed-threshold pilot is kept and disclosed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
CSI feedback, link adaptation, URLLC, outer-loop link adaptation, reliability, 5G NR.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Adaptive modulation and coding turns a channel state report into a rate decision.
The transmitter selects the highest modulation and coding scheme whose required
signal-to-interference-and-noise ratio the report clears, and the selection is
tuned to an error budget. The trouble is that the traffic behind one report does
not share one budget. A broadband flow tolerates one block error in ten. A
low-latency flow tolerates one in a thousand or less. The same decision, from the
same report, on the same channel, is judged against both.

This paper measures that mismatch with two quantities. The first is the achieved
block error rate of a selection policy. The second is the budget overrun, which
is the achieved rate divided by the consumer's budget. The overrun is where
consumer relativity lives. The achieved rate is a property of the policy and the
channel, while the overrun depends on who is asking. Certificates that do not
set a rate need one more quantity, introduced with them in \S\ref{sec:families}.

Three results follow. The first (\S\ref{sec:budget}) is partly mechanical and we
say so. A policy run near one error in ten must overrun a budget of one in a
thousand by about a hundredfold. The measured content is the exact overrun of
112, the fact that it holds across the whole SNR range including the cell edge,
and the stated cost of the budget-aware policy that removes it. The second
(\S\ref{sec:age}) is the paper's core. With the link calibrated so that a fresh
report actually meets the broadband budget, the age horizon, meaning the longest
a report can be held before the budget is lost, collapses to four transmission
intervals at walking pace and to a single interval above a bicycle's speed. An
earlier exploration at a relaxed threshold suggested that this horizon scales with
the coherence time. The calibrated recomputation shows that at the budget itself
there is almost no horizon left to scale. The third (\S\ref{sec:families}) tests
whether any of this is particular to rate selection. It is not. Four further NR
reports, the beam index at millimetre wave, the precoder, the rank, and the
uplink timing advance, each fail against their own witness at roughly three to
four times the target when held over one report period. The corrections are old, outer
loop link adaptation and beam failure recovery above all, and our measurement says
when they have stopped being optional.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Substrate.} We use an NR LDPC-based link-to-system simulation. For each
MCS of the NR table (TS~38.214 Table~5.1.3.1-1, QPSK to 64-QAM, code rates
$\ge 1/5$) we measure a block-error curve $e_m(\gamma)$ with the Sionna 5G LDPC
decoder \cite{sionna} over AWGN, 400 decoded blocks per point on a $-8$ to $28$\,dB grid at
$2$\,dB spacing, linearly interpolated between points. The time-domain link draws
a per-TTI SNR trace from a 3GPP TR38.901 TDL-A channel ($3.5$\,GHz, $30$\,ns
delay spread) and resolves each transmission as
$\mathrm{NACK}(t)\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(e_{m}(\gamma(t)))$. Two accuracy limits
follow and bound our claims. At 400 blocks per point, a measured zero errors
still leaves a binomial $95\%$ upper bound near $7.5\times10^{-3}$, so the
curves are trustworthy at the $10^{-2}$ level and above, and any smaller value
is an interpolation rather than a count. The linear interpolation is a disclosed
simplification that is coarse on steep tails. And the SNR-to-error mapping
ignores fading within a TTI. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters.

\textbf{System model.} Let $\gamma(t)$ be the true SNR in dB at TTI $t$ and
$\hat{g}(t)$ the estimate the transmitter holds. Fresh CSI reads
$\hat{g}(t)=\gamma(t)+n(t)$ with $n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$, $\sigma=1$\,dB.
A report held with period $P$ reads
$\hat{g}(t)=\gamma(t_k)+n(t_k)$ with $t_k=P\lfloor t/P\rfloor$, so the estimate
carries the report noise of its issue time and then ages. The certificate selects,
at reference target $\beta_0=10^{-1}$ with backoff $\Delta$,
\begin{equation}
m^\star(\hat{g})=\max\{m:\rho_m(\beta_0)+\Delta\le\hat{g}\},
\label{eq:cert}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_m(\beta)=\min\{\gamma:e_m(\gamma)\le\beta\}$. The achieved BLER is
estimated two ways. The age study uses the empirical NACK rate
\begin{equation}
\widehat{B}=\tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{NACK}(t)]
\label{eq:bler}
\end{equation}
and the budget study uses the expected rate
$\widehat{B}=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t e_{m^\star}(\gamma(t))$, because a scale of one
error in a thousand cannot be resolved by counting at $T{=}6000$. The
consumer-relative observable is the \emph{budget overrun}
$\Omega(\beta)=\widehat{B}/\beta$. $\widehat{B}$ itself does not depend on
$\beta$, which is why the eMBB and URLLC-naive columns of
Table~\ref{tab:results}(a) coincide by construction. The two consumers differ in
$\Omega$, where the same $\widehat{B}\approx 0.112$ reads $\Omega\approx 1.1$ for
eMBB and $\Omega\approx 112$ for URLLC.

\textbf{Budget-aware policy.} It raises the backoff to $\Delta=4$\,dB and
combines $K{=}3$ diversity branches, independent TDL-A realizations from distinct
seeds standing in for frequency or spatial replicas, and a block fails only if
all branches fail. A backoff alone cannot reach $10^{-3}$, because a single Rayleigh
branch spends about $6\%$ of its time in fades where even the lowest MCS misses
the $10^{-3}$ budget. Independence
makes the diversity a best case, and the claim rests on a product rather than a
directly counted deep tail. The per-branch failure rates sit at the percent
level, where the curves are reliable, and their independence product lies below
the $10^{-3}$ budget by a factor of about fifty. The absolute product value
($\approx 10^{-5}$) inherits the interpolation's error and should be read as an
order of magnitude.

\textbf{OLLA.} The correction of \S\ref{sec:age} selects
$m^\star(\hat{g}(t)+o(t))$ and updates the offset on HARQ feedback,
\begin{equation}
o(t{+}1)=o(t)+\delta_{\mathrm{up}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{ACK}(t)]
-\delta_{\mathrm{dn}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{NACK}(t)],
\label{eq:olla}
\end{equation}
with $\delta_{\mathrm{up}}=0.1$\,dB and
$\delta_{\mathrm{dn}}=\delta_{\mathrm{up}}(1-\beta)/\beta$, so ACKs push the
selection up and the loop settles where the NACK rate equals $\beta$. This
recurrence matches the verified implementation line for line.

\textbf{The age horizon.} Because the naive fresh policy lands near but above the
budget ($\widehat{B}\approx 0.11$), the age study first calibrates. A fixed
backoff $\Delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$, chosen in $0.25$\,dB steps on a disclosed
calibration seed, brings the fresh policy to $\widehat{B}\le 0.09$ at every
Doppler. All policies then carry $\Delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$. The age horizon is
$P^\star(f_D)=\max\{P:\widehat{B}(P)\le 0.10\}$. A Doppler at which no period
qualifies is recorded as censored and never clamped.

\textbf{Four more certificates.} Sections \ref{sec:budget} and \ref{sec:age}
study one report, the channel quality estimate that sets a rate. Four further NR
reports carry the same structure, and each has an independent witness of whether
the served transmission worked. The beam index reported at millimetre wave is
witnessed by HARQ. The precoder and the rank are witnessed by the decode of the
layers they select. The uplink timing advance is witnessed by the uplink decode,
which fails once the residual delay exceeds the cyclic prefix. For these the
observable is the rate $F$ at which a held report is paired with a failed
transmission, against a target of $0.10$ throughout. Three policies run over one
aging track per cell. The naive policy holds the periodic report. The witnessed
policy re-reports after two consecutive failures, the deployed loop in each case,
which is beam failure recovery for the beam, re-selection for the precoder,
reduction for the rank, and the timing advance command. The fresh policy
re-reports every slot and carries no aging. The beam cell uses an exact
sixteen-element half-wavelength array factor over a twenty-four-beam codebook
spanning $\pm 60$ degrees, with the dominant direction drifting at $300$ degrees
per second and a one-degree measurement error. The precoder, rank and timing
cells age parametrically, with an angular drift of $1200$ degrees per second at
$0.3$\,dB of gain loss per degree, a rank changing $270$ times per second over
four ranks, and a delay drifting $20\,\mu$s per second against a $4.7\,\mu$s
cyclic prefix. All four decode on the measured Sionna LDPC curves at a fixed
modulation and coding scheme, set two decibels below the aligned operating point
so that the certificate under test and not rate selection drives the failures.
Only the decode is measured in these four cells, a weaker substrate than
\S\ref{sec:age} uses, and \S\ref{sec:families} states what that limits.

\textbf{Discipline.} Each experiment is pre-registered with numerical bars fixed
before the graded run. Records are content-hashed and graded on seeds disjoint
from design, and failed, voided and superseded outcomes stay in the record.
Review of an earlier draft found that the age sweep had used a relaxed $0.15$
threshold while the text claimed $0.10$. That exploration is kept, and
\S\ref{sec:age} reports the calibrated recomputation.

\section{The Reliability Budget}
\label{sec:budget}
A base station serving mixed traffic derives one CSI estimate per user and picks
an MCS from it. Consider the same estimate judged under two budgets, $10^{-1}$
for eMBB and $10^{-3}$ for URLLC. Figure~\ref{fig:urllc} shows the sealed result,
three graded seeds of $6000$ TTI each with fresh CSI. The naive policy achieves
$\widehat{B}\approx 0.112$, near its eMBB budget. The URLLC consumer reads the
same $0.112$ as an overrun of $112\times$. Part of this is arithmetic, and we do
not claim otherwise. A policy run near one budget must overrun a budget a
hundred times stricter by about that factor. What the measurement adds is the
exact figure, the fact that nothing in the scheduler's view flags it, and a
feasible policy that removes it. The budget-aware policy of \S\ref{sec:method}
meets the $10^{-3}$ budget with margin. Its protection is stated, a $4$\,dB
backoff and threefold diversity, though we do not quantify the resulting
spectral-efficiency loss here.

The overrun is not an artifact of the operating point. A separately sealed cell
sweeps the mean SNR from $3$ to $21$\,dB (Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}). The certificate
rides the eMBB budget, so the URLLC overrun stays between $77\times$ and
$142\times$ at every in-range SNR and is still $54\times$ where the MCS table
saturates. At $6$\,dB and below even the eMBB consumer loses its budget, since the
lowest MCS is already too fast for the channel
($\widehat{B}\approx 0.20$ at $6$\,dB and $0.33$ at $3$\,dB). No margin repairs a certificate whose
weakest format is too aggressive.

\section{The Age Horizon}
\label{sec:age}
The second axis is time, and it is where the budget question bites hardest. A
report is fresh when issued and ages as the channel decorrelates. At $200$\,Hz
Doppler with a $20$-TTI report period, the held estimate is off by $5.7$\,dB on
average (mean absolute error, of which aging dominates and report noise
contributes about $1$\,dB), and the naive certificate runs at
$\widehat{B}\approx 0.36$ against the $0.10$ budget, an overrun of $3.6\times$
from age alone. OLLA, Eq.~\eqref{eq:olla}, corrects the same held report and
holds $0.10$ over three sealed seeds, $0.34$ to $0.37$ naive against $0.101$ to
$0.102$ corrected, with the per-seed record in the public ledger
\cite{otcampaigns}.

How long may a report be held before the budget is lost? An exploratory sweep
at a relaxed $0.15$ threshold suggested a coherence-proportional law,
$P^\star\approx 0.18\,T_{\mathrm{coh}}$. It was superseded by the calibrated
$0.10$ experiment reported here, and both records are kept. The calibrated
recomputation at the true $0.10$ budget gives a starker
answer (Fig.~\ref{fig:age}, Table~\ref{tab:results}(b)). With the fresh
baseline calibrated to
$\widehat{B}\le 0.09$ at every Doppler (a $0.5$\,dB backoff suffices), the
horizon over the three graded seeds is $P^\star=4$ TTI at $10$\,Hz ($6$ on one
seed), $2$ at $25$\,Hz ($3$ on one seed), and $1$ TTI, the frame minimum, at
$50$\,Hz and above, with no censored points.
There is no room for a proportionality law. Once the budget is tight, the first
few milliseconds of aging consume it, and almost no horizon is left to scale. At $3.5$\,GHz these Dopplers are modest speeds. Ten hertz is about
$3$\,km/h, a walk, and $50$\,Hz about $15$\,km/h, a bicycle. The operational
statement follows. Below walking pace a report survives a few TTIs. At moderate
mobility and above, periodic reporting meets the budget only at the
frame-minimum interval, so HARQ-driven correction is necessary for any less
aggressive reporting cadence. The coherence-proportional behaviour is real but
belongs to relaxed thresholds, where degradation has room to accumulate before
the budget is lost.

\section{Four More Certificates Fail the Same Way}
\label{sec:families}
The results so far concern one report. If the budget question belonged only to
rate selection it would be a narrow finding. It does not. Figure~\ref{fig:fam}
and Table~\ref{tab:fam} give four further certificates, three graded seeds each,
every bar fixed before the graded run.

Held over one report period, each fails against its own witness well above the
$0.10$ target. The beam index reaches $0.310$ to $0.329$, the precoder $0.299$ to
$0.311$, the rank $0.268$ to $0.299$, and the uplink timing advance $0.419$ to
$0.425$. Averaged over seeds the four rates span $2.8$ to $4.2$ times the target.
The drift behind them is large in each case. Mean beamforming loss runs $4.67$ to
$4.79$\,dB for the beam and $3.09$ to $3.11$\,dB of precoding gain for the
precoder, and the timing advance leaves a mean residual of $0.42$ cyclic
prefixes. For the rank the drift measure and the failure rate coincide by
construction, because a stale high rank fails exactly when the current channel
cannot carry the layer, so that row reports one number rather than two.

The deployed re-reporting loops repair three of the four. Rank reduction reaches
$0.079$ to $0.085$ and the timing advance command reaches $0.055$ to $0.056$,
both under the target. Beam failure recovery lands on it at $0.098$ to $0.104$.
Precoder re-selection does not, holding $0.124$ to $0.130$, which is $24$ to
$30$ percent above the budget it exists to protect. Waiting for two consecutive
failures is a slow trigger against a precoder that drifts continuously, and the
cell re-reports 549 to 558 times in 6000 slots without catching up. This is the
one place in this paper where a deployed correction, run as specified, does not
reach the target.

With the report refreshed every slot the beam cell runs at $0.021$ to $0.027$,
and the other three record no failure at all in 6000 slots. Three seeds of
6000 slots with zero events bound the fresh rate below $5\times10^{-4}$ at
$95\%$ confidence rather than establishing zero. Aging causes these failures and
the model is not broken.

One of the four is an uplink certificate. The timing advance sets when the UE
transmits, and a stale value is paid for by the base station's decoder rather
than the UE's. The same holding failure appears on both links, so it is not a
property of downlink rate adaptation.

The scope is narrower here than in \S\ref{sec:age}. These four cells age
parametrically and measure only the decode, so they establish that the failure
structure carries across certificates. They do not fix the rates a deployed
network would see.

\section{Related Work}
Link adaptation, CSI aging and outer-loop correction are old subjects. OLLA
\cite{olla} holds an error target under imperfect or stale CSI by adjusting an
SINR offset on HARQ feedback, and it is exactly the correction our age result
makes mandatory. Short-packet analysis \cite{urllc} establishes the reliability
setting in which these budgets are written. NR configures CSI reporting as
periodic, semi-persistent, or DCI-triggered aperiodic \cite{cqireport}. None of
these adapt the cadence to the consumer's budget, and our horizon result says how
little cadence alone can do at a tight one. Event-based sampling such as
send-on-delta \cite{sod} is a natural extension we tested only in exploratory
form (a sealed confirmation attempt was voided by a control check and we claim
nothing).
Learned CSI feedback trained on reconstruction error \cite{csinet} is active.
In our controlled tests the difference in objective it might suggest did not
appear at practical operating points. The four certificates of
\S\ref{sec:families} all have standardised correction loops of their own. Beam
management at millimetre wave, including beam failure detection and recovery, is
surveyed in \cite{beamtut} and specified in \cite{ts38213}, which also carries
the uplink timing advance command. Precoder and rank reporting and their
adaptation are specified alongside CSI reporting in \cite{cqireport}. We credit
all of these as the deployed repairs and measure how far each one gets. We offer
no new estimator, scheduler or offset rule. We offer the measurement, which is
achieved error against the consumer's budget, the age horizon at the budget
itself, and the same failure in four more certificates.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The unit of correctness in link adaptation is not the report. It is the pair of
report and budget. Sized to the consumer's budget, margins and reporting cadence
hold the certificate to its target. Sized to a nominal channel they do not, and
the costs are now numbers, a $112\times$ overrun for a mismatched budget, an age
horizon of a few milliseconds at a tight one, and three to four times the target
on four further certificates whenever a periodic report is held. The evidence has
limits worth stating. It is an NR LDPC-based link-to-system simulation, one TDL-A
profile, and one MCS table topping at 64-QAM, and values below one error in a
thousand are limited by the resolution of the decoder curves. The four
certificates of \S\ref{sec:families} age parametrically and measure only the
decode. Over-the-air HARQ traces are the natural next step for all of it. In
companion sealed experiments, two margin policies with equal fleet budgets
received opposite verdicts from two user fleets whose failures ride different
axes of the channel (cells XPROTO-CSI-FLIP and XPROTO-QOT-FLIP in the sealed
repository \cite{otcampaigns}). A full treatment of that inversion, with
scheduler interpretations and goodput, is reported separately. The same
budget-relative failure appears in optical quality-of-transmission and in
database replication \cite{otcampaigns}, so the method is not specific to radio.
The results here stand on the 5G NR certificates alone.
