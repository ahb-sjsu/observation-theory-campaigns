\begin{abstract}
A CSI report drives one rate decision, but different consumers hold that decision
to different error budgets. We measure the resulting mismatch on an NR LDPC-based
link-level simulation, in two ways. First, budget overrun. A selection policy
whose achieved BLER sits near an eMBB budget of $10^{-1}$ overruns a URLLC budget
of $10^{-3}$ on the same channel by a factor of 112, and the overrun persists at
every SNR from cell edge to cell centre; a budget-aware policy removes it with
explicitly specified protection. Second, age. With the link calibrated to
actually meet the $10^{-1}$ budget when CSI is fresh, we ask how long a report
may be held before the budget is lost. The answer is a handful of milliseconds
at walking speeds and a single transmission interval at moderate mobility and
above,
so there periodic reporting meets the budget only at the frame-minimum interval,
and HARQ-driven correction is necessary for any less aggressive reporting
cadence. Every number comes from a sealed, pre-registered record, including a
superseded relaxed-threshold pilot that this version recomputes at the
calibrated target.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
CSI feedback, link adaptation, URLLC, outer-loop link adaptation, reliability, 5G NR.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Adaptive modulation and coding turns a CSI report into a rate decision. The
transmitter selects the highest MCS whose required SINR the report clears, and the
selection is tuned to an error budget. The trouble is that the traffic behind one
report does not share one budget. An eMBB flow tolerates a block error rate of
$10^{-1}$; a URLLC flow tolerates $10^{-3}$ or less. The same decision, from the
same report, on the same channel, is judged against both.

This paper measures that mismatch with two quantities that stay fixed throughout.
The first is the achieved BLER $\widehat{B}$ of a selection policy. The second is
the budget overrun $\Omega(\beta)=\widehat{B}/\beta$, the achieved BLER in units
of the consumer's budget. The overrun is where the consumer-relativity lives:
$\widehat{B}$ is a property of the policy and the channel, while $\Omega$ depends
on who is asking.

Two results follow. The first (\S\ref{sec:budget}) is partly mechanical and we
say so: a policy run near $10^{-1}$ must overrun $10^{-3}$ by about a hundredfold.
The measured content is the exact overrun (112), its persistence across the whole
SNR range including the cell edge, and the quantified cost of the budget-aware
policy that removes it. The second (\S\ref{sec:age}) is the paper's core. With
the link calibrated so that fresh CSI genuinely meets the $10^{-1}$ budget, the
age horizon, the longest a report can be held before the budget is lost, collapses
to a few transmission intervals at $10$\,Hz Doppler and to a single interval above
$50$\,Hz.
An earlier exploration at a relaxed threshold suggested a coherence-proportional
law; the calibrated recomputation shows that at the budget itself there is almost
no horizon to scale. The corrections are old, outer-loop link adaptation above
all, and our measurement says when they have stopped being optional.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Substrate.} We use an NR LDPC-based link-to-system simulation. For each
MCS of the NR table (TS~38.214 Table~5.1.3.1-1; QPSK--64-QAM, code rates
$\ge 1/5$) we measure a block-error curve $e_m(\gamma)$ with the Sionna 5G LDPC
decoder \cite{sionna} over AWGN, 400 decoded blocks per point on a $-8$ to $28$\,dB grid at
$2$\,dB spacing, linearly interpolated between points. The time-domain link draws
a per-TTI SNR trace from a 3GPP TR38.901 TDL-A channel ($3.5$\,GHz, $30$\,ns
delay spread) and resolves each transmission as
$\mathrm{NACK}(t)\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(e_{m}(\gamma(t)))$. Two accuracy limits
follow and bound our claims. At 400 blocks per point, a measured zero errors
still leaves a binomial $95\%$ upper bound near $7.5\times10^{-3}$, so the
curves are trustworthy at the $10^{-2}$ level and above, and any smaller value
is an interpolation, not a count; the linear interpolation itself is a disclosed
simplification that is coarse on steep tails. And the SNR-to-error mapping
ignores fading within a TTI. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters.

\textbf{System model.} Let $\gamma(t)$ be the true SNR in dB at TTI $t$ and
$\hat{g}(t)$ the estimate the transmitter holds. Fresh CSI reads
$\hat{g}(t)=\gamma(t)+n(t)$ with $n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$, $\sigma=1$\,dB.
A report held with period $P$ reads
$\hat{g}(t)=\gamma(t_k)+n(t_k)$ with $t_k=P\lfloor t/P\rfloor$: the estimate
carries the report noise of its issue time and then ages. The certificate selects,
at reference target $\beta_0=10^{-1}$ with backoff $\Delta$,
\begin{equation}
m^\star(\hat{g})=\max\{m:\rho_m(\beta_0)+\Delta\le\hat{g}\},
\label{eq:cert}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_m(\beta)=\min\{\gamma:e_m(\gamma)\le\beta\}$. The achieved BLER is
estimated two ways: the empirical NACK rate
\begin{equation}
\widehat{B}=\tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{NACK}(t)]
\label{eq:bler}
\end{equation}
for the age study, and the expected rate
$\widehat{B}=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t e_{m^\star}(\gamma(t))$ for the budget study,
whose $10^{-3}$ scale $T{=}6000$ cannot resolve by counting. The
consumer-relative observable is the \emph{budget overrun}
$\Omega(\beta)=\widehat{B}/\beta$. $\widehat{B}$ itself does not depend on
$\beta$, which is why the eMBB and URLLC-naive columns of
Table~\ref{tab:results}(a) coincide by construction; the two consumers differ in
$\Omega$: the same $\widehat{B}\approx 0.112$ is $\Omega\approx 1.1$ for eMBB and
$\Omega\approx 112$ for URLLC.

\textbf{Budget-aware policy.} It raises the backoff to $\Delta=4$\,dB and
combines $K{=}3$ diversity branches, independent TDL-A realizations from distinct
seeds standing in for frequency or spatial replicas: a block fails only if all
branches fail. A backoff alone cannot reach $10^{-3}$, because a single Rayleigh
branch spends about $4\%$ of its time in fades too deep for any MCS. Independence
makes the diversity a best case, and the claim rests on a product, not a
directly counted deep tail: the per-branch failure rates sit at the percent
level, where the curves are reliable, and their independence product lies below
the $10^{-3}$ budget with two orders of margin. The absolute product value
($\approx 10^{-5}$) inherits the interpolation's error and should be read as an
order of magnitude.

\textbf{OLLA.} The correction of \S\ref{sec:age} selects
$m^\star(\hat{g}(t)+o(t))$ and updates the offset on HARQ feedback,
\begin{equation}
o(t{+}1)=o(t)+\delta_{\mathrm{up}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{ACK}(t)]
-\delta_{\mathrm{dn}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{NACK}(t)],
\label{eq:olla}
\end{equation}
with $\delta_{\mathrm{up}}=0.1$\,dB and
$\delta_{\mathrm{dn}}=\delta_{\mathrm{up}}(1-\beta)/\beta$, so ACKs push the
selection up and the loop settles where the NACK rate equals $\beta$. This
recurrence matches the verified implementation line for line.

\textbf{The age horizon.} Because the naive fresh policy lands near but above the
budget ($\widehat{B}\approx 0.113$), the age study first calibrates: a fixed
backoff $\Delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$, chosen in $0.25$\,dB steps on a disclosed
calibration seed, brings the fresh policy to $\widehat{B}\le 0.09$ at every
Doppler. All policies then carry $\Delta_{\mathrm{cal}}$. The age horizon is
$P^\star(f_D)=\max\{P:\widehat{B}(P)\le 0.10\}$; a Doppler at which no period
qualifies is recorded as censored, never clamped.

\textbf{Discipline.} Each experiment is pre-registered with numerical bars fixed
before the graded run; records are content-hashed and graded on seeds disjoint
from design; failed, voided and superseded outcomes stay in the record. Review of
an earlier draft found that the age sweep had used a relaxed $0.15$ threshold
while the text claimed $0.10$; that exploration is kept, and \S\ref{sec:age}
reports the calibrated recomputation.

\section{The Reliability Budget}
\label{sec:budget}
A base station serving mixed traffic derives one CSI estimate per user and picks
an MCS from it. Consider the same estimate judged under two budgets, $10^{-1}$
for eMBB and $10^{-3}$ for URLLC. Figure~\ref{fig:urllc} shows the sealed result,
three graded seeds of $6000$ TTI each with fresh CSI. The naive policy achieves
$\widehat{B}\approx 0.112$, near its eMBB budget. The URLLC consumer reads the
same $0.112$ as an overrun of $112\times$. Part of this is arithmetic, and we do
not claim otherwise: a policy run near one budget must overrun a budget a
hundred times stricter by about that factor. What the measurement adds is the
exact figure, the fact that nothing in the scheduler's view flags it, and a
feasible policy that removes it: the budget-aware policy of \S\ref{sec:method}
meets the $10^{-3}$ budget with margin. Its protection is explicitly specified,
a $4$\,dB backoff and threefold diversity, though we do not quantify the
resulting spectral-efficiency loss here.

The overrun is not an artifact of the operating point. A separately sealed cell
sweeps the mean SNR from $3$ to $21$\,dB (Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}). The certificate
rides the eMBB budget, so the URLLC overrun stays between $77\times$ and
$142\times$ at every in-range SNR and is still $54\times$ where the MCS table
saturates. Below about $6$\,dB even the eMBB consumer loses its budget, since the
lowest MCS is already too fast for the channel
($\widehat{B}\approx 0.33$ at $3$\,dB). No margin repairs a certificate whose
weakest format is too aggressive.

\section{The Age Horizon}
\label{sec:age}
The second axis is time, and it is where the budget question bites hardest. A
report is fresh when issued and ages as the channel decorrelates. At $200$\,Hz
Doppler with a $20$-TTI report period, the held estimate is off by $5.7$\,dB on
average (mean absolute error; aging dominates, report noise contributes about
$1$\,dB), and the naive certificate runs at $\widehat{B}\approx 0.36$ against the
$0.10$ budget, an overrun of $3.6\times$ from age alone. OLLA,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:olla}, corrects the same held report and holds $0.10$ (three
sealed seeds, $0.34$--$0.37$ naive against $0.101$--$0.102$ corrected; the
per-seed record is in the public ledger \cite{otcampaigns}).

How long may a report be held before the budget is lost? An exploratory sweep
at a relaxed $0.15$ threshold suggested a coherence-proportional law,
$P^\star\approx 0.18\,T_{\mathrm{coh}}$; it was superseded by the calibrated
$0.10$ experiment reported here, and both records are kept. The calibrated
recomputation at the true $0.10$ budget gives a starker
answer (Fig.~\ref{fig:age}, Table~\ref{tab:results}(b)). With the fresh
baseline calibrated to
$\widehat{B}\le 0.09$ at every Doppler (a $0.5$\,dB backoff suffices), the
horizon over the three graded seeds is $P^\star=4$ TTI at $10$\,Hz ($6$ on one
seed), $2$ at $25$\,Hz ($3$ on one seed), and $1$ TTI, the frame minimum, at
$50$\,Hz and above, with no censored points.
There is no room for a proportionality law because there is almost no horizon to
scale: once the budget is tight, the first few milliseconds of aging consume it.
At $3.5$\,GHz these Dopplers are modest speeds: $10$\,Hz is about $3$\,km/h, a
walk, and $50$\,Hz about $15$\,km/h, a bicycle. The operational statement
follows. Below walking pace a report survives a few TTIs; at moderate mobility
and above, periodic reporting meets the budget only at the frame-minimum
interval, so HARQ-driven correction is necessary for any less aggressive
reporting cadence. The coherence-proportional behaviour is real but belongs to
relaxed thresholds, where degradation has room to accumulate before the budget
is lost.

\section{Related Work}
Link adaptation, CSI aging and outer-loop correction are old subjects. OLLA
\cite{olla} holds an error target under imperfect or stale CSI by adjusting an
SINR offset on HARQ feedback; it is exactly the correction our age result makes
mandatory. Short-packet analysis \cite{urllc} establishes the reliability regime
where the budget gap lives. NR configures CSI reporting as periodic,
semi-persistent, or DCI-triggered aperiodic \cite{cqireport}; none of these adapt
the cadence to the consumer's budget, and our horizon result says how little
cadence alone can do at a tight one. Event-based sampling such as send-on-delta
\cite{sod} is a natural extension we tested only in exploratory form (a sealed
confirmation attempt was voided by a control check and we claim nothing).
Learned CSI feedback trained on reconstruction error \cite{csinet} is active;
in our controlled tests the objective gap it might suggest did not appear at
practical operating points. We offer no new estimator, scheduler or offset rule.
We offer the measurement: achieved BLER against the consumer's budget, and the
age horizon at the budget itself.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The unit of correctness in link adaptation is not the CSI report. It is the pair
of report and budget. Sized to the consumer's budget, margins and reporting
cadence keep the certificate honest; sized to a nominal channel, they do not,
and the costs are now numbers: a $112\times$ overrun for a mismatched budget,
and an age horizon of a few milliseconds at a tight one. The evidence has limits
worth stating. It is an NR LDPC-based link-to-system simulation, one TDL-A
profile, one MCS table topping at 64-QAM, and deep-tail values below $10^{-3}$
are curve-resolution-limited; over-the-air HARQ traces are the natural next
step. In companion sealed experiments, two margin policies with equal fleet
budgets received opposite verdicts from two user fleets whose failures ride
different axes of the channel (cells XPROTO-CSI-FLIP and XPROTO-QOT-FLIP in the
sealed repository \cite{otcampaigns}); a full treatment of that inversion, with
scheduler interpretations and goodput, is beyond this paper's scope and is
reported separately. The methodology is not radio-specific; the same
budget-relative failure appears in optical quality-of-transmission and in
database replication \cite{otcampaigns}. The results here stand on the 5G NR
certificate alone.
