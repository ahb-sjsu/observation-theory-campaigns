\section{Introduction}
Machine-learning quality-of-transmission (ML-QoT) estimation is a leading route to
reducing the system margin that optical operators carry against uncertain reach
performance~\cite{mlqot,pointon}. That margin is deliberately stranded capacity, so
every fraction of a dB matters. ML-QoT models are almost always trained to minimise a
prediction error, mean-squared or mean-absolute GSNR error. We argue, and then
measure, that this objective serves the wrong master. The consumer of a QoT estimate
is the format-selection decision, and behind it the FEC decoder, and the decoder reads
a threshold. A lightpath either meets its format's required GSNR or it does not. An
estimator can be excellent on average and still, near a format boundary, over-predict
by just enough to select a rate the link cannot support. The result is an in-service
outage even though the mean error was small. We call this a false-clear.

We measure the false-clear rate on the GN-model physical layer of GNPy~\cite{gnpy}
over the CORONET-CONUS reference network and show three things. An average-error
ML-QoT objective false-clears at the FEC cliff while a threshold-aware objective does
not, at equal capacity. Which lightpaths are at risk is set by spectral position and
reach, so no single blanket margin fits all of them. And the risk grows with the fill
added after provisioning, which gives the certificate a refresh horizon.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Substrate.} Lightpaths are transparent shortest-path routes of the
CORONET-CONUS reference topology (75 nodes; up to $2500$\,km, 2--31 spans of
80\,km). Per-channel GSNR comes from the GN model in GNPy~\cite{gnpy} on standard
single-mode fibre (0.2\,dB/km, $D=17$\,ps/nm/km) with 6\,dB-NF EDFAs, $+2$\,dBm per
channel, 32\,GBd on a 50\,GHz grid. The band runs from a lightly filled provisioning
state of 6 channels to a full C-band of 76.

\textbf{Certificate, consumer, witness.} A certificate selects the highest format
whose required GSNR the estimate clears. The consumer is that decision. The witness is
the true deployed GSNR, in practice the receiver's pre-FEC BER. For the ML study the
features are the provisioning-time GSNR plus spectral position, reach and fill, the
label carries an irreducible term for unmeasured network state, and two
gradient-boosted objectives compete: average-error (squared loss) and consumer-aware
(a conservative lower-quantile loss that protects the decision rather than the mean).
Bars and manipulation checks are pre-registered. The sealed record is content-hashed
and graded on seeds disjoint from those used during design~\cite{otcampaigns}.

\section{Results}
\subsection{ML-QoT trained on the wrong metric}
Under irreducible prediction uncertainty the average-error estimator wins the metric
it was trained on and loses the one that matters. Its MAE is $0.65$\,dB against
$0.95$\,dB for the consumer-aware objective, yet its false-clear rate is about
$0.041$ against $0.008$, five times higher, at comparable delivered spectral
efficiency near $3.9$\,bits/symbol. Figure~\ref{fig:ml}(a) shows the two objectives
trading places between the two metrics. Figure~\ref{fig:ml}(b) shows where the
failures live: the average-error false-clears concentrate where the GSNR headroom
above the nearest format threshold is small. That is the cliff, and it is exactly
where a symmetric error metric is blind. The fix is to train ML-QoT on the consumer's
threshold decision rather than on volume-averaged fidelity.

\subsection{The margin is consumer-relative}
The same threshold-blindness afflicts the margin itself as the band fills. A
lightpath's GSNR drops as neighbours arrive, and whether it crosses a format threshold
depends on where it sits: false-clears concentrate on services whose spectral position
and reach leave their GSNR near a boundary, and are absent elsewhere
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fc}). So a single blanket margin cannot be right for every lightpath.
We price this against today's practice of one uniform system margin sized to keep the
whole fleet safe. The deployed fill is real. Each lightpath's channel load is drawn
from the per-link fill distribution of the SNDlib janos-us-ca demand
matrix~\cite{sndlib} (61 links; median 10 channels, 90th percentile 35, of 76). Real
traffic is heavy-tailed. A uniform margin sized for the few near-full links
over-provisions the lightly filled majority. We give both policies the same
QoT-estimator uncertainty of $0.5$\,dB RMSE, so the footprint-aware certificate is
not assumed perfect. Sizing each margin to the lightpath's own fill then recovers
about $8\%$ of spectral efficiency, near $0.33$\,b/sym or $21$\,Gb/s per channel, at
matched safety (false-clear at most $0.01$) on a rate-flexible transceiver model. The
gain is modest and free. It is capacity the uniform margin strands on links that
never fill.

\subsection{The refresh floor}
The risk is driven by the fill added between provisioning and use, so a certificate
has a refresh horizon. Sweeping the deployed fill from 6 to 76 channels, the naive
false-clear rises roughly linearly with the number of added channels,
$\mathrm{FC}\approx 0.008\,\Delta N$ with $R^2=0.95$. At a $0.05$ target the floor is
about 8 added channels. The rule is concrete. Re-certify a service before the band
around it grows by about 8 channels, rather than on a fixed calendar.

\section{Related work}
QoT estimation and physical-layer-aware routing and spectrum assignment are
mature~\cite{gnpy,pointon}. ML-QoT is active, and its estimators are trained mostly on
prediction error~\cite{mlqot}. We do not offer a better estimator or allocator. We
offer the measurement, the false-clear rate read against the consumer, and the finding
that both the ML-QoT training objective and the margin allocation should target the
decoder's threshold.

\section{Discussion}
Train QoT models, and size QoT margin, for the decision the FEC decoder makes rather
than for average fidelity. The evidence stands on the GN model. A coherent-receiver
pre-FEC-BER witness on a fibre testbed, and the EGN or split-step model, are the
natural next steps. The method is not specific to optics. The same consumer-relative
false-clear appears in radio link adaptation and in database
replication~\cite{otcampaigns}. The results here stand on the optical QoT certificate
alone.
