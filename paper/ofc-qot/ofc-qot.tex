\title{Consumer-Aligned Learning for Quality-of-Transmission:
The False-Clear Rate at the FEC Cliff}

\author{Andrew H. Bond$^{*}$}
\address{Department of Computer Engineering, San Jos\'e State University, San Jos\'e, CA 95192, USA}
\email{$^{*}$andrew.bond@sjsu.edu}

\begin{abstract}
An ML-QoT estimator trained on average GSNR error is misaligned with the FEC
decoder's threshold: it false-clears at the cliff. On GNPy over CORONET-CONUS a
consumer-aware objective cuts false-clears $\sim$5$\times$ at equal capacity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Machine-learning Quality-of-Transmission (ML-QoT) estimation is a leading route to
reducing the system margin---the deliberately stranded capacity---that optical
operators carry against uncertain reach performance~\cite{mlqot,pointon}. ML-QoT
models are almost always trained to minimise a \emph{prediction error} (mean-squared
or mean-absolute GSNR/OSNR error). We argue, and measure, that this objective is
misaligned with the consumer it serves. The consumer of a QoT estimate is the
\emph{format-selection decision}, and ultimately the forward-error-correction (FEC)
decoder, which reads a \emph{threshold}: a lightpath either meets its format's
required GSNR or it does not. An estimator can be excellent on average yet, near a
format boundary, over-predict just enough to select a rate the link cannot support---
a \emph{false-clear} (an in-service outage), even though its mean error is small.

We make the false-clear rate a first-class, measured KPI and show three things on the
GN-model physical layer of GNPy~\cite{gnpy} over the CORONET-CONUS reference network:
(i) an average-error ML-QoT objective false-clears at the FEC cliff, while a
consumer-aware objective does not, at equal capacity; (ii) the underlying QoT margin
is \emph{consumer-relative}---which lightpaths are at risk is a footprint property
(spectral position and reach), so no blanket margin fits all; and (iii) a
fill-driven \emph{refresh floor} sets when a certificate must be re-validated.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Substrate.} Lightpaths are transparent shortest-path routes of the
\textbf{CORONET-CONUS} reference topology (75 nodes; $\le 2500$\,km, 2--31$\times$
80\,km spans). Per-channel GSNR is computed with the GN model in GNPy~\cite{gnpy} on
standard single-mode fibre (0.2\,dB/km, $D=17$\,ps/nm/km), EDFAs (NF 6\,dB),
+2\,dBm/ch, 32\,GBd, 50\,GHz grid, from a lightly filled ``provisioning'' band
(6 channels) to a full C-band (76 channels).

\textbf{Certificate, consumer, witness.} A certificate selects the highest format
whose required GSNR the estimate clears. The \emph{consumer} is that decision; the
\emph{witness} is the true deployed GSNR (the receiver's pre-FEC BER). For the ML
study, features are provisioning-time GSNR and (spectral position, reach, fill), the
label carries an irreducible unmeasured-state term, and two gradient-boosted
objectives are compared: \emph{average-error} (squared loss) and \emph{consumer-aware}
(a conservative lower-quantile loss that protects the decision, not the mean). Bars
and manipulation checks are pre-registered; the sealed record is content-hashed with
disjoint graded seeds~\cite{otcampaigns}.

\section{Results}
\subsection{ML-QoT trained on the wrong metric}
Under irreducible prediction uncertainty, the average-error estimator attains lower
mean error yet false-clears far more at the FEC cliff than the consumer-aware
objective: MAE $0.65$ vs $0.95$\,dB (it wins reconstruction) but false-clear
$\approx 0.041$ vs $\approx 0.008$ ($\sim$5$\times$) at comparable delivered spectral
efficiency ($\approx 3.9$\,bits/symbol). Figure~\ref{fig:ml}(a) shows the
reconstruction-vs-consumer dissociation; (b) shows that the average-error
false-clears concentrate where the GSNR headroom above the nearest format threshold
is small---the cliff---exactly where a symmetric error metric is blind. The
prescription is to train ML-QoT on the consumer's threshold decision, not on
volume-averaged fidelity.

\subsection{The margin is consumer-relative}
The same threshold-blindness afflicts the margin itself under incremental fill. As a
lightpath's band fills toward its deployed load, its GSNR drops, and whether it
crosses a format threshold depends on its \emph{footprint}: false-clears concentrate
on the services whose spectral position and reach place their GSNR nearest a boundary,
and are absent elsewhere (Fig.~\ref{fig:fc}). Consequently a single blanket margin
cannot be right for all. We quantify the cost against today's practice---one
\emph{uniform} system margin sized so the fleet stays safe---using \emph{real}
deployed fill: each lightpath's channel load is drawn from the per-link fill
distribution of the SNDlib \textbf{janos-us-ca} demand matrix (61 links; median 10,
90th-percentile 35 of 76 channels~\cite{sndlib}). Real traffic is heavy-tailed, so a
uniform margin sized for the few near-full links over-provisions the lightly-filled
majority. Giving \emph{both} policies the same QoT-estimator uncertainty (0.5\,dB
RMSE, so the footprint-aware certificate is not assumed perfect), a certificate that
sizes each margin to the lightpath's own fill recovers $\approx 8\%$ of spectral
efficiency ($\approx 0.33$\,b/sym, $\sim$21\,Gb/s per channel) at matched safety
(false-clear $\le 0.01$), on a rate-flexible transceiver model. The gain is modest
but free: it is capacity the uniform margin strands on the majority of links that
never fill.

\subsection{The refresh floor}
Because the risk is driven by the fill \emph{increment} between provisioning and use,
a certificate has a refresh horizon. Sweeping the deployed fill from 6 to 76
channels, the naive false-clear rises approximately linearly with the number of added
channels ($\mathrm{FC}\approx 0.008\,\Delta N$, $R^2 = 0.95$); at a 0.05 target the
refresh floor is $\approx 8$ added channels. The rule is concrete: re-certify a
service before the band around it grows by $\sim$8 channels, not on a fixed calendar.

\section{Related work}
QoT estimation and physical-layer-aware routing/spectrum assignment are
mature~\cite{gnpy,pointon}; ML-QoT is active, with estimators trained predominantly
on prediction error~\cite{mlqot}. Our contribution is orthogonal: not a better
estimator or allocator, but a \emph{measurement}---the false-clear rate as a
first-class, consumer-relative KPI---and the finding that the ML-QoT training
\emph{objective}, and the margin allocation, should target the consumer's threshold.

\section{Discussion}
Train QoT models, and size QoT margin, for the decision the FEC decoder makes, not for
average fidelity. The GN model is the evidence rung; a coherent-receiver pre-FEC-BER
witness on a fibre testbed, and the EGN/split-step model, are the external-validity
graduations. (The consumer-relative, false-clear methodology is not
optics-specific~\cite{otcampaigns}, but the results here stand on the optical QoT
certificate alone.)
