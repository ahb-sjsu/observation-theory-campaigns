\title{The Optical Margin is a Consumer-Relative Certificate: Measuring the
Quality-of-Transmission False-Clear Rate}

\author{Andrew H. Bond$^{*}$}
\address{Department of Computer Engineering, San Jos\'e State University, San Jos\'e, CA 95192, USA}
\email{$^{*}$andrew.bond@sjsu.edu}

\begin{abstract}
A Quality-of-Transmission certificate set under reference channel loading
false-clears many lightpaths as the band fills---consumer-relatively, by
footprint---while a footprint-witnessed certificate recovers margin at a measured
false-clear rate. % <=35 words (verify count on final)
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Elastic optical networks provision lightpaths from a pre-transmission
Quality-of-Transmission (QoT) estimate that selects the highest modulation format
whose required generalized signal-to-noise ratio (GSNR) it clears. This estimate is
a \emph{certificate}: a claim, issued under the channel loading present at
provisioning, that the chosen format meets its forward-error-correction (FEC)
threshold. As neighbouring channels are added the nonlinear interference grows and
the true GSNR drops, so the certificate can \emph{false-clear}---the provisioned
format no longer meets its threshold. Operators mask this with margin, i.e.\ stranded
capacity~\cite{pointon,ml-qot}. We argue, and measure, that this margin is a
\emph{consumer-relative} quantity: whether a lightpath crosses its threshold depends
on its \emph{footprint} (spectral position and reach), so a single blanket margin is
adequate for one lightpath and catastrophic for another. The operational figure is
the \emph{false-clear rate}---the fraction of provisioned lightpaths whose witnessed
GSNR violates the certificate---which sets how much margin can be removed safely.

\section{Method}
We compute per-channel GSNR on a standard single-mode-fibre line system
(0.2\,dB/km, $D=17$\,ps/nm/km, EDFA NF 6\,dB, 80\,km spans, +2\,dBm/ch, 32\,GBd on a
50\,GHz grid) using the GN model in GNPy~\cite{gnpy}, the community-standard QoT
engine. The \emph{reference} loading is a sparsely provisioned band; the
\emph{actual} loading is the fully populated C-band. Each consumer is a lightpath
(spectral position, reach); its certificate is the modulation selected from the
GSNR under reference loading (1\,dB design margin, 0.3\,dB monitoring noise), and its
\emph{witness} is the true GSNR under full loading---the coherent receiver's
pre-FEC BER in deployment. A \emph{naive} policy trusts the reference-loading
certificate; a \emph{footprint-aware} policy re-evaluates GSNR under the actual
loading footprint. Bars, cooling-off, and manipulation checks are pre-registered;
the sealed record is versioned with a content hash~\cite{otcampaigns}.

\section{Results}
Across seeds, the naive QoT certificate false-clears
$\mathrm{FC}_{\text{naive}}\approx 0.40$ % GRADED-SWAP
of lightpaths once the band fills (mean loading penalty $\approx 2.6$\,dB), while the
footprint-aware certificate holds at $\mathrm{FC}_{\text{aware}}\approx 0$. % GRADED-SWAP
The false-clears are \emph{footprint-dependent} (per-lightpath false-clear spread
$\approx 0.49$): % GRADED-SWAP
they concentrate on the lightpaths whose spectral position and reach place their GSNR
nearest a format threshold, and are absent elsewhere---so no blanket margin is
simultaneously safe and efficient for all consumers. Figure~\ref{fig:fc} shows the
naive-versus-aware false-clear rate and the per-footprint concentration.

\section{Discussion}
Margin reduction via monitoring and ML-QoT is an active area~\cite{ml-qot}; the
contribution here is not another optimizer but a \emph{measurement}: the false-clear
rate as a first-class, consumer-relative KPI, and the demonstration that QoT margin
is a footprint property. A corollary for ML-QoT: an estimator minimising
\emph{average} OSNR error is misaligned with a consumer that reads a \emph{threshold}
and can false-clear at the FEC cliff; the consumer-read metric is the right training
objective. The GN model is the evidence rung; a coherent-receiver pre-FEC-BER witness
on a fibre testbed, and the EGN/split-step model, are the external-validity
graduations.
