# balance coordinate that degenerates at a boundary

**id.** degenerate-balance-coordinate
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Methods, Volume 1 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A balance coordinate d = (g - l)/(g + l) of two competing non-negative magnitudes equals plus or minus 1 exactly when one magnitude is absent. On those sets it is constant, an interaction term d times x equals plus or minus x, and its coefficient cannot be separated from the coefficient of x.

**Example.** With g = 3 and l = 1 the coordinate is 2/4 = 0.5. With l = 0 it is 1 whether g is 3 or 7, so on that set it tells nothing about g.

## conditions

- The chart-selection rule applies where the endpoints of a bounded coordinate mean absence and not extremity. Fit each side separately, and do not read an interaction coefficient estimated across an endpoint as a property of either side.
- The evidence is a study of 95,748 risky-choice trials in an archived draft of the geometric-economics repository. A pooled interaction of +0.25 fell to about -0.03, within its noise, once the regions were separated.
- The draft was archived on 2026-11-02 because the model it tested has a diagonal metric and hand-chosen coordinates. The volume states that the rule is about coordinates and does not depend on that model.
- The discontinuities the draft reports are jumps in a risk-sensitivity term at two different boundaries. They are not edge weights of the nine-dimensional decision complex.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-methods.toml` rather than read from a record.
