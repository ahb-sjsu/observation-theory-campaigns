# Aesthetic regime

**id.** aesthetic-regime
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Aesthetics, Volume 13 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The local metric of one stratum of the aesthetic manifold. A regime is a set of active dimensions, a local metric on them that fixes how they trade off, and a set of structural invariants. The volume models the metric as roughly constant inside a stratum and as jumping or changing signature at stratum boundaries.

**Example.** The volume's within-stratum figures differ by regime. Fiction gives R = 0.131 on 2,250 books and Classical music gives R = -0.013 on 584 tracks, which the volume reads as null.

## conditions

- Strata correspond to a substantial degree to genres. The volume draws the rule to fit within strata, because a single model across strata mainly learns the genre classifier.
- The strata observed are not required to be Whitney-regular in the full technical sense.
- Section 8.3 names four boundary types (genre boundaries, style thresholds, phase transitions with metric change, absorbing strata). It says there is no clean empirical taxonomy for the absorbing type.
- The signal inside a stratum is supported in fiction and in four mainstream music genres. It is null in non-fiction history and biography and in classical and jazz music.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-aesthetics.toml` rather than read from a record.
