# uncanny valley of flavor

**id.** flavor-uncanny-valley
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The conjecture that hedonic response dips in the near shell of a familiar flavor anchor, close enough that the category fires and far enough that the prediction is violated. The volume's shell penalty is $K(d) = (d^2/\sigma^2)\exp(-d^2/\sigma^2)$, zero at the anchor, largest at distance $\sigma$ and fading far away.

**Example.** With $\sigma = 1$ the shell penalty is 0 at the anchor, $e^{-1}$ or about 0.37 at distance 1, and $4e^{-4}$ or about 0.07 at distance 2. The largest penalty sits at the moat radius, as the volume states.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The uncanny valley of Mori (1970), carried over to flavor expectation.

## conditions

- Tagged a conjecture, by analogy with Mori's uncanny valley for faces. The evidence cited is the literature on expectation and sensory incongruity.
- The penalty is one term of a posited hedonic kernel, beside an inverted-U term and a complementarity reward $\gamma[1 - \cos(\mathbf{f}_1, \mathbf{f}_2)]$.
- The anchors, their weights and the moat radius are said to be personal and cultural, fit to one person's experience. That a recommender fitted this way avoids the valley is a conjecture the volume calls falsifiable and does not test.
- The strawberry and tomato case is the chapter's running example, told as a story about a pairing app. No data are given for it.
