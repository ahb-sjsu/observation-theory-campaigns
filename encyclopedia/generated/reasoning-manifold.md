# Reasoning manifold

**id.** reasoning-manifold
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Reasoning, Volume 3 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The set of coherent reasoning states of a cognitive system, taken to be a low-dimensional manifold $M$ inside the high-dimensional activation space. Reasoning is a trajectory on $M$, and its quality is read from the geometry of $M$ and from how the trajectory sits against the geodesics of $M$.

**Example.** In the volume's illustration a model's moral judgment is a point in a harm space of 7 dimensions, each rated 0 to 10, while the activations that produce it have thousands of dimensions.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The manifold hypothesis of machine learning, which the volume cites from Bengio, Courville and Vincent and extends from data to reasoning states.

## conditions

- Tagged a modeling axiom. The volume calls it a substantive empirical claim and not a definition, and it does not measure the manifold or its metric.
- It asserts three things. Coherent states are a thin subset of activation space, the subset is smooth and low-dimensional, and its geometry is not uniform.
- The support offered is indirect. It is representation engineering, circuit findings, low-rank fine-tuning and the volume's own 7-dimensional harm ratings, which use a Euclidean metric as a first approximation.
- Chapter 15 leaves open whether a Riemannian manifold is the right object and lists Finsler, sub-Riemannian and stratified alternatives.
