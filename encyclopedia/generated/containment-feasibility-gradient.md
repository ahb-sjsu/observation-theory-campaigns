# Feasibility gradient of structural containment

**id.** containment-feasibility-gradient
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric AI, Volume 11 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The ordering of application domains by how well they supply what structural containment needs, which is physical grounding, canonical forms and audit infrastructure. The volume rates medical AI and autonomous vehicles as highly feasible, finance and law as moderate, and content moderation and general assistants as challenging.

**Example.** In the volume's table medicine has physical grounding, ICD and SNOMED-CT codes and electronic health records, and is rated high. Content moderation has linguistic grounding, no standard canonical form and limited audit, and is rated low.

## conditions

- The ratings are qualitative judgments. Section 19.3 proposes a quantitative feasibility metric from grounding quality, canonicalization maturity and audit infrastructure as future work, and none is computed.
- The volume says the gradient does not make the theorem inapplicable in the challenging domains. It says the engineering investment varies and the guarantees are strongest where the grounding is strongest.
- The two statements differ slightly in their labels. Section 8.6 puts finance with law as moderately feasible, and the table of Section 19.3 rates finance moderate to high.
