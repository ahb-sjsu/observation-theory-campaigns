# Political preference manifold

**id.** political-preference-manifold
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The six-dimensional space $\mathcal{P}$ in which each voter is a point with coordinates for economic policy, social values, environmental priority, foreign policy, institutional trust and identity. An electorate is a distribution of points on it.

**Example.** The volume's illustration places the university enclave of District 7 at $(-1.8, -2.0, 2.0, -1.0, 0.5, -1.5)$ in standardized coordinates. Reading the first and third numbers, the enclave is progressive on economics and high on environmental priority.

## conditions

- The volume calls the claim empirical and not axiomatic. The six dimensions are proposed from a theoretical decomposition and factor analysis, and the exact decomposition varies with the survey year and the items.
- Chapter 18 lists the measurement of the manifold and its correct dimensionality as open questions. It says the effective dimensionality may differ across electorates, across time and across scale.
- The manifold is a model of the structure of disagreement. Chapter 4 says it is not a claim about the psychology of the voter.
