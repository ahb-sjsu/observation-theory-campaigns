# Selective Path Dependence (Proposition 13.3)

**id.** selective-path-dependence
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Communication, Volume 7 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The order in which two considerations are presented changes the judgment only on cross-type paths, where the factors point toward different vertices of the Hohfeldian square, and not on within-type paths, where all factors point toward the same vertex. The proposition explained this by non-commuting elements of a $D_4$ gauge group. The chapter marked that explanation withdrawn on 2026-11-26, pending restatement under the Klein four-group $V_4$, and keeps the observed order effect.

**Example.** With the volume's actions s exchanging O with C and L with N, and n exchanging O with L and C with N, applying s then n to O gives n(C) = N, and applying n then s gives s(L) = N. The two orders agree, so $V_4$ alone gives no order effect. In the volume's table 2 of 8 scenarios showed one.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Order effects in judgment, stated in the vocabulary of lattice gauge theory. Geometric Ethics v1.25 withdrew the dihedral group D4 for Hohfeldian transitions, and Chapter 13 has recorded that withdrawal since 2026-11-26.

## conditions

- The evidence cited is SQND Protocol 2 with 640 evaluations over 8 scenarios. Two scenarios, journalist and teacher, showed path dependence (estimates 0.659 and 0.862 in the table) and six did not, with combined chi-squared 72.14 on 16 degrees of freedom.
- The tabulated numbers are estimates and not group elements. The protocol takes the overlap of the response distributions under the two orders (the Bhattacharyya coefficient), which is 1 when the two orders give the same distribution of judgments.
- The sorting of the eight scenarios into cross-type and within-type is given after the results table, in the same section.
- The quarter-turn r of the $D_4$ statement is withdrawn. The chapter's group is now $V_4$, generated by the correlative exchange s and the negation n, which commute, so the group cannot be the source of the order effect. The chapter attributes the effect to curvature of the continuous connection.
- The volume's corrections record says that restating the proposition needs a continuous connection whose curvature gives the order effect, and leaves it to the author.
