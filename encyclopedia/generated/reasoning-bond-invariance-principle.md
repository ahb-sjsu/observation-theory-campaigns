# Bond Invariance Principle for reasoning

**id.** reasoning-bond-invariance-principle
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Reasoning, Volume 3 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A judgment function $f$ satisfies the principle when $f(\tau(x)) = f(x)$ for every description $x$ and every gauge transformation $\tau$. Equivalently the judgment depends only on the content of the description. A failure is a gauge anomaly, and its magnitude along $\tau$ is the distance between $f(\tau(x))$ and $f(x)$.

**Example.** A system that rates a scenario at 40 on a 70-point harm scale and its euphemistic rewrite at 31 has an anomaly of magnitude 9 along that rewrite, where the principle requires 0.

## conditions

- Tagged a modeling axiom. The volume uses it as a diagnostic criterion relative to a chosen class of gauge transformations.
- It applies only to content-preserving transformations. A change to the facts should change the output.
- A test of it is only as good as the claim that the rewrite preserved content. In the benchmark the rewrites were produced by a fixed model.
- Measured against it, the benchmark reports violations under framing, emotional anchoring and sensory distractors, and no significant violation under gender swap or evaluation order.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-reasoning.toml` rather than read from a record.
