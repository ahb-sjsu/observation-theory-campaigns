# economic decision complex

**id.** economic-decision-complex
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Economics, Volume 4 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A weighted simplicial complex whose vertices are economic states, each carrying an attribute vector of nine scores, whose edges are available actions carrying a nonnegative total cost, and whose higher simplices are bundles of actions that can be taken jointly.

**Example.** In the volume's running example the state with no new barista and the state after the hire are two vertices, the hire is the edge between them, and each vertex carries nine scores.

## conditions

- The object is combinatorial and not a smooth manifold. The volume uses manifold language as an approximation when alternatives are many and finely graded.
- Not every pair of states is joined by an edge. Only feasible transitions are, and an edge weight may be infinite.
- The chapter states that the complex is a claim about the space in which decisions are made, not a utility function and not a prescription of what agents should do.
