# Scalar Irrecoverability Theorem

**id.** scalar-irrecoverability-theorem
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Cognition, Volume 6 of the Geometric Series

## definition

For any map from profiles with two or more components to a single real number, each value in the range has a continuum of distinct profiles in its preimage. No function of the score can therefore return the profile.

**Example.** The volume's illustration has three models with scores (0.95, 0.60, 0.80, 0.70, 0.85), (0.70, 0.85, 0.75, 0.80, 0.80) and (0.80, 0.75, 0.78, 0.77, 0.80). Each sums to 3.90, so all three composites are 0.78 and the score cannot say which profile produced it.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The failure of injectivity for a continuous map from two or more dimensions to one. The volume names Spearman's g, IQ and benchmark accuracy as instances of the contraction.

## conditions

- The formal version assumes the contraction is a continuous surjection and the profile has at least two components.
- The theorem says the profile cannot be recovered from the score. The further claim that the discarded structure determines how a system reasons is a separate ledger row held at posited (GC-10).
- The empirical demonstration of Section 1.5 uses the five benchmark models, whose run records are not in the repository.
