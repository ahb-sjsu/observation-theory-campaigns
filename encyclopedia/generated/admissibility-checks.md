# admissibility (checks A1 to A4)

**id.** admissibility-checks
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Methods, Volume 1 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The property that an experimental design could have failed. It has four checks, each discharged by a named artifact. A1 asks whether any outcome could have withheld the pass, A2 whether every fitted object is identified, A3 whether the manipulation moved the named construct, and A4 whether the estimate is the registered one.

**Example.** The A1 witness for an evaluation function is to evaluate it at two different parameter vectors and require the outputs to differ. In the volume's Chapter 19 run, 189,462 calls came down to 32 distinct models, one for each on or off pattern of 5 groups.

## conditions

- A1 is a property of the design and is settled before any data exist, by a constructed record that the registered grading code scores as a miss.
- A2 to A4 are instrument gates. A miss voids the run and does not refute the claim, where a miss at a physics gate refutes it, and the registration separates the two kinds in advance.
- A registration that declares all four checks not applicable has checked nothing.
- The witness of A1 is an incomplete defence. It can be weaker than the real alternative, and a bar that refuses a pure null can still credit a proxy for the effect.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-methods.toml` rather than read from a record.
