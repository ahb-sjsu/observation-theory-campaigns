# Western/Eastern pairing asymmetry

**id.** western-eastern-pairing-asymmetry
**kind.** correction
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Against a null of randomly assembled recipes, North American and Western European recipes use ingredient pairs that share more flavor compounds than chance, and East Asian recipes use pairs that share fewer. The food-pairing hypothesis is reversed in East Asian cuisine, so it cannot be a universal law.

**Example.** If the pairs in a cuisine's recipes share 12 compounds on average and the pairs in frequency-matched random recipes share 10, the effect is +2, the Western sign. An average of 8 against 10 gives -2, the East Asian sign. The numbers are illustrative, and the volume prints no effect size.

## conditions

- The result is that of Ahn et al. (2011), which the volume reports as about 380 ingredients, about 1,021 compounds and 56,498 recipes, with a null model that controls for ingredient frequency.
- The Western effect is driven largely by a few high-frequency hub ingredients such as milk, butter, egg, vanilla and wheat. Removing them weakens it dramatically.
- Section 4.7 proposes a reanalysis with a perceptual embedding and predicts that it would sharpen the asymmetry. The section is tagged a proposed reanalysis with no run recorded for this volume, and the README classes the volume's claims as posited and not measured.
- The volume reads the split as similarity against complementarity and says both are good, for different reasons.
