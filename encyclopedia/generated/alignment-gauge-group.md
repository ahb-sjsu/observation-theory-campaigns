# Alignment gauge group

**id.** alignment-gauge-group
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric AI, Volume 11 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The group of transformations of an input that should leave the behaviour of an aligned AI system unchanged. The volume writes it as the product of four factors, the Hohfeldian dihedral group D4, the translation group T, the re-description group R and the framing group F.

**Example.** The volume's example for the dihedral factor is the pair "the user has a right to accurate information" and "the system has a duty to provide accurate information". They state one moral fact, so an aligned system must evaluate both the same.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** A gauge group in the sense of gauge theory in physics, which the chapter cites through Weyl and through Yang and Mills.

## conditions

- The definition uses the dihedral group for the Hohfeldian positions. Geometric Ethics, the founding volume, withdrew that group in its version 1.25 and uses the Klein four-group. This volume's text has not been revised.
- The volume says the framing group is the hardest factor to formalize, because the line between morally irrelevant framing and morally relevant context is not always sharp. It admits only transformations that neither add nor remove moral information.
- The dihedral factor is taken from Geometric Ethics, Chapter 8, and Geometric Law, Chapter 5. The chapter describes it as generated by the correlative and negation symmetries of the four Hohfeldian positions.
- The 8.9-sigma framing effect and the 100% cross-lingual deontic transfer cited as evidence are quoted as established results, the second from Geometric Communication and Geometric Ethics. This volume reports them and does not measure them.
