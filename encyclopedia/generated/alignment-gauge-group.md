# Alignment gauge group

**id.** alignment-gauge-group
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric AI, Volume 11 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The group of transformations of an input that should leave the behaviour of an aligned AI system unchanged. The volume writes it as the product of four factors, the Hohfeldian Klein four-group V4, the translation group T, the re-description group R and the framing group F.

**Example.** The volume's example for the Hohfeldian factor is the pair "the user has a right to accurate information" and "the system has a duty to provide accurate information". They state one moral fact, so an aligned system must evaluate both the same.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** A gauge group in the sense of gauge theory in physics, which the chapter cites through Weyl and through Yang and Mills.

## conditions

Geometric Ethics withdrew that group in its version 1.25, and a note of 2026-11-26 in Section 7.2.1 restates the definition with the Klein four-group, of order 4. - The volume says the framing group is the hardest factor to formalize, because the line between morally irrelevant framing and morally relevant context is not always sharp. It admits only transformations that neither add nor remove moral information.
- The Hohfeldian factor is taken from Geometric Ethics, Chapter 8, and Geometric Law, Chapter 5. The chapter describes it as generated by the correlative and negation symmetries of the four Hohfeldian positions, which commute.
- The 8.9-sigma framing effect and the 100% cross-lingual deontic transfer cited as evidence are quoted as reported results, the second from Geometric Communication and Geometric Ethics and called a preliminary result in embedding space. This volume reports them and does not measure them.
