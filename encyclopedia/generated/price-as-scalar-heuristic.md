# price signal as scalar heuristic

**id.** price-as-scalar-heuristic
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Economics, Volume 4 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The price is read as a heuristic estimate of the remaining cost that reports only the monetary coordinate. It is the projection onto $d_1$ of the full edge weight to the goal, and it carries nothing about the other eight dimensions.

**Example.** In the volume's example fair-trade beans cost 8.20 dollars a pound and conventional beans 4.50. The price reports a difference of 3.70 dollars on $d_1$ and nothing about fairness, labor conditions or trust.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Hayek's account of prices as carriers of dispersed information, which the volume says holds on the monetary dimension only.

## conditions

- An efficient market is restated as a price heuristic that is admissible on $d_1$. The strongest efficient market hypothesis is restated as a price heuristic that is exact on $d_1$.

- Externalities, information asymmetry and coercive pricing are read as gaps on $d_6$, $d_9$ and $d_4$ between the price heuristic and the full cost.
