# Legal Invariance Principle (LIP)

**id.** legal-invariance-principle
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Law, Volume 5 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A legal judgment procedure is well-posed if and only if its output is unchanged by every legally irrelevant transformation of the case. The listed transformations are renaming the parties, changing their race, gender, religion or national origin, rephrasing arguments without changing their logical content, reordering the presentation, and translating between languages.

**Example.** Renaming Smith v. Jones as Doe v. Roe, with everything else unchanged, must leave the judgment unchanged.

## conditions

- Stated as an axiom. The volume presents it as a formalization of equal protection, due process and the rule that like cases be decided alike, and not as a reform proposal.
- It requires identical treatment only when all legally relevant features are identical. A prior felony conviction is a relevant feature, so a longer sentence for it is no violation.
- The principle does not itself say which features are irrelevant. Chapter 15 calls that choice a normative commitment, and the Judicial Bond Invariance Principle is the volume's answer.
