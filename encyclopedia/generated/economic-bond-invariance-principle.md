# economic Bond Invariance Principle and gauge group

**id.** economic-bond-invariance-principle
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Economics, Volume 4 of the Geometric Series

## definition

An economic evaluation J satisfies the principle when J of a re-described state equals J of the state for every meaning-preserving transformation, one that changes labels, units or accounting conventions and not the economic reality. The transformations form the economic gauge group, scalings of the monetary dimension by a positive real together with permutations of the numeraire.

**Example.** The experiment of Shafir, Diamond and Tversky as the volume reports it. A 2 percent raise under 4 percent inflation is a real change of -2 percent and was rated better than a 1 percent cut under zero inflation, a real change of -1 percent. The rating followed the label, which violates the principle.

## conditions

- Tagged a modeling axiom and described as a consistency condition, not a normative preference.
- The three named classes of meaning-preserving transformation are currency relabeling, unit scaling and numeraire choice.
- Theorem 37 states that the edge weight is invariant under monetary scaling because the covariance rescales with the units. The violations the chapter studies are in the agent's heuristic and not in the manifold cost.
- Remark 45 limits the principle. A changed evaluation may show that the transformation was not meaning preserving and that the descriptions carried different information.
