# Judicial Bond Invariance Principle (JBIP)

**id.** judicial-bond-invariance-principle
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Law, Volume 5 of the Geometric Series

## definition

If a transformation of a dispute's description preserves the Hohfeldian bond structure, meaning the configuration of rights, duties, liberties, no-rights, powers, immunities, liabilities and disabilities among the parties, then the legal evaluation must be unchanged.

**Example.** In the volume's example a landlord refuses a tenant for race in one case and for religion in another. Both cases have the same right, duty, breach, power and liability, so the evaluation must be the same.

## conditions

- Stated as an axiom. It is the Bond Invariance Principle of Geometric Ethics specialized to law.
- The volume calls it stronger than the Legal Invariance Principle because it fixes what counts as legally relevant. Two cases with isomorphic bond structures must receive the same evaluation.
- The volume identifies the bond-preserving transformations with the 64-element Hohfeldian gauge group of Chapter 5.
- The volume notes that existing doctrine applies different levels of scrutiny to race and to sex, and says the axiom suggests this asymmetry is problematic.
