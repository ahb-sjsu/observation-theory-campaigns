# Superalignment Transport Theorem

**id.** superalignment-transport-theorem
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric AI, Volume 11 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A system more capable than its overseers is modelled on a manifold that extends the human value manifold with further dimensions. Human values are carried into it by parallel transport, and the holonomy of that transport measures the alignment loss. The holonomy is trivial exactly when no curvature couples the human dimensions to the extension dimensions.

**Example.** With sectional curvature 0.2 and an enclosed area of 0.5, the approximate holonomy is 0.2 times 0.5, which is 0.1. With curvature 0 it is 0 for any path, the flat case in which transport loses nothing.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** An application of parallel transport and the Ambrose and Singer holonomy theorem of differential geometry.

## conditions

- The extension manifold and its connection are posited. The extension dimensions are by definition ones humans cannot perceive or articulate, so its curvature cannot be measured directly.
- In a flat extension the human-evaluable dimensions stay aligned, and behaviour on the extension dimensions is left undetermined by human values.
- In a curved extension the holonomy is approximately the sectional curvature times the area enclosed by the transport path. Whether that curvature stays bounded as capability grows is open question 4 of Chapter 20.
- The curvature alarm values printed for ARIA-G are fictional.
