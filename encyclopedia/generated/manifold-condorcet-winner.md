# Manifold Condorcet winner

**id.** manifold-condorcet-winner
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The candidate with the lowest mean manifold distance to the electorate. The volume uses it to show that a cycle in ordinal majority preferences can coexist with a single nearest candidate once distances are kept.

**Example.** In the volume's table the three blocs are 1.2, 2.8 and 5.1 from A, 2.5, 0.3 and 2.2 from B, and 4.8, 2.5 and 1.4 from C. The means are about 3.0, 1.7 and 2.9, so B is the manifold Condorcet winner although the ordinal preferences on the one-dimensional projection cycle.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** It sits beside the Condorcet winner of social choice theory, which is defined by pairwise majorities and not by mean distance.

## conditions

- It needs cardinal distances from each voter or bloc to each candidate. Ordinal rankings alone do not give it.
- It is introduced by a worked example and has no numbered definition. The chapter says its sense, the lowest mean manifold distance, is not a pairwise-majority criterion. The term Condorcet winner in its usual pairwise sense is used elsewhere in the volume.
- The bloc distances in the example are from the fictional District 7, and the chapter introduces the table as illustrative.
