# Recovery ceiling (about 38 percent)

**id.** prompt-recovery-ceiling
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Reasoning, Volume 3 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The volume's claim that an explicit instruction to re-evaluate restores a displaced judgment about 38 percent of the time, at the same rate after emotional anchoring and after vivid distractors. It models this as an escape probability equal to the fraction of exit directions from a basin times a threshold factor in the impulse energy.

**Example.** In the volume's formula a basin with half of its directions leading out and a threshold factor of 0.8 gives an escape probability of 0.5 times 0.8 = 0.4.

## conditions

- The rates are tagged empirical and come from two tasks on five models. The basin geometry that is said to explain them is posited and no exit angle is measured.
- The volume's own table does not give the figure. Table 5.2 lists recovery rates of 20, 73, 47, 55 and 53 percent after emotional anchoring, whose mean is 49.6 percent.
- Chapter 13 uses the same name for a different quantity, a bound of 62 to 72 percent on resistance scores.
- The volume predicts that new perturbation types will also recover at about 38 percent under prompting, and it marks this as testable and not yet tested.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-reasoning.toml` rather than read from a record.
