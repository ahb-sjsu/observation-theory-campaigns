# Polarization Illusion Theorem

**id.** polarization-illusion-theorem
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A preference distribution with a single region of high density on the manifold can look bimodal when projected onto one axis oblique to its principal axis. A bimodal one-dimensional projection is therefore necessary but not sufficient evidence of polarization on the manifold.

**Example.** A one-axis histogram with two peaks is consistent with two separated clusters and with one elongated cluster seen obliquely. In the volume's District 7 illustration the party centroids are 1.5 Mahalanobis units apart, which the chapter reads as moderate separation with substantial overlap.
