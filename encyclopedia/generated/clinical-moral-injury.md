# Moral injury as forced boundary crossing

**id.** clinical-moral-injury
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Medicine, Volume 8 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A clinician is morally injured when the institutionally mandated path requires crossing a boundary to which the clinician's own complex assigns a penalty above the clinician's tolerance threshold. Each such crossing adds the excess of the penalty over the threshold, and the sum over time is the cumulative moral injury.

**Example.** In the volume's example a clinician assigns the abandonment boundary a penalty of 100 and has a threshold of 50, so each forced crossing adds 100 - 50 = 50. A clinician with penalty 40 and the same threshold adds nothing. One forced crossing a day for thirty days gives thirty increments, a total of 30 times 50 = 1500, and that last product is arithmetic.

## conditions

- Three things must hold together, namely an institutional mandate, a boundary crossing, and a penalty above the threshold. A voluntary crossing or one below the threshold gives distress but not injury.
- Theorem 13.1 adds that the total never decreases and that above a clinician-specific critical level the heuristic is permanently altered, by inflated penalties (hypervigilance) or deflated ones (numbing).
- The penalties and thresholds of clinicians have not been measured. Chapter 14 marks its prediction from pre-crisis penalty profiles as not yet tested.
- Section 18.9 gives the claim the status consistent with the existing moral injury literature, generates novel predictions.
