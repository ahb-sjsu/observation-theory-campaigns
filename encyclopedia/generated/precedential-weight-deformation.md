# Precedential weight deformation

**id.** precedential-weight-deformation
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Law, Volume 5 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A binding precedent at a vertex adds a term to the weights of nearby edges. The size of the term scales as the precedent's hierarchical weight divided by the graph distance from the precedent raised to a decay exponent $n$, and the deformations of several precedents add.

**Example.** With hierarchical weight 8 and decay exponent 2, the deformation scales as 8 / 4 = 2 at graph distance 2 and as 8 / 16 = 0.5 at graph distance 4.

## conditions

- The decay law is given as a scaling relation. The volume gives no values for the exponent, the hierarchical weights or the holding operator.
- Hierarchical weight is ordered with the Supreme Court above a circuit court and a circuit court above a district court.
- The deformation defines the legal connection. Carrying a rule along heavily deformed edges may rotate it, and the rotation around a closed path is the holonomy read by the Wilson loop.
