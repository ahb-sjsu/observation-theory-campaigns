# learner manifold

**id.** learner-manifold
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The six-dimensional Riemannian manifold of learner states. A state is a point $(d_1, \ldots, d_6)$ whose coordinates are domain knowledge, procedural skill, metacognitive awareness, motivation and engagement, transfer ability, and creativity, each scored from 0 to 1. Learning is a path on it.

**Example.** The volume's illustrative state for Alex at college entry is (0.45, 0.85, 0.70, 0.80, 0.75, 0.65). The first coordinate, domain knowledge, is the lowest and the second, procedural skill, is the highest, and all six lie between 0 and 1.

## conditions

- The description is exact where learning is continuous and smooth. Where learning has discontinuities such as sudden insight, the volume calls it an approximation.
- Whether six is the right number of dimensions is, in the volume's words, an open empirical question. Proposition 4.1 is checked against six scenarios the volume itself lists.
- Domain knowledge is treated as one aggregate of many subject-specific dimensions, and the volume notes that this aggregation is itself a contraction.
- The manifold grows as the student learns, so a path is planned on a space that will have changed by the time the student reaches each waypoint.
