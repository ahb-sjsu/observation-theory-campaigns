# Geometric justification of a public health mandate

**id.** public-health-mandate-justification
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Medicine, Volume 8 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A mandate is geometrically justified when three conditions hold together. The collective geodesic differs from the Nash equilibrium of individual choices, the restriction of autonomy crosses no sacred-value boundary, and the collective benefit on outcomes and social impact exceeds the individual cost on autonomy under the community's covariance matrix.

**Example.** If the covariance-weighted collective benefit is 4 and the autonomy cost is 1, the ratio is 4 and the third condition holds. If the same mandate crosses a sacred-value boundary it fails the second condition and is not justified at any ratio. The volume's own remark is that a mandate justified at 5 percent population immunity may not be justified at 85 percent.

## conditions

- All three conditions are necessary. A mandate that crosses a sacred-value boundary is never justified, whatever the collective benefit.
- Proposition 11.4 says the justification is not static, because the proportionality condition can stop holding as population immunity, transmissibility and treatment change.
- The proportionality ratio needs the community's covariance matrix, which has not been estimated. The applications to vaccination and quarantine are qualitative.
