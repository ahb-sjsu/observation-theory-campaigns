# Moral manifold

**id.** moral-manifold
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Ethics, Volume 2 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The space M whose points are structured moral situations, each a complete specification of agents, relationships, options and morally relevant context. Formally it is a paracompact Hausdorff space with a Whitney stratification into smooth manifolds. Agent perspectives are a fiber over each point and are not points of M.

**Example.** Alice's promise to Bob on a normal Saturday is one point of M. The same Saturday with an emergency is a different point, and Alice's and Bob's views of either one are two points of the fiber over it, not two points of M.

## conditions

- The volume labels it a modeling choice. It says the manifold is a model, not a discovery, and that it is posited, not proved.
- It is a stratified space and not a smooth manifold. Smooth calculus holds inside each stratum only.
- The volume says the manifold must be extended if a moral phenomenon appears that its dimensions cannot represent, and reduced if dimensions prove redundant. Section 5.3 reports a tenth axis that passed a preregistered gate and four learned axes that do not separate from one general-valence channel.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-ethics.toml` rather than read from a record.
