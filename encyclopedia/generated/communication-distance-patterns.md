# diagnostic distance patterns (translation, sarcasm, paraphrase)

**id.** communication-distance-patterns
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Communication, Volume 7 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Three profiles of the factor distances. Translation has a large signal distance with pragmatic and content distances near zero. Sarcasm has a small signal distance with large pragmatic and content distances. Paraphrase has a moderate signal distance with the other two near zero. A scalar evaluation that projects onto one factor conflates at least two of them.

**Example.** A metric that sees only the signal distance reads a translation (large) and a paraphrase (moderate) as two sizes of the same thing, since both have pragmatic and content distances near zero. This is the volume's case of a word error rate.

## conditions

- The profiles are stated for a faithful translation and for the textual representation of sarcasm. In the acoustic representation the signal distance of sarcasm is nonzero because prosody differs.
- The entries of the table are qualitative (large, small, moderate, near zero). No distances are measured.
- In the full metric with cross-terms a signal change may induce a small content shift, which the volume calls the holonomy of translation.
