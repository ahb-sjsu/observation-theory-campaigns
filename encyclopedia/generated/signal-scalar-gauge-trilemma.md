# No Scalar Is Gauge-Invariant (Proposition 3.5)

**id.** signal-scalar-gauge-trilemma
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Communication, Volume 7 of the Geometric Series

## definition

No scalar evaluation of a communicative act can be at once invariant under the communication gauge group, sensitive to differences in content, and computable from the signal alone. Any two of the three properties can hold together.

**Example.** A semantic distance read off the content coordinate is invariant and content-sensitive, and it needs the content, which the signal alone does not give. An n-gram overlap score is computable from the signal and content-sensitive, and it changes under paraphrase.

## conditions

- The argument assumes that different signals can express the same content and that the same signal can arise in different languages, so a function of the signal alone cannot separate content classes.
- Invariance with content sensitivity is obtained by projecting onto the content manifold, which needs access to the content. Content sensitivity with computability from the signal is what standard metrics have, without invariance.
- The chapter names three ways to approximate invariance. They are parallel data, structured evaluation, and geometric features invariant under a subgroup.
