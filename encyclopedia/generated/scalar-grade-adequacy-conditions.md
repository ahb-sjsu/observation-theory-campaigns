# adequacy conditions for a scalar grade

**id.** scalar-grade-adequacy-conditions
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A scalar assessment adequately represents the learner state when three conditions hold together. The assessment activates mainly one dimension, the students are about equal on every other dimension, and no equity, access or fairness issue arises from the contraction. Each level set then holds only educationally equivalent states.

**Example.** The volume's adequate case is a typing speed test in a homogeneous population. Its inadequate case is a university calculus exam in a diverse population, which activates at least three dimensions and feeds consequential decisions, so all three conditions fail.

## conditions

- All three conditions are needed at once. The volume says they hold for narrowly defined skill assessments in homogeneous populations.
- The volume says they fail for every course that involves higher-order thinking, diverse students or consequential stakes.
- The proposition is stated without proof, and the terms adequate and educationally equivalent are not given a numerical test.
