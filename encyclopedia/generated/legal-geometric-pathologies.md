# Four geometric pathologies of law

**id.** legal-geometric-pathologies
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Law, Volume 5 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Four ways the legal search goes wrong. Heuristic corruption adds to a doctrinal heuristic a bias tied to legally irrelevant features. Objective hijacking optimizes a measurable proxy in place of the legal objective. A legal local minimum is a precedent too costly to overrule. Gauge breaking is an evaluation that changes under an equal protection transformation.

**Example.** A heuristic whose true estimate is 6.5 and which adds 1.5 for a defendant's race returns 8.0, and the 1.5 is the corruption term. A local minimum with barrier 5 and gain 3 is inescapable because 5 exceeds 3.

- The examples, such as plea bargaining as objective hijacking and qualified immunity as a local minimum, are the volume's readings of known phenomena.
