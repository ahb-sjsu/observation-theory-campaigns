# Deliberative Recruitment Hypothesis (gate H1)

**id.** deliberative-recruitment-hypothesis
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Cognition, Volume 6 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The claim, labelled GC-DRH-1, that for a participant whose sensitivity profile was identified on a calibration block, process measures of deliberation on a disjoint test block peak at that participant's identified resolution boundary. The peak sits at a larger separation when the probe's attribute carries low workload variance in the context.

**Example.** In the self-test the synthetic rank budget observer scores 0.988 in the high role and 0.691 in the low role at the largest separation, a difference of 0.297, while the uniform noise observer scores 0.986 in both, a difference of 0.000. These are simulated observers and not participants.

## conditions

- The registration is a draft and is not sealed. No participant has been recruited, no pilot has run and no bar is fixed, and human data collection waits on ethics approval.
- The only stage run is a self-test on synthetic observers on 2026-09-18. It passed 20 of 20 checks on its fourth run, after three recorded failures that changed the estimator.
- The registration says the self-test shows the estimator reads the endpoints and the nulls correctly on built stimuli, and does not show that a human observer will produce them.
- The recruitment part is not graded when the identification part misses. The registration says a pass would say nothing about curvature or about language models.
