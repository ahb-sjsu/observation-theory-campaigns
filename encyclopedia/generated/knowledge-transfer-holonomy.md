# knowledge transfer as parallel transport, and transfer holonomy

**id.** knowledge-transfer-holonomy
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A piece of understanding is a tangent vector k at a point in one domain chart of the learner manifold. Transfer is parallel transport of k along a path into a second chart, giving k'. The holonomy H, equal to k' minus k, measures the distortion. Transfer succeeds when the norm of H is small.

**Example.** With a maximum curvature of 2 and a path that encloses an area of 0.3, the bound of Theorem 11.1 gives a holonomy norm of at most 0.6. On a flat region the curvature is 0 and the holonomy is 0.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Near, far and negative transfer in educational research, and holonomy of parallel transport in differential geometry.

## conditions

- Theorem 11.1 states that the holonomy is zero where the manifold is flat between the domains, is at most the maximum curvature times the area enclosed by the path, and depends on the path taken.
- The theorem is stated without proof, with a reference to the Ambrose-Singer theorem. It writes the holonomy as an integral of the connection form around a closed curve, while the path of the definition runs from one domain to another.
- Near transfer is read as low curvature between similar domains and far transfer as high curvature. A route through an intermediate domain may have lower total holonomy than the direct route.
- No curvature between domains has been measured. Prediction 16.3.3, on interdisciplinary courses and transfer scores, has not been tested.
