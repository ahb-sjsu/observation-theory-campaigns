# nutritional feasible set and the geometric diet

**id.** geometric-diet-set
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The nutritional feasible set $\mathcal{C}$ is the convex polytope of intake vectors with every nutrient between its lower requirement and its upper limit, $\ell_k \le n_k \le u_k$. The geometric diet set is the set of diets whose nutrition lies in $\mathcal{C}$ and whose flavor lies within $\varepsilon$ of the hedonic manifold.

**Example.** With two nutrients bounded by 50 to 100 and by 20 to 40, the intake (60, 30) is feasible and (60, 45) is not. In the volume's figures Stigler's hand solution of 39.93 dollars exceeds the simplex optimum of 39.69 by 0.24 dollars.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Stigler's diet problem (1945) and linear programming over a convex polytope.

## conditions

- Both sets are tagged modeling axioms. The palatability and nutritional-quality scalars of Section 11.2 are defined and not measured.
- Optimizing one scalar over the polytope lands on a vertex. The volume's case is Stigler's diet problem, with nine nutrients and 77 foods, costing 39.93 dollars a year by hand and 39.69 by the simplex method, in 1939 dollars.
- That the diet set is nonempty is argued from traditional cuisines. That seasoning moves are cheap in nutrition and make the problem locally tractable is tagged a conjecture.
- Both sets differ from person to person. The volume conjectures that there is no universal optimal diet.
