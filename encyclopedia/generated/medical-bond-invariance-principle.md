# Medical Bond Invariance Principle

**id.** medical-bond-invariance-principle
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Medicine, Volume 8 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Clinical evaluations must be invariant under patient re-description. If a re-description of the patient's state preserves the attribute vector, for example a different physician, language or chart format, the clinical geodesic must be the same.

**Example.** The volume's example is the algorithm studied by Obermeyer and colleagues (2019), which used health costs as a proxy for health needs. Changing the race recorded on the intake form, with the clinical state held fixed, would change its recommendation, and that is a violation. A recommendation that is the same for a dignity score recorded as 7 of 10 or 70 of 100 passes on that re-description.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The clinical instance of the Bond Invariance Principle of Geometric Ethics.

## conditions

- It is a normative requirement, stated as a definition. It applies only to re-descriptions that leave the nine-component attribute vector unchanged, and deciding which do is a judgment.
- The volume reads algorithmic bias as a violation, when a recommendation changes with race, zip code or insurance status.
- Section 18.3 says the symmetry group is identified in principle only, and the front matter says the conservation law for clinical reasoning remains conjectural.
