# flavor space and the flavor vector

**id.** flavor-space
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A posited finite-dimensional perceptual space $\mathcal{F}$ in which every flavor experience is a point, every ingredient a vector and every culinary operation a map from the space to itself. The flavor vector concatenates a taste block (about 5 dimensions), an aroma block (about 10 to 30), a trigeminal block (about 5) and a texture and temperature block.

**Example.** Adding the block sizes the volume gives, 5 taste, 10 to 30 aroma and 5 trigeminal dimensions make 20 to 40 before the texture and temperature block, against about 400 receptor types. In the volume's line-cook example a flat dish is fixed by solving for two amounts, one of lime and one of miso.

## conditions

- Tagged a modeling axiom. The existence of the space and its modest dimension are posited, not measured.
- The coordinates are perceptual, recovered by dimensionality reduction from receptor responses, and are not columns of a chemistry table. The chain the volume gives runs from about 10,000 volatiles through about 400 receptor types to perceptual dimensions on the order of tens.
- The static point is an idealization. The volume says the honest object is a curve traced in time, and Chapter 14 lists that temporal geometry as an open problem.
