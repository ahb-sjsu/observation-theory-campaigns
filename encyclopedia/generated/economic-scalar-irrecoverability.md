# Scalar Irrecoverability Theorem (economic form)

**id.** economic-scalar-irrecoverability
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Economics, Volume 4 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Any continuous map from nine-dimensional attribute vectors to a scalar utility is not injective and has no left inverse, and the optimal path on the decision complex can differ from the utility-maximizing path by an amount that no function of the scalar bounds.

**Example.** In two dimensions the map that adds the coordinates sends (1, 0) and (0, 1) both to 1, so the pair cannot be recovered from the sum. The volume's illustration is a shop valued at 200,000 dollars, a value shared by every configuration on one level set.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Brouwer's invariance of dimension, and the non-injectivity of a continuous map from a higher-dimensional space to the line.

## conditions

- The map is assumed continuous. Non-injectivity is proved from Brouwer's invariance of dimension on the unit cube.
- The unbounded divergence of paths in part 3 uses a boundary penalty that can be infinite while the scalar difference stays finite.
- The front matter tags irrecoverability a conditional theorem. It is a statement about maps, and it bears on economic choice only where choice depends on more than one coordinate, which the volume posits.
