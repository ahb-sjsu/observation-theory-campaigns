# grading moral injury

**id.** grading-moral-injury
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The injury a teacher incurs when a mandated assessment G requires a grade the teacher judges to be a lossy projection of the true learner state x. Its increment is $\|x - \psi(G(x))\|$, the distance from x to the state an observer would infer from the grade alone, and the career total is the sum over assessment events and students.

**Example.** If the teacher sees a state (0.3, 0.9) on two dimensions and the grade alone suggests (0.6, 0.6), the increment is the square root of 0.09 + 0.09, about 0.42. Ten assessment events with 30 students at 0.42 each total about 127.

## conditions

- The reconstruction $\psi$ is called the best reconstruction and is not specified, so the increment has no computed value in the volume.
- Theorem 8.1 is the sum of the increments. The volume draws from it that injury is greatest for teachers who grade often, teach diverse students and perceive the full learner state accurately.
- The volume separates it from burnout, which it ties to workload. Prediction 16.3.6 proposes a survey test of that dissociation, and Chapter 18 says the predictions have not been tested.
- The reading of the rise in average grades from 2.52 in 1950 to 3.15 in 2020 as moral numbing is the volume's interpretation, not a tested claim.
