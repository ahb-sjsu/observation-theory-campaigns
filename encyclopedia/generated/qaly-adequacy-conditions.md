# When QALYs are adequate

**id.** qaly-adequacy-conditions
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Medicine, Volume 8 of the Geometric Series

## definition

QALY-based analysis is an adequate approximation of the clinical geodesic when and only when three conditions hold together. The decision activates mainly the outcomes dimension, the patient population is homogeneous on the other eight dimensions, and no moral boundary is crossed by any available action.

**Example.** Comparing two pharmacologically similar drugs for one well-defined condition in a homogeneous population meets all three conditions, which is the volume's example. Add one available action that crosses the futility boundary and the third condition fails, so the scalar is no longer adequate whatever the first two say.

## conditions

- The volume says the conditions are approximately satisfied for routine cost-effectiveness comparisons of pharmacologically similar drugs for a well-defined condition in a homogeneous population.
- It says they are violated in end-of-life care, mental health, disability, resource rationing, vulnerable populations and cross-cultural care.
- The proposition is stated without a proof, and adequate approximation is not given a numerical tolerance.
