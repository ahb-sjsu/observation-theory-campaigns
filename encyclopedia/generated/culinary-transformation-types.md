# geometric types of culinary transformation

**id.** culinary-transformation-types
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Each cooking process is a map $\Phi_\theta$ on flavor space set by process variables such as temperature, time, water activity and pH. The volume sorts the maps into five types. Maillard browning expands dimension, caramelization follows a one-dimensional curve, protein denaturation collapses dimensions, fermentation is a slow flow, and smoking, curing and aging are approximately translations.

**Example.** In the volume's translation model, smoked salmon is the salmon vector plus a smoke vector. With salmon at (5, 1, 0) and smoke at (0, 0, 2) the result is (5, 1, 2), so the base survives and one new component is added. The numbers are illustrative.

## conditions

- Tagged a modeling axiom. The types are a classification, and no map is fitted.
- The Maillard map is close to the identity until the surface dries and passes about 140 degrees Celsius, where it switches on sharply.
- Caramelization cannot be reversed, since its progress coordinate is monotone in accumulated heat.
- The volume qualifies two types. Denaturation also frees substrate for other maps and can open texture dimensions, and the translations carry second-order couplings through water activity and pH.
