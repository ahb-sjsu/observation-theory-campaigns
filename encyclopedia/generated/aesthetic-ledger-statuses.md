## definition

The labels the volume puts on each formal claim. Proved means standard mathematics. Verified empirically means tested on the volume's data, with effect size, sample size and p-value. Conjectured means stated as a prediction and not tested. Analogy means mathematical language used without a literal claim on the domain.

**Example.** In the ledger the cross-lingual correlations (F12.1) are verified empirically, while their reading as a Noether conservation law (F12.3) is analogy, so one chapter carries two statuses.

## conditions

- A verified claim is a finding in the volume's corpora, not a theorem. The appendix says the verification is withdrawn if a future replication fails.
- Null results are entered as verified nulls (non-fiction, Classical, Jazz).
- The Noether reading (F12.3) and the quantum chapter (F13.1 to F13.3) are the two places labeled analogy.
- The ledger does not record the pre-registration or the failed compressibility formula that the README's status block mentions.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-aesthetics.toml` rather than read from a record.
