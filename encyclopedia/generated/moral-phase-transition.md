# Moral phase transition

**id.** moral-phase-transition
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Ethics, Volume 2 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A point on a path through moral space, at a value of a continuous control parameter, where the satisfaction function is discontinuous (first order), or is continuous with a discontinuous gradient (second order), or where both are continuous and the metric is discontinuous (higher order).

**Example.** At the consent boundary the volume sets satisfaction to minus infinity below the threshold (treatment is battery) and to a finite value above it. The jump is a first-order transition.

## conditions

- The definition is a modeling axiom, and the parallel with phase transitions in statistical physics is the volume's analogy.
- A phase transition boundary (Type II) is one across which the metric changes in rank or signature. A threshold (Type I) keeps the same framework on both sides and changes only the conclusion.
- The critical phenomena the volume lists (heightened concern, amplified disagreement, slow deliberation near a boundary) are offered as indirect evidence and are not measured in the volume.
- An auxiliary prediction of asymmetric obligation thresholds, called hysteresis, was falsified and led to a revision of the framework.
