# Moral residue of contraction

**id.** moral-residue-of-contraction
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Medicine, Volume 8 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The clinical friction of the path a forced contraction mandates minus the clinical friction of the clinical geodesic on the full complex, for the same clinical state. It is never negative, and it is zero only when the contraction selects the same path as the geodesic.

**Example.** If the path a triage rule forces has clinical friction 12 and the geodesic for the same patient has friction 4, the moral residue is 8. If the rule happens to pick the geodesic the residue is 0. The numbers are arithmetic from the definition.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The volume takes the term moral residue from Epstein and Hamric (2009) and gives it this geometric reading.

## conditions

- Non-negativity holds because the forced path is one feasible path and the geodesic is the minimum-cost path.
- The definition is given in Chapter 2, before clinical friction is defined in Chapter 4.
- The volume says the residue accumulates in the clinician and alters the heuristic. That claim belongs to its account of moral injury and is not measured.
