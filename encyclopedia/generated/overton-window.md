# Overton window as geodesic constraint

**id.** overton-window
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A connected, bounded subset $W$ of the preference manifold. Positions inside it cost nothing, and a position outside pays a penalty equal to a coefficient times the squared manifold distance to $W$. A trajectory is Overton-admissible when it stays inside the window of its time.

**Example.** With penalty coefficient 2, a position 3 manifold units outside the window costs $2 \times 3^2 = 18$, and a position inside costs 0. If the geodesic between two positions has length 4 and the shortest path inside the window has length 6, the Overton cost of the path is 2.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The Overton window of Joseph Overton, usually drawn as an interval on the left-right axis.

## conditions

- The boundary is a gradient and not a wall. The quadratic penalty and its coefficient are posited.
- The window moves. The volume names four forces, which are elite adoption, crisis events, generational replacement and media amplification.
- The Overton cost of a constrained path is its length minus the length of the unconstrained geodesic. It is zero when the geodesic stays inside the window.
- The shapes given for the American and Scandinavian windows and for District 7 over three decades are descriptive.
