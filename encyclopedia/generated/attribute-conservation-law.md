# attribute conservation in closed exchange

**id.** attribute-conservation-law
**kind.** result
**volume.** Geometric Economics, Volume 4 of the Geometric Series

## definition

In a closed bilateral exchange between agents A and B the changes on each transferable dimension sum to zero, so that the change for A plus the change for B is zero on money $d_1$, rights $d_2$ and autonomy $d_4$. The evaluative dimensions are not conserved and may rise for both parties.

**Example.** The volume's worked example. Maria pays 2,000 dollars and Elena receives 2,000, which sums to zero on $d_1$. The evaluative gains sum to 0.7 + 0.5 + 0.9 + 0.3 = 2.4.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The accounting identity that every payment is a receipt, and Noether's theorem for the derivation the volume gives.

## conditions

- The exchange is closed. No third party enters and no external rule changes during it.
- In an open system the sum equals the net external input on that dimension. The volume reads an observed violation as a sign that the system boundary was drawn too narrowly.
- The theorem is tagged conditional. Money is conserved by accounting identity, rights by the Hohfeldian correlative structure taken from Geometric Ethics, and autonomy in the sense that voluntary constraints are symmetric.
- Section 9.9 rederives the law from the invariance principle by a Noether argument, also tagged conditional. Chapter 16 lists the empirical pattern as an untested prediction.
