# Aesthetic Invariance Claim

**id.** aesthetic-invariance-claim
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Aesthetics, Volume 13 of the Geometric Series

## definition

If two descriptions of a work are related by an admissible coordinate transformation, such as faithful translation between natural languages, then any legitimate geometric aesthetic feature assigns the same value to both. The volume treats each language as a coordinate chart and translation as a change of coordinates.

**Example.** Exact invariance would give a correlation of 1 between a feature measured on English books and on their Finnish translations. The volume measured 0.77 for the Hellinger feature on 288 pairs, so the claim holds approximately.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The volume's analogue of the Bond Invariance Principle of Geometric Ethics, framed after Noether's theorem.

## conditions

- Appendix F marks the Noether reading as analogy (F12.3). No Lagrangian with a continuous symmetry group is established and no conservation law is proved.
- The empirical witness is the cross-lingual correlation near 0.70 on six language families, measured with LaBSE in an English-derived basis. Section 12.9 lists the encoder, the corpus, the basis and the projection as limits.
- Swapping modality is declared not an admissible transformation. Across modalities the sign of a feature's effect is not preserved, which the volume calls broken symmetry.
- The conserved object is the feature vector, not a scalar beauty. Verdicts are not claimed to be preserved by translation.
