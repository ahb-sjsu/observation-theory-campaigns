# withdrawn versus refuted

**id.** withdrawn-versus-refuted
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Methods, Volume 1 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A claim is refuted when a valid measurement contradicted it, and its negation is then supported. A claim is withdrawn when its evidence turns out to be invalid, through a broken instrument, a contaminated arm or a sampling rule that was not what it was thought to be. It returns to unknown, and its negation gains nothing.

**Example.** The volume's case is the claim that hubness rises with corpus size at about +0.5 per decade. With the rows shuffled before the split the slope was +0.26 plus or minus 0.20, which fits a rise and fits none, so the claim was withdrawn and its negation was not asserted.

## conditions

- Both are classes of the claim ledger of PE-CLS-1.0, and that specification governs where the chapter differs from it.
- The two are kept apart only if physics gates and instrument gates are separated before the run. Otherwise a failed experiment can be redescribed afterwards as a broken instrument.
- When a claim is refuted or withdrawn, every claim that depends on it is suspended until it is re-examined.
