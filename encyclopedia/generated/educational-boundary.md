# educational boundary

**id.** educational-boundary
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A threshold on the learner manifold where the region a learner can reach changes discontinuously. The volume defines three types. A prerequisite boundary comes from the structure of knowledge, an institutional boundary is imposed by an institution, and a developmental boundary comes from the learner's developmental stage.

**Example.** With a prerequisite threshold of 0.6 on linear algebra, a student at 0.4 cannot reach the region labeled quantum mechanics and a student at 0.7 can. The volume's example of an institutional boundary is a 3.0 grade point average to stay in an honors program.

## conditions

- A prerequisite boundary is unavoidable. The volume says a student who skips the prerequisite is in a region where the directions of motion needed for the content do not exist.
- An institutional boundary may or may not match the structure of knowledge. The volume's diagnostic is that a prerequisite whose removal leaves success in the later course unchanged, with other variables controlled, is institutional.
- A developmental boundary cannot be crossed by instruction until the connectivity exists. The volume takes the stages from Piaget.
- The diagnostic is proposed and the volume reports no application of it.
