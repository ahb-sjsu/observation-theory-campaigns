# Sacred value as curvature singularity

**id.** sacred-value-singularity
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A sacred value is a position a voter treats as non-negotiable. The volume models it as a region where the metric component $g_{ii}$ on that dimension grows without bound, so that crossing the boundary has infinite cost.

**Example.** Two voters agree on five dimensions and sit on opposite sides of a sacred boundary on the sixth. Their coordinate difference may be one scale point, and the volume puts their distance in the political metric at infinity.

## conditions

- The construction is a hypothesis. Chapter 18 asks whether the manifold is flat in the center and curved near sacred values, as Chapter 4 hypothesizes, and says the data to test it do not exist.
- It applies to the dimension that carries the sacred value. The other five coordinates of the two voters may agree.
- The volume draws a design reading from it, that procedures which make voters trade a sacred value against other dimensions are ill-conditioned. That reading is not tested in the volume.
