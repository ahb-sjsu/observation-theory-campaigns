# Bond geodesic equilibrium (BGE)

**id.** bond-geodesic-equilibrium
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Economics, Volume 4 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A profile of paths, one per agent, in which each agent's path is a Bond geodesic on that agent's decision complex given the paths of the others. No agent can lower its behavioral friction, its total path cost on the full complex, by changing path alone.

**Example.** The volume's ultimatum game with 10 dollars. A responder whose fairness penalty exceeds $1^2/\sigma_1^2$ rejects an offer of 1 dollar, and a proposer who anticipates this offers 40 to 50 percent.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Nash equilibrium, applied to the augmented game whose payoffs are the negative of behavioral friction.

## conditions

- The front matter tags the choice of the BGE as the equilibrium concept a modeling axiom.
- A pure BGE need not exist. Theorem 23 gives a mixed BGE for a finite game in which every agent always has a path of finite cost to its goal.
- A BGE is not Pareto optimal in general, and several can coexist. Lemma 26 gives uniqueness when each agent's self-separation exceeds its cross-sensitivity.
- Agents are assumed to pathfind on the manifold they perceive. The volume says a miscalibrated heuristic or covariance gives a BGE of the perceived game that can differ from that of the true game.
