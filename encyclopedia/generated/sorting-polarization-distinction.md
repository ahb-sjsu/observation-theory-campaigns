# Sorting versus polarization

**id.** sorting-polarization-distinction
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Sorting is the alignment of the covariance matrix, with positions on different dimensions becoming correlated through party identity. Polarization is the separation of the party centroids on the manifold. The first concerns second moments and the second concerns means.

**Example.** Two parties have centroids at $(-0.5, -0.5)$ and $(0.5, 0.5)$, a distance of $\sqrt{2}$, about 1.41. If the variance inside each party falls from 1 to 0.25 the centroids have not moved, so sorting has risen and polarization has not.

## conditions

- The two are treated as geometrically independent. Either can be high while the other is low.
- Both produce a bimodal one-dimensional projection, so one axis cannot tell them apart. Telling them apart needs the full covariance structure.
- The volume's reading of the 2020s American electorate as high sorting with moderate polarization, with party means about 1.5 standard deviations apart, is stated as approximate.
