# Bond Invariance Principle

**id.** moral-bond-invariance-principle
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Ethics, Volume 2 of the Geometric Series

## definition

If two descriptions d and d' of one situation are related by an admissible coordinate transformation, a legitimate moral evaluation assigns both the same value, $E(d) = E(d')$. In group terms the evaluation is constant on the orbits of the admissible re-description group.

**Example.** If withholding treatment and allowing natural death pick out the same action in all morally relevant respects, an evaluation that scores one 0.6 must score the other 0.6. A score of 0.6 against 0.4 violates the principle.

## conditions

- The volume tags it as a definition and modeling choice. It is an axiom of the framework, not a measured fact about evaluators.
- It demands invariance under re-description, not under re-situation. Descriptions that pick out different features or contexts may be evaluated differently.
- Both descriptions must pass the five admissibility conditions of Section 5.5, which are same physical grounding, same agent structure, same stratum, reversibility and no evaluative loading.
- The volume posits the admissible group as V4 times U(1)_H. The discrete factor V4 is proved, and that this is the whole group is a modeling hypothesis.
