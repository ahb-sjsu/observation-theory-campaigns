# the 3.5 Decomposition

**id.** three-point-five-decomposition
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The volume's worked illustration of two students, Alex and Ethan, who carry the same 3.5 grade point average and occupy different learner states. Alex is $(0.45, 0.85, 0.70, 0.80, 0.75, 0.65)$ and Ethan is $(0.80, 0.50, 0.40, 0.55, 0.40, 0.35)$, and a weighted sum dominated by domain knowledge stands for the grade.

**Example.** With the volume's weights, Alex scores 0.270 + 0.213 + 0.035 + 0.040 + 0.023 + 0.013 = 0.593 and Ethan 0.671. The coordinate differences are 0.35, 0.35, 0.30, 0.25, 0.35 and 0.30, whose squares sum to 0.61. The volume prints the distance as about 0.77, and the square root of 0.61 is 0.78.

## conditions

- Both students are composites and their coordinates are estimated from a biographical sketch. Nothing here is measured.
- With the printed weights 0.60, 0.25, 0.05, 0.05, 0.03 and 0.02 the two projections are 0.593 and 0.671, which are not equal. The volume says the projections can equalize with different courses and that the structural fact matters, not the specific numbers.
- The weighted sum is a stand-in. The volume notes that a real grade point average is also filtered through letter grades and particular exams.

Conditions are curated in `volumes/geometric-education.toml` rather than read from a record.
