# gauge-variant pedagogical heuristic

**id.** gauge-variant-pedagogical-heuristic
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A teacher's estimate of a student's distance to the goal that depends on features of the student that are not coordinates on the learner manifold, such as race, gender, accent, first language or family background. Two students at the same learner state then receive different estimates.

**Example.** Two students are at the same learner state, at true distance 5 from the goal. A teacher who estimates 4 for one and 7 for the other because of their accents has a gauge-variant heuristic, since the state is the same and the estimates differ by 3.

## conditions

- It need not be conscious bias. The volume says it can arise when a teacher applies a correct statistical observation about a group to a new student without checking that student's state.
- The volume states that the damage is directional, underestimating students from disadvantaged backgrounds and overestimating students from advantaged ones. This is asserted and not measured in the volume.
- It is the fourth of the four heuristic failure modes of Section 6.5, which the volume takes from Geometric Reasoning.
