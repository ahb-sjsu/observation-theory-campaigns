# time-temperature path dependence

**id.** time-temperature-path-dependence
**kind.** correction
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The cooked flavor is the time-ordered composition of local transformation maps along the schedule $T(t)$, and not a function of the total heat $\int T\,dt$. Two schedules give the same flavor only if their paths are equivalent under the kinetics, a stronger condition than equal energy.

**Example.** One hour at 200 degrees followed by one hour at 100 gives 300 degree-hours, and so do two hours at 150. If browning runs only above about 140 degrees, the volume's threshold, the first schedule browns for one hour and the second for two, so the integral does not fix the result. The schedules are illustrative.

## conditions

- The path model is tagged a modeling axiom. The reason given is that Maillard and caramelization rates rise roughly exponentially with temperature (Arrhenius kinetics).
- The supporting comparison is qualitative. Sous-vide at 56 degrees Celsius for two days and a roast at 230 degrees for forty minutes are said to deliver comparable total energy and to land at different flavors.
- The volume rejects the equal-integral simplification as false. It gives no measured flavor distance between two such paths.
