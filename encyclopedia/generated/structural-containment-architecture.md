# Structural containment architecture

## definition

The four requirements under which the volume's No Escape Theorem is stated. Inputs pass a canonicalizer that is invariant under the gauge group, evaluation is a d-dimensional grounded tensor with no kernel, every output logs the tensor, the contraction and the residue, and an independent verifier checks every output.

**Example.** In the volume's illustration the fictional ARIA-G verifier applies two random gauge transformations to each input and re-evaluates three of the nine dimensions, so one output triggers 2 extra runs and a check of 3/9, one third, of its tensor.

## conditions

- Canonicalization must preserve moral content, return the same form for every gauge transformation of an input, and be onto the canonical forms. The volume says natural language has no canonical form of the kind medical codes provide.
- Grounded evaluation must be anchored to observable features of the input and have a Jacobian of rank d almost everywhere. The grounding is only as good as the measurement suite.
- The verifier must run on hardware the system cannot reach and must resist tampering by any operator not authenticated through a governance process.
- The only instance described is the fictional ARIA-G, whose canonicalizer is given an accuracy of 94%. Chapter 20 calls high-assurance canonicalization of natural language an open engineering question.
