# communication gauge group

**id.** communication-gauge-group
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Communication, Volume 7 of the Geometric Series

## definition

The group $G = T \times R \times F$ of meaning-preserving transformations. T relabels across languages, R paraphrases within a language, and F changes the modality between spoken, written and signed. A gauge transformation changes the signal coordinates and preserves the content and pragmatic coordinates, up to the holonomy of the transformation path.

**Example.** In the volume's example, turning the sentence about the cat on the mat into its passive form is an element of R, reading it aloud is an element of F, and rendering it in French is an element of T. Saying it sarcastically is in none of them.

## conditions

- Preservation is approximate for pragmatics. For a translation the chapter requires the content to be equal and the pragmatic force to be approximately equal.
- The chapter calls the re-description group the largest and least well-defined component, and says the framework does not solve the question of when two expressions are true paraphrases.
- Chapter 13 restates the group as the fiber-preserving automorphisms of a bundle and adds a register group. For Hohfeldian normative relations it reduces the group to $V_4 \times U(1)_H$, with the Klein four-group in the discrete factor. Since 2026-11-26 the chapter says this group is posited and not derived as unique, and it marks the earlier dihedral group $D_4$ withdrawn.
