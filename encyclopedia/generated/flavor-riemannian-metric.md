# Riemannian metric of flavor

**id.** flavor-riemannian-metric
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Gastronomy, Volume 12 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A symmetric positive-definite tensor $g_{ij}(\mathbf{f})$ at each point of flavor space that gives a small displacement the squared length $ds^2 = \sum g_{ij}\,df_i\,df_j$. Along one intensity axis the coordinate is the logarithm of concentration, so the distance between concentrations $C_1$ and $C_2$ is $|k \log(C_2/C_1)|$, a ratio and not a difference.

**Example.** With $k = 1$ and natural logarithms, going from 1 to 2 grams of salt per litre and going from 2 to 4 grams both cover a distance of log 2, about 0.69, although the second step adds twice the salt. The volume's kitchen form of this is salt held at a fixed percentage of total mass, often cited near 1%.
