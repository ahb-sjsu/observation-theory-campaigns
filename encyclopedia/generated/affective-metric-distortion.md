# Affective polarization as metric distortion

**id.** affective-metric-distortion
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series

## definition

Affective polarization is modeled as the condition in which the perceived distance between an in-group voter and an out-group voter is much larger than their actual manifold distance.

**Example.** In the volume's District 7 illustration the party centroids are 1.5 Mahalanobis units apart and voters perceive about 4.2. The ratio $4.2 / 1.5$ is 2.8, which the chapter calls nearly three times the actual distance.

## conditions

- It concerns perceived distance, estimated from party labels and media impressions. Actual positions need not move.
- The volume says it is strongest on social values and identity, weak on environment and foreign policy, and concentrated near sacred-value boundaries.
- The self-reinforcing loop that ends in a singularity of perceived distance is described in prose. The District 7 perceived and actual distances are illustrative.
