# scaffolding as local metric adjustment

**id.** scaffolding-as-metric-adjustment
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

A scaffold is a temporary change of the local metric near the student's current state, so that a transition too costly in the natural metric becomes feasible in the scaffolded one. It does not move the student. It is removed when the student can make the transition unaided.

**Example.** For a step of 0.1 in domain knowledge, a natural metric component of 4 gives a cost of 0.1 times the square root of 4, which is 0.2. A scaffold that lowers the component to 1 gives a cost of 0.1.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** Scaffolding within Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, which the volume names as its source.

## conditions

- The timing of removal is a heuristic judgment by the teacher. Removed too early, the student is back in a high-curvature region without support. Removed too late, it wastes time and can create dependence.
- The volume's examples are worked examples, visual representations, peer discussion and simplified notation.
- It is the volume's formal reading of Vygotsky's zone of proximal development and is posited. No scaffolded metric has been estimated.
