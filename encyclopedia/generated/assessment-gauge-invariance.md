# assessment gauge invariance

**id.** assessment-gauge-invariance
**kind.** concept
**volume.** Geometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series

## definition

An assessment A, which maps a learner state x and an instrument I to a score, is gauge-invariant when $A(x, I_1)$ and $A(x, I_2)$ differ by no more than measurement noise for every state and every meaning-preserving change of instrument. The volume names five such changes, of examiner, format, language, time and context.

**Example.** A student scores 82 on a free-response form and 74 on a multiple-choice form of the same content, and the noise bound is 3 points. The difference of 8 exceeds 3, so for this pair of instruments the assessment is not gauge-invariant.

**Known as, or related to prior art.** The Bias Invariance Principle of Geometric Reasoning, which the volume names as its source.

## conditions

- It does not require identical performance on every instrument. It requires the measured understanding to be the same once instrument-specific variance is removed.
- The volume calls it a necessary condition for assessment validity. Variance tied to the instrument is treated as systematic bias and not as noise.
- Which changes of instrument preserve meaning is posited. The volume's argument that format changes the score is that formats project onto different learner dimensions.
- The effect sizes quoted for the five changes are cited from the literature in general terms. The volume ran no study, and Prediction 16.3.2 on format has not been tested.
